Orientation: the verification workspace

After Google sign-in the portal asks what you want to do: Assigned tasks or Add places. The assignment sheet lists only your batch tasks; your own nominations live under My nominations in Add places, framed in teal. The header keeps two links — Exit (the public map) and Guide (this page). A basemap pill on the map switches between Streets, Hybrid, and Satellite; placing a pin switches to Hybrid automatically unless you have picked a basemap yourself.

Genuine change versus map correction

When your evidence adds or removes a place, the form asks: what kind of claim is this? The answer drives the annual September census.

  • Genuine change — the place actually opened, closed, or changed use around a date. These are the events the census counts: real gain and loss of places of worship.
  • Map correction — the world did not change; the map record was wrong or incomplete (a missed place, a double count, a wrong location). Corrections rewrite the historical record; they are never counted as gain or loss.
  • Can't tell yet — the honest default. Leave it selected unless your evidence supports one of the other two; a reviewer or a later source can upgrade it.

If this distinction were ignored, every improvement in the map would masquerade as religious change — a wave of "new" places that were always there. Answering it carefully is one of the most consequential things an RA does in this project.

Nominate a missing place with a pin drop

While you are entering, the sidebar shows only the entry. The task list, My work, the filters, and the counts leave when a pin flow, a task's form, or the periods pane opens, and return when it closes; "Show task list" on the signed-in line brings them back early. The address search and the coordinate boxes sit directly under Add a missing place. "Move pin" moves the pin to the coordinates you typed, or, with both boxes empty, to the first search result on screen. The buttons say what they do: Back to map (keeps your draft) leaves the form with your entry saved on this device; Discard this entry (deletes it) removes it after a confirmation; Cancel placement drops an unconfirmed pin.

Dates on a nomination or a revision. The rapid form now carries When was this place used for worship? — the same period cards and the chain block as an assigned task — open on a revision and collapsed on a new place. They are checked before the observation is sent and recorded with it; if that second step fails, your observation stands, your cards are kept, and Add where and when records them. The current-use options explain themselves: "used for worship" means worship happens here at any known frequency, and you give the frequency in the periods; a place used once a year is in use at an intermittent level, never uncertain. A fifth option, Used to exist here, but no longer does, is for a building that has gone: choose it and the periods block opens under the heading When did this place exist and was it used for worship?, with the first card's end waiting for your date rather than "still in use"; the preview then derives absent for the census years after a stated closure. On an assigned task the same path is No building here now. Reviewers see one finding for it either way.

The first map click places the pin; later clicks move it. Cmd/Ctrl+Z steps back, Esc cancels, and confirming requires the pin on screen at building zoom. Search resolves towns and streets, rarely church names — search the town, then drag the pin onto the building.

Revising a place already on the map. In Add or revise places, click an amber dot and press Revise this place, the first button in its popup (the pane that opens shows the same button under its red heading). It opens the same flow as a new place: the pin starts on the current record (drag it if the record is misplaced, or switch to an area with a radius), then the current-use form, then photos and documents as Step 2 of 2, then known history. Pick what is wrong or new about the record at the top of the form. Your entry lands in the review queue beside the record's original point, so the reviewer sees both. Just flag it stays for a concern you cannot evidence; "submitted in error" lives there too. From the public map, the Contribute entry at top right opens the portal, and a place's popup there has its own Revise this place link that opens the portal at that place.

Every country, every place. The portal opens for any country: verification.html?country=fj opens Fiji, and the review page takes the same parameter. A country without a dated data product draws its unvalidated dots from the same OpenStreetMap places tiles as the public map, from zoom 8, in Unvalidated: all mode (no period mode, because the tiles carry no dates). Such a country has no census years yet, so the per-year status grid says so and the dates you enter in the periods and the change chain carry the history; the census-year states derive the day the country's census years are set. There are no assigned tasks in these countries: after sign-in you land straight in Add or revise places. Signed out, you can look at every dot and its Street View and OSM links; the popup's Sign in to revise this place parks the place on the sign-in card and opens its revise card the moment you are in. A dot just across a border names its own country and links to that country's portal.

Markers on imagery. On the Hybrid and Satellite basemaps every marker wears a white halo and the amber unvalidated dots keep a dark edge over a white halo, so nothing disappears against trees or roofs. The ring colours keep their meaning; only the halo is added.

OSM history for context. An amber dot's popup has an OSM history button, and the revise pane shows the same timeline automatically: when the object was first tagged as a place of worship (the earliest OSM evidence, never a founding date), each dated change to its name, religion, denomination, or date tags, and whether it was retagged or deleted. Editor names are not shown; the full record is one click away on openstreetmap.org. Reviewers see the same panel on any task matched to an OSM object.

How sure are you of this location? Leave "I can pinpoint the building" when the pin sits on the building. Choose "I can only place an area" when you know only a vicinity — common for historical places — then pick how far the place could be from the pin (50 m to 100 km, or type a radius). A shaded circle shows the area, the card names the grade that will be recorded (parcel, street, locality, or area level), and you must say what places it there. An area only needs the map zoomed far enough to place its centre.

Where and when was this place used for worship? After a submission, Add where and when sits beside Add known history (the known-history pane offers the same button). You can also come back later: while your evidence for a place awaits review, its dot's popup and its recorded-observation card in Add or revise places offer Add where and when, with any periods you already recorded loaded into the cards. Saving records the whole set again and replaces your earlier set, so edit the loaded cards rather than adding a duplicate. Done — back to this place returns you to the record. Each card is one period at one location: how the start is known (a date, two bounds, a latest date, or unknown), how the end is known (still in use, a date, bounds, an earliest date, or unknown), and what the date rests on. Tick Around this year to record a year with one year's slack either side; the card restates what it will record before you save. Same place as the pin is ticked by default; untick it and Place this period on the map reopens the pin flow for that period alone, then returns you to your cards. A period that ended by relocation opens the next card at a new place. One source block covers every period in the submission, and the chain block under the cards (What was it, and did that change?) rests on the same source: the label at the start, then each change in order. The portal derives a proposal for each census year from your periods, and a denomination per year from the chain; a reviewer confirms each one, so nothing you enter here changes a census-year state or denomination on its own.

Nominations in every enabled country (Vanuatu and New Zealand) use the rapid current-observation form: one current-status answer, the observation date, and how you know it. Assigned-task forms are unchanged for each country. Under How do you know this? two buttons fill the usual named sources in one click: OpenStreetMap record cites the matched OSM object (or the map at your pin) and Google Street View cites the imagery at the point; each switches the basis to a named public source and fills the title and link. You still set the observation date, which for Street View is the capture date Google shows, and the OSM record remains context for identity rather than proof of current use. Photos & documents accepts a photo or source document (JPEG, PNG, WebP or PDF, under 10 MB) — review-only, never public.

How the AI evidence checks work

Some tasks in the review queue carry an AI recommendation beside the human controls. Here is what that recommendation is, and what it is not.

The AI review is advisory. It cannot accept a claim, change a task, edit evidence, or record a decision — the tables it writes to are append-only annotations, and the portal renders them beside the evidence, never in place of it. Every recommendation you see was produced by a versioned prompt against one specific version of the claim; if the claim is revised, the old recommendation stays attached to the old version and a new review must be run. The human decision is the gate: when you record a decision you also record whether you followed, disagreed with, or did not consider the AI recommendation, and that agreement record is part of the audit trail.

Decision history and hashing

Alongside the hashes, the task event log records every transition — imported, claimed, drafted, submitted, decided, exported — with actor, role, time, and reason.
