# Agent research and validation pipeline for places of worship — design brief (2026-09-10)

Status: DRAFT for JB, 2026-09-10; pilot built and run on `feat/agent-research-pipeline-2026-09-10` (`scripts/agent_research/`), nothing deployed, no Convex write, no schema change. Drafted after Joseph Watts (JW) sent a one-place evidence dossier for St Martin's Anglican Church, Loburn (OSM way 643590665), produced by a single pass of GLM 5.3 Flash through OpenRouter, and asked whether the project had "thought about having a swarm of agents search and expand information on places of worship". JB's view: the project cannot get through 1.54 million records and their histories without agent assistance; agent costs are falling; the properties of human data entry and the parameters of data collection must be kept, and the parameters may be expanded, for example several independent lines of research verifying the locations in the New Zealand Anglican history data. This brief sits between the annual OSM audit (`osm-annual-audit-scoping-2026-09-03.md`), the AI-and-human review brief (`ai-human-review-at-scale-brief-2026-09-04.md`, rulings R-A1 to R-A8), and the PI acceptance layer (`pi-acceptance-layer-brief-2026-09-04.md`). Its rulings R-A1 to R-A8 bind everything below.

## 1. The problem

JW's spreadsheet is the artefact the 2026-09-04 brief called AI-drafted evidence (R-A6), and it shows both what a research agent can do for one place in a few minutes and what the project cannot accept as it stands. Its thirteen evidence rows trace the OSM object through four versions, then a 2013 funeral notice, a 2011 photograph caption giving the 1891 build and a 1988–89 renovation, a library catalogue record, the parish's service page, the diocesan directory, two issues of the diocesan newsletter announcing a "sequence of final services" in May 2026 and approval for sale, and the parish site listing the church as closed. Its summary infers the site is likely inactive and the OSM record stale. It is a good dossier. It is also unusable as evidence in the project's terms: no row carries a verbatim quotation, so nothing can be checked against a page without re-reading the page; the OSM timestamps are approximate because the reader could not read the history page; the directory row carries the vicar's name, a mobile number and an office email, which the project's standing policy forbids recording; and every judgement is one model's reading, with no independent line to disagree with it.

The scale question is the 2026-09-04 problem again from the evidence side. The review brief settled how the stock is characterised (deterministic checks over everything, AI screening over the informative slice, a human-reviewed probability sample). What it did not design is how evidence for a place is gathered once a record becomes a case: what the agent-autonomous lane of `ra-ai-interaction-options.md` produces, in what shape, under what checks, and how its output reaches a reviewer and a PI without anyone reading 1.54 million dossiers.

## 2. The principle: a dossier is a lead file, and reader agreement is data

Three sentences carry the design. An AI statement without a source locator is a lead, not evidence (JB, 2026-07-07); a statement with a locator but without a verbatim quotation is a lead that a machine can turn into evidence only by finding the quotation, so the pipeline demands the quotation at capture. A screening verdict is an auxiliary variable and never a validation state (R-A1); the same holds for a dossier's status assessment, its candidate location, and the agreement or disagreement between readers, which are recorded as data about the record and change only where human effort goes. Humans decide, AI recommends, PIs accept (R-A8): a dossier becomes a decision when a reviewer confirms its claims in the portal and a record when a PI accepts them, and nothing in the pipeline short-cuts that chain.

Two consequences follow for what the pipeline is. First, its output is not a place record but a per-place research dossier: a first-class artefact, versioned by schema, stored beside the OSM editions (R-A5), never written into Convex as a whole; only the claims a human confirms enter the review chain, as evidence drafts marked AI-generated. Second, verification is split into what a machine can settle deterministically (does the locator resolve, is the quotation on the page, does the coordinate fall on the OSM building, do two readers give the same year) and what only a human can settle (is this the same place, has worship ended, which of two contradicting sources is right). The machine does the first exhaustively and hands the second to the queue with the disagreement attached.

## 3. The unit: the dossier

`scripts/agent_research/schemas/agent-dossier.v1.json` fixes the shape: place identity (the seed reference, name, country, seed coordinate and the edition it came from); a candidate location with its basis and an uncertainty radius; a list of claims, each with a type, a value, dates and their precision, a source (locator, name, type, the date the source bears and its basis, the retrieval date), a verbatim quoted support, an evidential weight, the reader (id, backend, model id, prompt version) and a confidence; a status assessment (current status, basis, as-of date, the OSM-stale flag with its basis); the OSM version chain; a personal-details quarantine; provenance (agent reader, collaborator import, or human; which lane); and a run manifest (run id, backend, model requested and reported, prompt version, start and end, usage and cost with the basis on which cost is known, an idempotency key over place, prompt version, model and edition, the allowlist version, the tool permissions granted, and the exit status).

### 3b. What the schema lacks, proposed as versioned additions

None of these is a silent change to `convex/schema.ts`; each is a proposed field for a later schema PR, named here so the dossier can carry it now.

1. **OSM object version chain** on the evidence side: version, changeset, exact timestamp, the tag set at that version, and what changed. The audit editions hash the state at each anchor; the chain is the object's own history and answers "when did OSM last say this was active".
2. **Source date distinct from retrieval date**, with a basis (`printed`, `metadata`, `inferred`, `not_stated`). `evidence_drafts.source_date_or_capture_date` conflates them; a 2011 caption retrieved in 2026 needs both.
3. **Evidential weight** as a controlled vocabulary (`primary_institutional`, `contemporary_report`, `secondary`, `user_contributed`, `inferred`) beside the free-text `confidence_basis`, so weights can be counted and stratified.
4. **A current-status assessment with its basis and as-of date** at the dossier level, distinct from the rapid lane's first-hand `current_observation_status`; the vocabulary here is `active`, `likely_active`, `likely_inactive`, `inactive`, `unknown`.
5. **A stale-OSM flag with its basis**, the most useful single output for the annual audit: it names records whose OSM tags no longer match the sources and so should enter the census-of-change queue even when OSM itself has not changed.
6. **Reader agreement per claim type** and the escalation list, recorded with the dossier set rather than on any one dossier.
7. **A quarantine block** with hashed values so a later run can tell whether the same personal detail recurred without holding it.

## 4. Architecture

Six stages, each with a fixed input and output, none able to change a record.

1. **Seed.** A place from an edition (the OSM id, type, name, seed coordinate, tags) or, for a place absent from OSM, a name, coordinate and country (`new:<slug>`). The seed carries the edition name so the idempotency key names the edition.
2. **Independent readers.** Two or more researcher agents from different model families, each given the same prompt (`prompts/researcher.v1.md`) and the same seed and nothing else: no other reader's output, no project data beyond the seed. The pilot's backends are `claude` (`claude -p` headless on JB's subscription, tools restricted to WebSearch and WebFetch, structured output enforced through `--json-schema`, an empty working directory so no project file or instruction is in reach), `codex` (`codex exec` with live web search, a read-only sandbox in an empty directory, `--output-schema`), and `openrouter` (an HTTPS call with the web plugin, JW's route; implemented, untested here because no key is set). Each writes one dossier with full provenance.
3. **Deterministic validator** (`validate_dossier.py`). No model runs here. For every claim it fetches the locator once (robots respected, ten-second timeout, one request per second, private hosts refused as `claudeReviews.ts` refuses them, the OSM API exempted from robots on the API policy's own terms) and records the HTTP status and whether the quoted support appears on the page after normalisation (exact, or at least sixty percent of its word bigrams). It reads the OSM object and its history from the API, compares the seed and each reader's candidate coordinate with the object's centroid, checks each reader's version chain against the true chain (changesets and years), and notes whether the current tags still say `amenity=place_of_worship`. It lists internal contradictions (two dates of one type more than a year apart in one dossier). Locators the website robots forbid but the API can confirm (OSM history pages) are verified through the API.
4. **Agreement.** With two or more dossiers for one place, per claim type: agree when every pair of readers gives the same value within tolerance (a year for dates, 75 m for coordinates, token overlap for text, status collapsed to active or inactive), disagree otherwise, single reader when only one made the claim. The agreement rate is a property of the place and its readers; the disagreement list is the escalation list.
5. **Escalation and confirmation.** Every claim type on which readers disagree, every claim whose quotation was not found, every locator a human must open, and every place whose status assessment or stale flag differs between readers becomes a human task, with the dossiers attached, in the review portal's existing AI-recommendation panel. Claims on which readers agree and the validator confirmed the quotation are shown as confirmed-by-validator leads; a reviewer still confirms each before it is a decision. A collaborator's own agent output (JW's spreadsheet) enters through the agent-assisted lane with the collaborator as confirming human, which is what that lane already means.
6. **PI acceptance and export.** Unchanged: `pi_accepted` before export, the second-opinion gate, the six validation states.

How this satisfies the rulings. R-A1: no stage writes a validation state; the dossier, the validator's report and the agreement file are artefacts beside the editions, and the portal shows them as readings. R-A6: every claim is AI-drafted evidence recorded as such, with agent, provider, model and prompt version on the claim itself, and a lead until confirmed. R-A7: readers are independent by construction (different families, no shared context, the same seed only), agreement is recorded per claim, and disagreement raises the record's priority in the human queue exactly as the review brief's section 6 asks. The locator rule: a claim without a locator is refused by the schema; a claim with a locator and no quotation is a lead and shown as one; a claim whose quotation the validator found is still a lead, but one a reviewer can confirm in seconds.

## 5. The expanded parameters: several lines of research for one location

JB's example was the Anglican church history data for New Zealand: several independent lines of research verifying each location. In this design a location is a claim like any other, with a basis (the OSM object, an address geocode, a source map or photograph, imagery, or the reader's inference) and a radius. Each reader makes its own location claim; the validator adds two deterministic lines (the seed and the OSM centroid) and the distance between every pair; the addressed parcel is a third line once a LINZ address lookup is added to the validator (the OSM record already carries `ref:linz:address_id` for St Martin's). A location ruling is then derived from agreement, never accepted automatically: when all lines fall within tolerance, the reviewer sees "three readers and the OSM building agree within 30 m" and confirms in one action; when a line falls outside, the case goes to the existing revise flow with the wrong-location issue type preselected, and the reviewer records `accept_moved_point`, `keep_original_point` or `uncertain` as the location contract of 2026-09-07 already provides. The public map reads the ruling, as PR #105 built. Nothing new is needed on the ruling side; what is new is that the reviewer arrives with the lines already drawn.

The same shape extends to any parameter JB wants verified by independent lines: the start of worship (readers, Papers Past, the heritage list), the denomination at a date (readers, the diocesan directory, Wikidata P140), and the end of worship (readers, the diocesan newsletter, the parish site). Each line is a claim with its own locator and weight, and agreement across lines is what the reviewer confirms.

## 6. Safety and controls

- **Prompt injection.** Fetched content is data. The reader prompt says so, and the readers have no tool downstream of page content except further reads: no file writes, no shell, no Convex, no email. The validator runs no model at all, so an instruction on a page can reach nothing that acts. This is the CaMeL principle (untrusted data never controls program flow; Debenedetti et al. 2025) and the OWASP LLM01 mitigation list applied literally: least-privilege tools, untrusted content clearly separated, a human before any consequential action. The residual risk is a page that misleads a reader into a false claim; the quotation check and the second reader are the defence, and a reader that reports a page addressing it is asked to note the locator.
- **Allowlists.** `fixtures/allowlist-nz-v1.json` names the domains a reader prefers and the validator fetches without a ruling, and the domains never fetched (social networks). Readers may use other public domains but must mark the evidential weight honestly; the allowlist is per country and versioned, and the version is in every manifest.
- **Personal details.** Stripped at capture: the prompt forbids recording names, telephone numbers or email addresses of living people including clergy, contacts, photographers and map editors; the runner then scans every claim text and the version chain for phones, emails and honorific-led names and moves them into the quarantine, replacing them in the text; the committed copy holds only hashes. The pilot's readers complied: no telephone number or email address appeared in any of the twelve reader dossiers, and the only two quarantined items were honorific-led names of historical figures (a nineteenth-century bishop, a nineteenth-century builder) that the regex cannot tell from living people and a reviewer restores; JW's import needed sixteen. Institutional numbers are quarantined too by default, which is conservative and is an open ruling.
- **Sensitivity gate.** The kastom gate (`convex/lib/sensitivity.ts`) applies before any seed leaves the project: a Vanuatu record, a privacy-flagged draft or a culturally-sensitive row never reaches a reader. The pilot is New Zealand Anglican only.
- **Licences and terms** (all pages read 2026-09-10; section 8 gives the literature). Papers Past: robots allows all agents except one, only PDF images disallowed; the National Library asks to be acknowledged as source; items over 120 years old may be assumed out of copyright; bulk use is pointed to the open dataset or the DigitalNZ API. DigitalNZ: metadata licensed by its owners, keys not shared, source identified; the developer terms page could not be retrieved from this network (CloudFront 403). Heritage New Zealand List: reproduction beyond private study, research, review or education needs written permission, so readers may cite list entries and quote short passages for research, and nothing from the list is republished without permission; no robots rules are published. Wikidata: CC0, no attribution required, a descriptive User-Agent and back-off on 429. Flickr: the site terms forbid scraping, the API is the sanctioned route (under 3,600 queries per hour per key, thirty user photos per page, removal within 24 hours on request), photo licences are per image; the pilot's readers cited Flickr pages and the validator got 502s, so Flickr captions are a lead only until the API route exists. Charities Register: Crown copyright, publication must acknowledge source and copyright status, the open-data service is for developers, addresses must not be harvested; the CC BY claim for the dataset lives on data.govt.nz, which could not be reached. OpenStreetMap: ODbL with attribution; the editing API "is provided in order to edit the map data, not for read-only purposes", a valid User-Agent is required and at most two threads; Overpass allows about 10,000 requests and 1 GB a day per instance. The pilot reads a handful of objects from the API; at scale the validator reads the project's R2 edition instead, which R-A5 already implies.
- **Robots and rate.** The validator honours robots.txt per host, sends a named User-Agent with a contact route, waits a second between requests, and caches every URL within a run. The readers' own fetching is inside the vendor CLIs and follows their policies; the report counts what the reader said it opened.
- **Cost caps and idempotency.** `claude -p` runs under `--max-budget-usd`; codex runs under a wall-clock timeout; each dossier is keyed by place, prompt version, model and edition, and the runner reuses a completed dossier with the same key unless forced, so a re-run costs only what changed. Daily and per-run caps, a pause switch and JB-triggered runs carry over from the batch-review lane and are not re-argued here.
- **Reproducibility.** Every dossier records the prompt version, the allowlist version, the model requested and the model reported, the tool permissions granted and the CLI session or thread id; the validator records its own version and the request count. The same edition, prompt and model give a comparable dossier, not an identical one: readers search the live web, so the brief treats a dossier as dated evidence, like a site visit.
- **Audit of dead and hallucinated locators.** The validator reports, per reader, the share of distinct locators that point at nothing (404 or 410), the share a polite reader could open, and the share that refused an automated reader and need a human. The pilot's rates are in section 7.

Reading the numbers. Every one of the 82 distinct locators the readers cited exists (no 404 or 410): the hallucinated-locator rate in this pilot is zero, against the 3 to 13 percent the literature reports for deep-research agents (section 9). What the validator could not do is open them all: between a quarter and a half of each reader's locators refused a polite automated reader, mostly because the diocesan site serves a JavaScript challenge after a few page views, Papers Past sits behind Incapsula, and the newspaper obituary site, Canterbury Stories, the city library and Flickr refuse or fail outright. Where a page was readable, the Claude reader's quotations were found on it 95 percent of the time (80 percent verbatim) and the Codex reader's 74 percent (65 percent verbatim); the misses are almost all quotations of PDF documents the validator does not parse, and Papers Past OCR text quoted from a page the validator cannot read. Every reader's candidate coordinate fell within 3 m of the OSM centroid, since both readers read the OSM API and both took the building as their basis; the location lines of section 5 will only separate once a reader is asked for an address geocode or a source map as well. Every version chain a reader gave was confirmed against the API (58 versions, none contradicted), which settles JW's "approximate" column exactly. Strict agreement across the six places was 88 percent of the 51 single-valued claim types compared, and six were escalated.

Three observations from the run that the numbers do not show. First, the escalations are the right ones. For St Columba's, Hawarden, the Claude reader dated the end of Anglican worship to a 2011 deconsecration and the Codex reader to the property's 2019 sale, and one reader's "building date" was the 1955 hall rather than the 1905 church: three claims a human must settle, each with its locator. For St Bartholomew's, Kaiapoi, the readers split on 1855 (the first church, on its original site) against 1860 (worship at the present site), which is the occupancy question the definition's identity rule exists for. For St Martin's, the Claude reader noticed that the parish page's closure sentence names "St. Matthew's Loburn", apparently a copy error for St Martin's, and both readers flagged that approval for sale is not a completed sale. Second, the readers are complementary rather than redundant: for St Martin's the Codex reader found an Easter 2025 service and the 21 May 2026 thanksgiving service, the Claude reader the Waimakariri Libraries history and the Flickr caption, and JW's reader the 2013 funeral notice and the Canterbury Stories catalogue record; in an earlier run of the same Codex reader, not in the report, it found the 1891 opening notice in *The Press* on Papers Past, which no reader found in the pilot run. No single reader found all of it, and no reader contradicted another on the closure. Third, the readers' status assessments agreed with each other on every place (active for the two city churches and Kaiapoi, inactive or likely inactive for Loburn, Hawarden and Leithfield), and the stale-OSM flag was raised for all three closed churches, whose OSM records still carry `amenity=place_of_worship`: three census-of-change cases the annual audit would not have found from OSM alone.

## 9. What the literature says

Read 2026-09-10. Anthropic's account of its multi-agent research system (anthropic.com/engineering/built-multi-agent-research-system, June 2025) uses an orchestrator with parallel subagents and a separate citation agent that locates the supporting passage for every claim, and reports that such runs use about fifteen times the tokens of a chat; the pilot's separate validator is that citation stage made deterministic. Verga et al. (arXiv 2404.18796, 2024) show a panel of smaller judges from disjoint model families outperforms one large judge and reduces intra-model bias, which is the direct case for readers from different families. On citations, Rao, Wong and Callison-Burch (arXiv 2604.03173, 2026) measure 3 to 13 percent hallucinated citation URLs and 5 to 18 percent non-resolving across commercial models and deep-research agents, and show a deterministic liveness check cuts non-resolving URLs below one percent; Onweller et al. (arXiv 2605.06635, 2026) find that across fourteen models links mostly resolve but only 39 to 77 percent of claims are actually supported by the cited source, so existence checks are necessary and not sufficient, which is why the validator tests the quotation and not only the status code; Mocan et al. (arXiv 2609.07652, 2026) reach 71 percent end-to-end verdict accuracy with a deterministic-then-retrieval pipeline. On judging, Gu et al. (arXiv 2411.15594) and Ye et al. (arXiv 2410.02736) document persistent bias in LLM-as-judge, the reason the pipeline records agreement rather than asking a model to adjudicate. On injection, Willison's lethal trifecta (simonwillison.net, June 2025), OWASP LLM01:2025, Debenedetti et al.'s CaMeL (arXiv 2503.18813) and OpenAI's Atlas hardening note (December 2025, "unlikely to ever be fully solved") all point the same way: keep private data, untrusted content and consequential actions apart, which section 6 does by giving the readers nothing to act on.

On gazetteers and heritage, the relevant work is narrower. Hu et al. (IJGIS 2024) fine-tune open 7 to 13 billion parameter models for toponym resolution and reach Accuracy@161 km of 0.91; Fernando et al. (arXiv 2512.14228, 2025, Massey) georeference relative locality descriptions with 67 percent within 1 km for New York; Mioduski (arXiv 2508.08266, 2025) geolocates colonial Virginia land grants with a median error of 14 km at about US$1.09 per thousand grants; Hiltmann et al. (arXiv 2502.04351, 2025) show prompted NER on historical texts beats trained baselines by 7 to 22 F1 points; Memduhoğlu, Fulman and Zipf (Earth Science Informatics 2024) enrich OSM building function from tag embeddings; Dabrock et al. (arXiv 2508.11486, 2025) reach macro F1 0.71 against expert heritage inventories in Stockholm from street imagery. None of these builds a verified place-of-worship gazetteer with dated occupancy, and none reports per-claim source verification of the kind the validator performs; the project would be publishing that measurement.
