# Circulation signals at places of worship — options brief (2026-09-10)

Status: OPTIONS BRIEF. Serious thought, no build beyond a signal register schema and a calibration-design script (section 8). Bob Woodberry wrote to JB on 2026-08-20: "I would love to do something in New Zealand linking cell phone data." JB's reply framed the question the project is asking: how to capture data now for future historians and social scientists, and how to do so cheaply, automatically and ethically. JB added on 2026-09-10 that signals from movements and circulations at potential places of worship might give a better sense of what is happening at them, and that the pathway "would require serious thought." This brief supplies that thought: what such a signal measures, which sources exist and on what terms, what the law and the project's own policies permit, and what a New Zealand pilot led by Bob could look like. Every price, term and availability claim was read from the cited page on 2026-09-10; anything read from memory or a search snippet is marked UNVERIFIED.

The headline is that no New Zealand mobile-presence product is available to a researcher today. Stats NZ disestablished Data Ventures, the telco-derived hourly population product, in December 2023; the Integrated Data Infrastructure holds no telecommunications data; and no commercial aggregator names New Zealand coverage on any fetched page. A New Zealand pilot therefore has to be built from consented on-site counting and denominational returns, with a bespoke telco agreement as a possibility to explore rather than a route to plan on.

## 1. The construct: a presence signal is evidence about the worship-function state

The project's analytical unit is a place of worship's time-indexed worship-function state ([operational definition v0.1.5](../operational-definition.md)). A footfall, presence or movement signal at a site is evidence about that state at that time. It is never a validation state: by ruling R-A1 an automated verdict is an auxiliary variable, and the same applies to a count of devices or bodies ([AI review brief](ai-human-review-at-scale-brief-2026-09-04.md)). A signal can raise or lower a reviewer's confidence that recurring worship occurs; it cannot accept a place onto the map.

### 1.1 Scientific purpose and working specification

The purpose is measurement, not causal inference. No intervention is contemplated. The specification below is the one every claim in this brief is held to.

- **Target population.** Mappable sites in New Zealand that are candidates for, or accepted as, places of worship in the project's database at a stated observation time, with the study's sample frame being the site list at that time. Generalisation beyond the frame (to unmapped sites) is a stated assumption, not a property of any signal.
- **Scientific objects.** Two objects at site $s$ and time $t$: the worship-function state $W_{st} \in \{0, 1\}$, whether the site hosts recurring religious worship by or for a community in period $t$ (year, or a finer period when the source supports it); and, where the question is attendance rather than function, the attendance count $A_{sw}$, the number of people present for worship at site $s$ in service week $w$.
- **Recorded measures.** A presence signal $Y_{st}$: a device count, a door count, a car count, a photo count or another circulation measure at $s$ in $t$, produced by a named source with its own aggregation, panel, suppression and time resolution. Each source is a different instrument with a different error structure.
- **Statistical estimands (measurement).** For function: the sensitivity $P(Y_{st} > c \mid W_{st} = 1)$ and specificity $P(Y_{st} \le c \mid W_{st} = 0)$ of a signal threshold $c$, and the calibration curve $P(W_{st} = 1 \mid Y_{st} = y)$, in the target population. For attendance: the calibration relation $E[A_{sw} \mid Y_{sw}]$ and its dispersion. Both are properties of the pair (signal, site population); neither is a property of the signal alone.
- **Descriptive quantities.** The Sunday-morning to weekday-morning ratio of presence counts by place-of-worship density is a descriptive population fact about areas, not a site-level measurement and not a causal effect of places of worship on movement.
- **Ground truth.** Denominational attendance returns, field observation, or congregation-supplied headcounts at a probability sample of sites. Each carries its own error (section 5).
- **Identification status.** The statistical estimands are identified from a probability sample of sites with ground truth under two assumptions: the ground truth is accurate for the sampled sites (or its error is characterised), and sampling from the frame is by a known design. Where ground truth is available only for one denomination, the statistical estimands are identified for that denomination's sites and their transport to other traditions is an unverified assumption.
- **Reserved for JB.** The observation period, the definition of $W_{st}$'s period, the denominations in the ground-truth arm, and any move from measurement to a causal question.

### 1.2 Named confounders of the signal

Presence at a site is caused by many things besides worship. Each item below is a reason a high $Y_{st}$ can occur with $W_{st} = 0$, or a low one with $W_{st} = 1$.

1. **Co-located institutions.** Church schools, kindergartens and halls: Pope (2024) found that relaxing the worship-day filter raised estimated weekly attendance from 5% to 9% and judged the higher figure "too high given that it captures people who show up regularly at a religious location for preschools or other events" ([BFI working paper](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/BFI_WP_2024-58.pdf)).
2. **Funerals, weddings and civic events.** Recurring in aggregate, occasional at any site, and concentrated at large and historic churches. The Christchurch Anglican parish returns exclude them for exactly this reason (section 5).
3. **Tourism at cathedrals and heritage churches.** Visitors on any day of the week, in daylight hours.
4. **Markets, polling, community meals and hall hire.** Weekday and Saturday presence that mimics a service.
5. **Shared car parks and transport nodes.** A church beside a supermarket, school or bus interchange inherits their footfall.
6. **Residential clergy and caretakers.** Continuous low-level presence, which for telco and device panels can register as a "home" location and be dropped by home-detection filters.
7. **Multi-use and shared buildings.** The operational definition allows one site to host several places of worship; a signal cannot attribute presence among them.

### 1.3 Named failure modes of the sources

1. **Small congregations under the privacy threshold.** Aggregated products suppress or perturb small counts (Meta's Movement Range Maps omitted any region "with fewer than 300 qualifying people"; Stats NZ output rules suppress counts under six and round to base three). Most New Zealand congregations are small: the Church Life Survey NZ 2023 reached 25,404 attendees across 501 churches, a mean near 50. The sites the project most needs evidence about are the ones a threshold erases.
2. **Rural coverage.** Cell-tower density and panel size fall with population; rural churches, which dominate the historical record, produce the weakest signal.
3. **Device-ownership and panel bias by age and culture.** Device panels skew wealthier and younger (Pope 2024 reports a wealthier panel and no observed race, gender or age); worshippers skew older; some communities leave phones at home on the sabbath (Orthodox Jews "drastically undercounted" in Pope).
4. **Home-location leakage and re-identification.** Device-level products carry inferred home locations; a worship visit joined to a home is a religious-affiliation inference about a household. This is the harm the FTC orders address (section 6).
5. **POI geometry.** Pope found 228,721 religious POIs in the 2019 SafeGraph/Veraset file against about 380,000 US congregations, and 35,000 of a later 427,275-POI file were "not places of worship" on Street View checks. Site polygons are themselves uncertain measurements, and the project's own location rulings apply.
6. **Panel drift.** Dewey warns that Advan's panel differs from SafeGraph's; a time series across a vendor change is two instruments.
7. **Timing.** Worship is not only Sunday morning. Friday prayers, Saturday sabbath, evening services, monthly and seasonal gatherings each need their own window, and the operational definition counts seasonal and annual recurrence.

### 1.4 What the signal cannot tell you

A presence signal cannot establish identity over time (whether worship at a location continues an earlier place of worship), cannot name the denomination or the community, cannot distinguish worship from the other uses in 1.2, cannot see home worship or worship in a venue outside the frame, and cannot be read below its source's threshold. It can, at best, tell a reviewer that people gather at a site at times consistent with a claimed pattern of worship, and how that has changed.

## 3. What the literature shows

The one rigorous device-based attendance study is Pope (2024), "Religious Worship Attendance in America: Evidence from Cellphone Data" (NBER WP 32334; BFI WP 2024-58; a journal version was not found — UNVERIFIED). Using Veraset visits from about 2.1 million consistently pinging phones over April 2019 to February 2020, Pope finds that "only 5% of Americans attend services 'weekly,' far fewer than the roughly 22% who report they do so in surveys", that 73% enter a place of worship at least once a year, and a mean worship visit of 97 minutes. His own robustness work shows the estimate moving between 5% and 9% with the visit definition, phones left at home (87% of a Prolific sample say they always or almost always carry theirs), and a POI file missing about 40% of congregations. Cools, Esparza and Park (2026, JSSR, doi 10.1111/jssr.70067) use smartphone data from 15 million users across 2019–2022 and find Catholic attendance "recovered slowly and more gradually than other activities like restaurant visits", with a 2–4 percentage-point rise after dispensations lifted; the diocese-level correlation reported in search results is UNVERIFIED. Chang et al. (2020, Nature, doi 10.1038/s41586-020-2923-3) model hourly SafeGraph visits to 552,758 POIs and place religious organisations among the categories with "the largest predicted increases in infections when reopened". Wang and Guo (2025, Scientific Data, doi 10.1038/s41597-025-05504-9) publish tract-level "Religious Participation" (NAICS 813110) activity from Advan for 2019–2024. Hill, Gonzalez and Burdette (2020, J Religion and Health, doi 10.1007/s10943-020-01058-9) and Hill, Gonzalez and Upenieks (2021, J Aging and Health, doi 10.1177/0898264320984016) use Cuebiq state-level mobility and religiosity, not site visits. DeFranza et al. (2021, American Psychologist, doi 10.1037/amp0000717) find higher religiosity associated with less adherence to shelter-in-place directives; the mobility vendor is unnamed on the fetched pages. Dube, Blumenstock and Callen (2022, NBER WP 30694) infer observance in Afghanistan from prayer-time dips in phone activity. The older headcount literature is the anchor: Hadaway, Marler and Chaves (1993, ASR, doi 10.2307/2095948) found "actual church attendance rates ... approximately 50% of reported levels".

Two gaps matter for a New Zealand pilot. There is no New Zealand or Pacific study, and there is essentially no external validation of device-derived attendance against congregation-supplied counts beyond Pope's internal checks. A calibration study of the kind proposed in section 7 would be a contribution in its own right.

## 4. Open attention proxies, and what they are worth

Three open, live, keyless signals were verified by direct calls on 2026-09-10 for New Zealand: Wikimedia Commons geosearch returns photographs with EXIF and upload dates within a radius of a point; the ohsome API returns the count of `amenity=place_of_worship` objects in a bounding box over time (a Wellington box: 31, 54, 123 and 124 at 2010, 2015, 2020 and 2025); and the Wikipedia pageviews API returns daily views of an article such as Old St Paul's, Wellington. These measure photographer, mapper and reader attention. They are cheap covariates for prioritising review and may help a screening model, and they say nothing about worship at a site. The same holds for Foursquare's open places (no popularity field) and Overture (a confidence that a place exists).

## 5. Ground truth in New Zealand

The calibration design needs attendance or function truth for a sample of sites. Four sources exist, at different grains.

1. **Presbyterian Church of Aotearoa New Zealand** publishes a per-parish XLSX for the year to 30 June 2025 with columns for "Attendance at Worship Adult / Children Under 13 / Youth 13-17", with a back series to 2001 ([statistics](https://www.presbyterian.org.nz/for-parishes/church-statistics/statistics-2025)). This is the best open per-parish series in the country.
2. **Anglican Diocese of Christchurch** publishes "Parish Stats for year ended Dec 2025" with per-parish "ANNUAL ATTENDANCES AT ALL SERVICES EXCLUDING WEDDINGS AND FUNERALS" (diocesan total 240,442) ([synod reports](https://www.anglicanlife.org.nz/christchurch-diocesan-synod/reports/)). No national Anglican attendance series was found; liturgy.co.nz states "we don't keep consistent national Anglican church stats". The 2000–2010 average annual attendance appendix in the Derbyshire thesis covers all seven dioceses at parish grain, under the licence and consent conditions recorded in the private tier.
3. **Catholic Mass counts**: the Diocese of Auckland counts attendance over the November weekends each year and publishes diocese totals (36,842 in 2023 to 39,968 in 2024 per CathNews); Christchurch's 2024 annual report gives a 10,853 Mass count across 18 parishes. Per-parish figures are held by the dioceses ([Auckland](https://www.aucklandcatholic.org.nz/bishops-office-mass-counts/)).
4. **Baptist Union**: 2025 returns give 29,084 weekly onsite attendance across 238 churches, with "individual church data ... available on request" (He Rito, 18 Feb 2026).

Two further sources frame the population rather than the site. The Church Life Survey NZ 2023 (25,404 attendees, 501 churches; next wave 2028) gives attender profiles; the New Zealand Attitudes and Values Study gives self-reported attendance frequency by residence for a national panel, which can be aggregated to an expected attendance per statistical area. Notably, NZAVS is not in the IDI (UNVERIFIED claims to the contrary appear in search results; the IDI data list of 9 July 2026 does not include it), so any join to census or area data runs through the project's own geocoding of residence, under NZAVS's ethics approval.

Parishes and sites are different grains. A parish return covers every building in the ministry unit, so a site-level calibration needs single-building parishes or a within-parish allocation, and the register in `docs/development/circulation-signal-register.v1.json` records this per source.

## 6. Ethics and law

### 6.1 New Zealand

The Privacy Act 2020 governs personal information about a living, identifiable person through thirteen information privacy principles: purpose of collection (1), source (2), collection from the subject (3) and from another source (3A, in force 1 May 2026), manner (4), storage and security (5), access (6), correction (7), accuracy (8), retention (9), limits on use (10), disclosure (11), disclosure outside New Zealand (12), and unique identifiers (13) ([OPC principles](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/)). The Act "does not prescribe fixed categories of 'sensitive' personal information", but the Privacy Commissioner names religious beliefs as an example warranting "a higher standard of care" ([OPC sensitive information guidance](https://www.privacy.org.nz/)). The Government Chief Data Steward's de-identification guidance warns that "it may be possible to identify an individual by combining an agency's data ... with data available elsewhere" ([digital.govt.nz](https://www.digital.govt.nz/)). The Telecommunications Information Privacy Code 2020 covers subscriber, traffic and content information about an identifiable individual, and location information only for emergency purposes under Schedule 4; aggregated tower counts fall outside it, identifiable device location within it ([TIPC 2020](https://www.privacy.org.nz/assets/Codes-of-Practice-2020/Telecommunications-Information-Privacy-Code-2020-website-version.pdf)). The Biometric Processing Privacy Code 2025 came into force on 3 November 2025; "Face or person detection (without unique identification or demographic categorisation)" is outside it, "though Privacy Act obligations remain", which is what makes anonymous door counters usable.

Māori data governance applies with force. Te Mana Raraunga's principles state that "Free, prior and informed consent (FPIC) shall underpin the collection and use of all data from or about Māori", that "In some contexts, collective Māori rights will prevail over those of individuals", and that "Māori shall decide which Māori data shall be controlled (tapu) or open (noa) access" ([principles](https://www.temanararaunga.maori.nz/principles-of-maori-data-sovereignty)). Stats NZ applies Ngā Tikanga Paihere to every Data Lab application. Presence counts at marae, urupā and Māori churches are Māori data; the pilot excludes them absent iwi or hapū consent.

Victoria University of Wellington's Human Ethics Policy requires approval for "access to personal identifying information not already publicly available" and exempts "Research involving existing publicly available documents or information"; aggregated commercial data under agreement is neither, so an application is the safe course, and "Retrospective approval is not possible" ([policy](https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/law/pdf/human-ethics-policy.pdf)).

### 6.2 GDPR for any European sites

Article 9(1) prohibits processing personal data "revealing ... religious or philosophical beliefs" absent an exemption; Recital 26 places anonymous information outside the Regulation, "including for statistical or research purposes". A device trace joined to a place of worship is Article 9 data; a suppressed, aggregated count is not. The Church of England's guidance already reads attendance as special-category data.

### 6.3 The regulatory signal from the United States

Three Federal Trade Commission orders treat "places of religious worship" as a sensitive-location category and ban the sale of location data about them: X-Mode Social and Outlogic (9 January 2024), InMarket (18 January 2024), and Kochava and Collective Data Solutions (4 May 2026) ([FTC X-Mode](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/01/ftc-order-prohibits-data-broker-x-mode-social-outlogic-selling-sensitive-location-data), [FTC Kochava](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-ban-kochava-subsidiary-selling-sensitive-location-data-settle-charges-they-sold-location-data)). The Gravy Analytics breach of January 2025 exposed "several terabytes of consumers' data" from a broker with no SDK of its own ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/13/gravy-analytics-data-broker-breach-trove-of-location-data-threatens-privacy-millions/)). Any US-sourced vendor supplying worship-site visits is operating against this grain, and a religion map that appeared to track worshippers would inherit the reputational risk whatever the law permits.

### 6.4 Proposed project policy (for ruling R-C1)

1. **Aggregate only.** The project holds and publishes counts per site per period, never device-level, person-level or trajectory data. No individual trace enters project storage, Convex or R2, under any agreement.
2. **Thresholds.** A site-period count is stored only when the source's own suppression rule has been applied and the count is at least ten (a project floor above the Stats NZ suppress-under-six rule); counts are rounded to base three before storage (Stats NZ convention) unless a source is coarser.
3. **Site-level, not person-level, inference.** Signals inform the worship-function state of a site; the project never infers, stores or displays a person's, household's or address's attendance.
4. **No home-location products.** Products that carry inferred home or work locations are excluded even when aggregated on delivery.
5. **No scraping.** Google Popular Times and any other terms-barred source are excluded.
6. **Public display.** The public map shows no circulation signal and nothing from screening (standing rule). If a signal ever reaches a public surface it appears as an evidence line in a reviewed record ("counts consistent with weekly gathering, 2027, source X"), never as a number per site.
7. **Māori data.** No counts at marae, urupā, or Māori churches without iwi or hapū consent under Te Mana Raraunga; consented counts are governed by the consenting body.
8. **Consent for on-site sensors.** Anonymous counters only, installed at the congregation's request with signage, data returned to the congregation, withdrawal at any time.
9. **Ethics approval** before any data agreement or installation; a privacy impact assessment for any telco route.
10. **Retention.** Site-period aggregates are durable project data with a manifest; any raw feed is deleted on aggregation.

## 7. A New Zealand pilot that Bob Woodberry could lead

### 7.1 Question

For a probability sample of New Zealand sites in the project's frame, how well do candidate presence signals recover (a) whether the site hosts recurring worship in the observation year and (b) the attendance count, against denominational returns and on-site counts?

### 7.2 Design

- **Frame and sample.** The project's NZ site list (3,370 OpenStreetMap-derived places in `data/nz_places.geojson`, to be replaced by the accepted site set at pilot time). A stratified probability sample by denomination group (Anglican, Presbyterian, Catholic, other Christian, non-Christian, and a sixth group of sites with no religion tag, the frame's best supply of sites that may host no worship) and urban or rural SA2 (a residential-density proxy, since the static data carry no settlement classification), with the review queue's unmapped candidates as a later addition once they are exported. The calibration script in section 8 builds this frame from the existing data and allocates a sample of 120 sites by proportional allocation with a floor of eight per stratum; the size proxy the design wants (building footprint) is absent from the current data, so the script records only whether the site is mapped as a building.
- **Ground-truth arm.** For Presbyterian and Christchurch Anglican sites, the published per-parish attendance returns; for Catholic sites, per-parish Mass counts requested from the dioceses; for a subsample of 30 sites across strata, a field-observation protocol (two service-time visits and one weekday visit by an RA, recording gathering and headcount bands) as the criterion that does not depend on any denomination's bookkeeping.
- **Signal arms**, run in parallel so their errors can be compared: (S1) anonymous door counters at 20 consenting congregations in the sample, continuous for 26 weeks; (S2) open attention proxies for every site (Commons photos, OSM edit history, Wikipedia views); (S3) permitted Google Places fields (business status, opening hours) fetched, used internally, and never displayed with the map; (S4) if and only if a telco agreement is reached, hourly SA2 counts for Sunday 09:00–12:00 and the same hours on Tuesday and Wednesday, from which the descriptive ratio by place-of-worship density is computed; (S5) portal check-ins by RAs and members as a citizen-science stream.
- **Statistical estimands** as in 1.1: sensitivity and specificity of each signal's threshold for $W_{st}$, calibration curves for $A_{sw}$ from S1 against returns and observation, and the descriptive Sunday-to-weekday ratio by density from S4.
- **Analysis.** Design-weighted estimates with stratum-specific reporting; explicit treatment of parishes with several buildings (single-building parishes only in the site-level calibration); pre-registered thresholds.
- **Cost, indicative.** Door counters: at Dor's published $150/month plus $300 per unit, twenty units for six months is about $24,000 USD; Density's Waffle at $229 per unit is a hardware-only alternative with unverified service costs. RA field observation for 30 sites at three visits each, about 90 site visits. No vendor data purchase is budgeted, since none covers New Zealand on any verified page. Ethics application and PIA in kind.
- **A positive result** would look like: a signal or combination with sensitivity above 0.9 and specificity above 0.8 for recurring worship in the sampled strata, including rural sites; a calibration curve from S1 to returns with a narrow dispersion; and, for S4 if it exists, a Sunday-to-weekday ratio that rises monotonically with place-of-worship density after conditioning on residential population, retail floor area and schools, with the rise absent on Tuesday. A negative result on S2 and S3 would be as useful, since it would retire cheap proxies from the screening design.

### 7.3 Data request sketch (Stats NZ or a telco)

Data Ventures no longer exists, so the request goes either to Stats NZ's data platform team (the contact named on the disestablishment notice) about any retained hourly SA2 series, or to Spark or One NZ for a bespoke aggregate. The ask, in the terms the former product used: hourly counts of devices present per SA2 (2023 boundaries) for a 12-month window, restricted to two three-hour windows per week (Sunday and Tuesday 09:00–12:00), randomly rounded to base three, with counts under a stated floor suppressed, delivered as a flat table of `sa2_code, date, hour, count`, with no device, home, work or movement fields, under a research agreement that permits publication of area-level descriptive statistics and bars any re-identification attempt. The project supplies place-of-worship counts per SA2 from its own data and computes the ratio. The privacy impact assessment and ethics approval precede the agreement.

## 8. What Pilot B is, and why

No genuinely open dataset with temporal resolution at small-area grain exists for New Zealand in 2026: Data Ventures' hourly SA2 product ended in December 2023, the Stats NZ COVID-19 data portal closed on 22 August 2024, MBIE's regional monthly estimates stopped in October 2023, and the one peer-reviewed use of the hourly series (Campbell et al. 2021, JECH, doi 10.1136/jech-2020-216108) carries no data-availability statement. Strava Metro requires a project-bound application. The Pilot B deliverables are therefore the register and the calibration design rather than a joined analysis, and no dataset was invented to fill the gap.

- `schemas/circulation-signal-register.v1.json`: the register contract (source, geography, resolution, POI basis, access, cost, terms, privacy model, status, verification date and URL, fitness for site-level calibration).
- `docs/development/circulation-signal-register.v1.json`: the filled register from section 2.
- `scripts/circulation_calibration_design.R`: builds the site frame from `data/nz_places.geojson` and `data/sa2.geojson`, derives the strata, draws a seeded stratified sample of 120 sites, and writes the frame summary and sample to `exports/circulation-calibration/`. Its results note is `docs/development/circulation-calibration-design-note-2026-09-10.md`.
