# Data-source register, round 2 — religionmap.org

Date: 2026-08-14. Audience: project owner, deciding which external sources to integrate. Scope: eight candidate sources scanned for licensing, spatial resolution, taxonomy crosswalk, access mechanics, and value — the three sources round 1 flagged as missed (Wikidata, Overture places, charity registers), plus Foursquare Open Source Places, heritage registers, denominational directories, IPUMS International, and the regional survey programmes. Frame: every source here complements the statistics-office outreach lane; none replaces it. Round 2 differs from round 1 in one respect: it surfaces three point-level POI corpora under genuinely open licences, so the candidate-site lane now has redistribution-grade feeds where round 1 had only GeoNames.

## Summary

Three sources in this survey earn integration outright, and all three are point-level POI corpora under clean licences: Wikidata (CC0, the only open source carrying denomination as structured data at site level, plus inception and dissolution dates feeding the opening/closure lane), Overture Maps places (CDLA-Permissive-2.0, deliberately disjoint from OSM, with Meta-derived Pacific coverage the priority countries need), and Foursquare Open Source Places (Apache 2.0, ~100M POIs, religion-level worship categories). A fourth, the heritage-register family, is integrate-grade register-by-register: England's NHLE is verified OGL and ready to wire, while the NZ Heritage List resolved as all-rights-reserved — internal research use is permitted under its research exception, but publishing derived fields needs written permission from Heritage New Zealand Pouhere Taonga, an email that fits the existing outreach lane. IPUMS International needs one confirmation email before any derived aggregate ships publicly, and then fills census-table gaps for Fiji, PNG, and some 57 other countries — though not NZ, Australia, or the small Pacific states. The charity registers are validation-only on semantic grounds despite open licences everywhere: registered-office points would falsify site locations, so they feed leads, coverage checks, and NZ deregistration-dated closure evidence, never the map. The denominational directories are the round's blocked cluster — LDS terms ban even building an internal machine-readable copy, the Adventist Yearbook forbids reproduction, and the rest default to all-rights-reserved — usable only as manually consulted verification references and as permission targets. The regional survey programmes score exactly as WVS did in round 1: one-line national context rows with citation, nothing more, and nothing for the Pacific. The round's chief lesson: the open-POI ecosystem now supplies everything the project needs for finding and cross-checking sites, but every source in it describes sites, never affiliation populations — the statistics-office outreach lane remains the only route to the project's distinctive assets, and outside the open-data movement the default is still all-rights-reserved, where the remedy is a short permission email, not a scraper.

## Wikidata — place-of-worship entities

**Verdict: integrate-grade.** The P31 place-of-worship subtree (church, mosque, temple, marae, synagogue and kin) with P140 religion, P625 coordinates, P571 inception, and P576 dissolution, via the SPARQL endpoint and dumps.

### Licensing

The cleanest licence in either survey round, verified on the Wikidata licensing page (https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing): "All structured data in the main, property and lexeme namespaces is made available under the Creative Commons CC0 License (Public domain)", while "text in other namespaces is made available under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 License". CC0 covers everything the map would use — coordinates, P31 types, P140 religion values, P571 inception dates, labels, external IDs — with no attribution obligation for internal analysis, published redistribution on religionmap.org, or derivative products; attribution is still good practice and we should give it. Two scope cautions. The first scope caution is that Wikipedia article text and Commons images linked from items are not CC0 and carry their own licences (CC BY-SA and others), so only the structured statements may be ingested. The second scope caution is provenance: Wikidata accepts only CC0-compatible contributions, but individual statements can be unsourced, so statement quality varies.

### Resolution

Point coordinates (P625) on individual buildings and sites — the top resolution tier. 880 of 904 NZ place-of-worship items carry coordinates, typically at building-centroid quality. No polygons, but items interlink to OSM geometry: 510 of ~920 NZ items carry an OSM way ID (P10689) and 20 carry an OSM node ID (P11693), and the richer join direction is OSM's wikidata=* tag pointing back.

### Crosswalk

Moderate, and easier than the survey sources because P140 values are named denominations that align with census categories: NZ top values are Anglicanism (73), Catholicism (54), Presbyterianism (36), Methodism (24), then Baptists, LDS, Islam, and Greek Orthodoxy — a near-direct match to Stats NZ religious-affiliation categories and the LEXICON site model. Two real costs. The first cost is value normalisation: the same denomination appears under multiple QIDs ("Catholicism", "Catholic", "Catholic Church" all occur in NZ data), so a QID-to-lexicon mapping table of roughly 100–200 rows, reusable globally, is needed. The second cost is sparse coverage: only ~245 of ~920 NZ items (27%) carry P140 at all, so it enriches rather than classifies the corpus. The P31 class itself (church building, mosque, marae, synagogue) gives a coarse religion signal where P140 is absent.

### Access

Excellent and free. The SPARQL endpoint at query.wikidata.org needs no auth, imposes a 60-second query timeout and polite-use rate limits with a User-Agent, and runs whole-country place-of-worship extracts in seconds — ten-country batch queries can time out, so query per country. Full JSON dumps at dumps.wikimedia.org support reproducible snapshots (100GB+ compressed; overkill for our slice), and a per-entity REST API supports incremental refresh. The practical join is bidirectional: pull items by P31 subtree plus P17 country, or start from OSM objects' wikidata=* tags. No keys, no quotas of consequence at our volume.

### Value

Attribute-enrichment join first, candidate-site feed second, and only in that order. Coverage sits well below OSM everywhere probed — NZ ~900 items versus several thousand OSM places of worship, and the Pacific is nearly empty (Fiji 38, Vanuatu 21, Tonga 17, Samoa 6, Solomon Islands 6, PNG 25; ~295k church buildings globally, heavily European) — so it cannot seed priority-country candidate sites. What it buys: denomination labels for ~27% of matched sites, redistributable without restriction; opening and closure evidence — 376 of ~930 NZ items carry inception dates (P571) and 23 carry dissolved dates (P576), directly feeding the LEXICON opening/closure-date lane and the Anglican review lane as source evidence; a durable external identifier (QID) per site for the master record, linking onward to heritage registers, Commons imagery (separately licensed), and Wikipedia context; and a small candidate-site residue — items with coordinates but no OSM link (~40% of NZ items) are cheap places-to-check rows. It complements, and cannot replace, statistics-office outreach: it describes sites, never affiliation populations. Effort: 1–2 days for a per-country SPARQL extraction script plus the QID normalisation table and a places-to-check diff against OSM for NZ; roughly a week to run the enrichment join across all 100 country pages with review of ambiguous matches. No licensing negotiation, no auth, no fees.

## Overture Maps Foundation — places theme

**Verdict: integrate-grade.** The POI supplement; monthly GeoParquet releases with stable GERS identifiers.

### Licensing

The places theme is the one Overture theme free of ODbL, verified on Overture's attribution page (https://docs.overturemaps.org/attribution/): places is CDLA-Permissive-2.0 (contributors Meta, Microsoft, PinMeTo, and others), with two permissive admixtures — Foursquare under Apache 2.0 (notice retention: "Copyright 2024 Foursquare Labs, Inc.") and AllThePlaces under CC0. The CDLA-Permissive-2.0 operative grant (https://cdla.dev/permissive-2-0/) reads: "A Data Recipient may use, modify, and share the Data made available by Data Provider(s) under this agreement if that Data Recipient follows the terms of this agreement" (s1.1), with the sole sharing condition that "the Data Recipient makes available the text of this agreement with the shared Data" (s2.1), and no restriction on Results: "This agreement does not impose any restriction or obligations with respect to the use, modification, or sharing of Results" (s3.1). Published redistribution on the public map is therefore permitted with a licence-text link plus the Foursquare Apache notice, and derivative products — the governed master record, density estimates — are unrestricted as Results. One caveat: the clearance is theme-specific. Overture's buildings, base, transportation, and divisions themes are ODbL and must not be conflated with places in any licensing statement.

### Resolution

Point coordinates per POI, no building polygons in this theme. Positional quality is heterogeneous: much of the ~75M-record corpus (about 79% Meta-derived, largely Facebook page geocodes) carries geocoder-grade coordinates that can land on road centroids or postcode centres, so treat each point as a candidate location to confirm against imagery, never as survey-grade. Each feature has an existence confidence score (0–1) for filtering.

### Crosswalk

Easy-to-moderate. The taxonomy (~2,300 categories, monthly-versioned CSV in the schema repo) has a dedicated top-level religious_organization branch with about 20 subtypes: church_cathedral plus denominational children (anglican_church, baptist_church, catholic_church, episcopal_church, evangelical_church, jehovahs_witness_kingdom_hall, pentecostal_church), mosque, synagogue, hindu_temple, buddhist_temple, sikh_temple, shinto_shrines, temple, convents_and_monasteries, mission, religious_destination. That maps cleanly onto the project's place-of-worship and denominational-family concepts and is richer than OSM's amenity plus religion/denomination tags for denominational churches, though US-centric — no Methodist, Presbyterian, LDS, or Rātana subtypes; those arrive as bare church_cathedral. Two crosswalk cautions. The first caution is that religious_organization includes non-site organisations (offices, missions), so it feeds the candidate-site lane rather than mapping one-to-one onto mappable sites. The second caution is that the taxonomy is mid-overhaul — a basic_category field and hierarchical taxonomy paths supersede the deprecated categories property in September 2026 — so pin the taxonomy version at ingest.

### Access

Excellent: no auth, no rate limits, no scraping. Monthly releases as GeoParquet on public S3 and Azure paths, queryable in place with DuckDB — a spatial plus category filter makes a per-country religious_organization extract one SQL statement — or via the overturemaps Python CLI, with GeoJSON export supported. GERS ids give stable entity identifiers across releases with bridge files and changelogs — a durable foreign key to store alongside site_id for refresh diffing, directly analogous to how OSM ids are handled now.

### Value

A candidate-site feed and independent cross-check that is disjoint from the current base: the places theme deliberately excludes OSM, so every match or miss against the OSM-derived point layer is real signal, never circularity. The Meta provenance is unusually valuable for the Pacific priority countries — congregations there often exist as Facebook pages long before anyone maps them in OSM — so a Fiji/Vanuatu/Samoa extract of religious_organization records above a confidence threshold is a ready-made possible-missing-site task list for the shared online task map. Denominational subtypes also add a weak-evidence denomination field for NZ church rows. It supplies no adherent counts and no time series, so it feeds the review lane, never the master record directly. Effort: 1–2 days for a DuckDB extract of religious_organization for NZ plus Pacific countries with GERS ids and confidence scores retained; the real cost is entity matching and dedupe against existing OSM-derived sites (name/distance matching, duplicate-prone Meta records) — roughly 1–2 weeks for a reviewed NZ+Pacific pipeline, plus a small recurring cost per monthly release using GERS changelogs. Adding the licence-text link and Foursquare notice to the map's attribution page is an hour.

## Foursquare Open Source Places

**Verdict: integrate-grade.** ~100M global POIs released November 2024, monthly refreshes (latest referenced 2026-08-11); distributed as Parquet via Hugging Face (foursquare/fsq-os-places), a public S3 bucket, and Snowflake Marketplace, plus a PMTiles vector build.

### Licensing

Apache License 2.0, confirmed on Foursquare's own access documentation (https://docs.foursquare.com/data-products/docs/access-fsq-os-places) — "Licensed under the Apache License, Version 2.0 (the 'License'); you may not use this file except in compliance with the License" — and independently on the Hugging Face dataset card (https://huggingface.co/datasets/foursquare/fsq-os-places: "The dataset is available under the Apache 2.0 license"). Apache 2.0 permits internal analysis, published redistribution on the public map, and derivative products, commercially, provided we retain attribution and the licence notice — a visible "Contains Foursquare Open Source Places data, Apache 2.0" credit plus the licence text in our data documentation satisfies the terms. Two cautions. The first caution is that Apache 2.0 is a software licence applied to data, so database-right edge cases in some jurisdictions are untested; the OSM community flagged this, and OSM's own ecosystem has proceeded with ingestion regardless. The second caution is directional: because religionmap's point base is OSM-derived, any FSQ-derived corrections merged into an ODbL database fall under ODbL share-alike — permissive-into-ODbL is the compatible direction, so this constrains nothing we do, but it belongs on record here.

### Resolution

Point coordinates per venue with address fields — the top resolution tier on paper. However, community testing outside the US found positional errors of hundreds of metres to kilometres in Europe, points on the wrong street side or outside the correct postcode; treat coordinates as approximate until conflated, especially outside the US where check-in density was low.

### Crosswalk

Easy at religion level, absent below it. The relevant node is Spiritual Center (4bf58dd8d48988d131941735) with roughly nine to fourteen children depending on taxonomy vintage: Buddhist Temple, Church, Hindu Temple, Monastery, Mosque, Prayer Room, Shrine, Synagogue, Temple, with newer releases adding Sikh Temple, Kingdom Hall, and Cemevi. These map cleanly onto LEXICON.md's place-of-worship concept and religion-level grouping, and Kingdom Hall and Prayer Room even capture the shared or non-church worship use the lexicon cares about. There are no denominational subtypes — no Anglican/Catholic/Methodist split, unlike OSM's denomination tag — so denomination must be inferred from venue names: feasible for NZ church names but review-dependent, which fits the RA evidence pipeline rather than automated ingestion.

### Access

No API needed: bulk Parquet, ~239GB total but column- and row-filterable, so a DuckDB or Polars query over the S3 bucket or Hugging Face parquet selecting the worship category IDs and target country codes yields a small extract without downloading the whole set. Hugging Face requires a free account and accepting access conditions; the S3 bucket and Snowflake listing are alternatives. Monthly release cadence; a Categories parquet ships alongside Places for the taxonomy join. Fields include date_created, date_refreshed, and date_closed — but community testing showed date_refreshed is unreliable, with 2024 dates on venues closed five-plus years.

### Value

A candidate-site feed and independent cross-check, complementing and never replacing statistics-office data. For each of the 100 country pages, an FSQ extract of Spiritual Center venues conflated against our OSM-derived points surfaces two lead types: FSQ venues absent from OSM (possible openings or OSM gaps) and OSM sites absent from FSQ. Known quality limits shape the workflow — roughly 30% unusable rates in one Swiss audit, stale check-in-era entries, duplicates, and weak coverage where Foursquare had few users, which likely includes most of the Pacific, so expect thin returns for Fiji, Vanuatu, and smaller islands, with NZ moderate. Every lead therefore enters the review queue as a place to check, never direct-to-map; the Apache licence means any FSQ-confirmed site can be published on the map with attribution. Dedupe against OSM is the main cost: name-plus-distance matching with generous distance tolerance given the positional error. Effort: 1–2 days for a DuckDB extraction of worship categories for NZ and Pacific country codes plus attribution wiring; 1–2 weeks for a reviewed conflation pipeline scaled across the 100 published pages.

## National charity registers — NZ, Australia, England & Wales, US

**Verdict: validation-only.** NZ Charities Register (DIA Charities Services, OData API), Australia ACNC Charity Register (data.gov.au), England & Wales Charity Commission full register download, US IRS Exempt Organizations Business Master File (EO BMF).

### Licensing

Licensing is open across all four registers, so the cap on this source is semantic — registered-office addresses are not worship sites — never legal. NZ (priority): the Charities Register open-data service is licensed CC BY 3.0 NZ per the DIA record on data.govt.nz (https://catalogue.data.govt.nz/dataset/charities-register-open-data; licence text at https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/nz/), permitting copying, redistribution, and derivative products with attribution to Charities Services/DIA; one caveat — the register carries officer names, and under NZGOAL practice personal information sits outside the CC grant, so use organisation-level fields only. England & Wales: the Charity Commission register-download page (https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/register/full-register-download) states verbatim "All content is available under the Open Government Licence v3.0, except where otherwise stated" — OGL v3.0 permits publication and derivative use with attribution. Australia: the ACNC register dataset on data.gov.au (https://data.gov.au/data/dataset/b050b242-4487-4306-abf5-07ca073e5594) carries CC BY 3.0 Australia (http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/au/). US: the IRS EO BMF page (https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/exempt-organizations-business-master-file-extract-eo-bmf) states no licence at all — it offers public CSV downloads with only a non-endorsement disclaimer — but as a US federal government work it is public domain under 17 U.S.C. §105; being strict, the page itself grants nothing explicit, so the public-domain position rests on statute rather than on stated terms. All four permit internal analysis, published redistribution, and derivatives; only the NZ personal-information carve-out and the CC/OGL attribution requirements constrain use.

### Resolution

Organisation-level postal and registered-office addresses, not buildings or worship sites — the weakest useful tier above admin polygons. NZ OData exposes full StreetAddress and PostalAddress fields (verified: 94 fields per Organisations record), geocodable via LINZ address data; E&W provides a contact address with postcode, geocodable via the ONS Postcode Directory (also OGL); ACNC carries town/state/postcode; the IRS BMF carries a mailing address. A geocoded register point may sit at a house, an office, or a PO box kilometres from the actual worship site, so these points must never be published as site coordinates.

### Crosswalk

Moderate-to-hard, in two layers. The first layer is classification: registers classify by charitable purpose, never denomination — NZ uses Sectors/MainActivityId ("Religious activities"), ACNC uses the "advancing religion" subtype, E&W uses charity_classification, and the IRS uses NTEE X codes, which give coarse tradition (X20 Christian, X30 Jewish, X40 Islamic, X50 Buddhist/Hindu and others) mapping cleanly to top-level tradition. The second layer is denomination itself, which must be parsed from organisation names ("Presbyterian Church of…", "Anglican Parish of…") — rule-list work that suits the project's reviewer workflow; LEXICON.md is a plain-language glossary rather than a formal category tree, so the crosswalk targets the master record's denomination fields directly. Known coverage biases complicate validation use: many US churches never enter the BMF (churches need not apply for exemption), E&W historically excepted many Church of England and other churches from registration, and AU Basic Religious Charities have reduced reporting.

### Access

All bulk, no auth, no meaningful rate limits. NZ: live OData API at odata.charities.govt.nz (Organisations endpoint, CSV or JSON, $returnall=true for a full extract including deregistered charities) — charities.govt.nz blocks generic fetchers with 403s, but the OData endpoint answers curl directly. E&W: thirteen zipped JSON/tab-delimited datasets plus a data-definition document. AU: a single CSV/XLSX on data.gov.au with a user-notes PDF; CKAN API available. US: state-and-region CSVs on irs.gov, ~1.96M organisations, refreshed roughly monthly (posting dated 2026-08-11).

### Value

Three concrete uses, all complementary to the statistics-office lane. The first use is a coverage-validation baseline: count registered religious bodies per locality and compare against OSM-derived site density to flag under-mapped areas — strongest in NZ, where the register is near-complete for active congregations. The second use is a candidate-site and closure-lead feed for the NZ pilot: the OData record carries RegistrationStatus, DeregistrationDate, and DeregistrationReasons, giving source-backed opening and closure leads keyed to the target years 2013/2018/2023 — a genuinely distinctive input the census lane cannot provide. The third use is a denominational-body crosswalk: register names give an authoritative list of denominational organisations per country for name-matching site records. The verdict is validation-only on semantic grounds: every licence permits publishing derivatives, but registered-office points would falsify site locations, so register data feeds the internal pipeline and reaches the map only after RA verification converts a lead into evidence. Coverage is four Anglosphere countries — no Pacific reach beyond NZ. Effort: the NZ pilot slice is roughly 1–2 weeks (OData ingest and geocode 2–3 days, denomination name-parsing rule list 3–4 days, coverage-validation join and deregistration-lead extraction 2–3 days); each additional register is about a week on a mostly reusable pipeline, and the IRS BMF's church undercount limits validation value there.

## Heritage and historic-building registers — NHLE, NZ Heritage List, UNESCO WHL

**Verdict: integrate-grade, register-by-register.** England's National Heritage List (NHLE), the NZ Heritage List / Rārangi Kōrero, the UNESCO World Heritage List, and the general pattern for other national registers.

### Licensing

Licensing splits cleanly by register, so the grade attaches register-by-register. England NHLE is now verified open, resolving round 1's unverified flag: Historic England's Open Data Hub terms (https://historicengland.org.uk/terms/website-terms-conditions/open-data-hub/) state "Subject to the Open Government Licence v3 (OGL), you are granted a worldwide, perpetual, non-exclusive licence to use the Historic England Data" for "commercial and non-commercial use", with a mandatory attribution chain — "© Historic England [year]" and, for spatial data, "Contains Ordnance Survey data © Crown copyright and database right [year]" — that must propagate into any sub-licence, plus a recommended currency-date statement; OS "may also claim Intellectual Property Rights" in spatial data derived from OS base mapping, so the OS attribution line is load-bearing for map republication. The NZ Heritage List is now resolved as all-rights-reserved, closing round 1's open question: heritage.org.nz terms (https://www.heritage.org.nz/terms-and-conditions) state "None of the content of this website may be reproduced, copied, used, communicated to the public or transmitted without the express written permission of Heritage New Zealand Pouhere Taonga, except for the purposes of private study, research, review or education" — internal analysis and validation sit inside the research exception, but publishing derived fields on religionmap.org needs written permission via information@heritage.org.nz (the CC 4.0 note in the data.govt.nz record is that site's own footer, never a licence on the dataset). UNESCO WHL is effectively closed per its syndication terms (https://whc.unesco.org/en/syndication/): "Any republication, online or in any other form, of any UNESCO/WHC data requires prior written authorization", syndication permission is personal and non-commercial only, and non-syndicated sections may not be scraped. General pattern: OGL-family jurisdictions (Historic Environment Scotland, Cadw) and CC BY or open-government registers (Ireland NIAH, US NRHP federal data, various Australian registers) likely mirror the NHLE position, but each register's terms must be read before ingest — heritage bodies are exactly the class of agency that defaults to all-rights-reserved, as NZ shows; the general-pattern claims beyond NHLE, NZ, and UNESCO are provisional, their terms not yet fetched.

### Resolution

Building-level, the best in the survey short of OSM itself. NHLE: a point per list entry (OS national grid) plus defined listing polygons for all new listings and substantive amendments since 4 April 2011, with the terms cautioning that the polygon shows the principal building, not curtilage. NZ Heritage List: a point per list entry (~5,700 entries; Category 1/2, historic areas, wāhi tapu). UNESCO WHL: ~1,200 site points worldwide, negligible coverage of ordinary places of worship.

### Crosswalk

Moderate, and asymmetric with the census sources: heritage registers carry no religious-affiliation taxonomy, so the crosswalk is extraction rather than mapping. Denomination lives in free-text names and list-entry prose ("Church of St Mary (Anglican)", "Wesleyan Chapel", "former Methodist church"); a rules-plus-review pass maps these onto LEXICON.md denomination values and feeds the opening/closure/change-date fields the lexicon defines — construction dates, and "former" flags signalling closure or changed use. Register categories map trivially onto the Place Of Worship and Mappable Site concepts. The hard join is entity resolution: matching register points to existing OSM-derived site_ids by name plus distance, with the register's list-entry number stored as a source identifier.

### Access

NHLE: no-auth bulk access via the Historic England Open Data Hub — CSV/GeoJSON/KML/shapefile downloads plus ArcGIS FeatureServer, WMS, and WFS endpoints; filter to places of worship by name and type (~14,500 listed places of worship in England). NZ: a public CSV export API exists (heritage.org.nz/api/register/GetRegisterResultsCsv/) and the List search supports use-type filters, but the terms above gate anything beyond internal research use; the ask fits the existing statistics-office outreach lane — one email to information@heritage.org.nz requesting written permission to republish derived fields with attribution. UNESCO: XLS/KML/GEORSS feeds exist, but republication is barred without a licence that "will imply a fee". Other jurisdictions: mostly portal downloads or ArcGIS services, terms to be read case by case.

### Value

Heritage registers buy three things no census or survey source in either round offers. The first is building-level provenance: authoritative construction dates and architects. The second is historic denomination at the site level, including "former church" entries that seed closure and change-date records for target-year analysis. The third is an independent candidate-site feed for pre-OSM-era buildings. For England this validates and enriches roughly 14,500 sites on an existing country page immediately. For NZ — the priority country — the List is the single best source of historic denominations and dates for the 2013/2018/2023 pilot and directly complements the Anglican review lane, but it is a permission-first source like the statistics offices. Pacific coverage is thin to nil, so the family supplements the NZ and England pages rather than the Pacific lane; it feeds the site-history layer, never the affiliation data. Effort: NHLE 2–4 days (download, filter, denomination extraction, fuzzy match to OSM points, attribution plumbing for the OGL+OS statement); NZ about an hour to draft the permission email now, reusing the statistics-office template, and 2–3 days of integration once permission lands, with internal validation use starting immediately under the research exception; UNESCO skip — near-zero value for effort plus a fee-bearing licence; each additional national register about half a day of licence reading plus 1–2 days of ingest where terms are OGL- or CC-grade.

## Denominational self-published directories

**Verdict: validation-only.** catholic-hierarchy.org, GCatholic, the LDS meetinghouse locator, the Adventist Yearbook and eAdventist, the WCC member directory, and Anglican provincial directories.

### Licensing

No directory in this cluster grants rights sufficient for republication on the map, and the two richest sources actively forbid the pipeline itself. The LDS terms of use (https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/legal/terms-of-use), read in full, state that materials "may not be posted from this site to another website or computer network without our prior written permission" and ban any "robot, spider, or other automatic device... to access this site for any purpose, including... monitoring or copying any of the material" — so even building an internal machine-readable copy of the meetinghouse locator breaches the terms; only manual lookup or written permission (cor-intellectualproperty@ChurchofJesusChrist.org) is compliant. The Adventist Yearbook front matter states "No part of the publication may be reproduced, stored in a retrieval system, or transmitted in any form... without prior written permission of the General Conference Office of Archives, Statistics, and Research" — note this quote was recovered from the Yearbook PDF (https://documents.adventistarchives.org/Yearbooks/YB2022.pdf) via search because the site itself returns 403 to fetchers, which is itself a signal; eAdventist is login-gated. catholic-hierarchy.org carries only "Copyright David M. Cheney" with no grant (https://www.catholic-hierarchy.org/notes.html); GCatholic publishes no terms at all (https://gcatholic.org/about.htm); the Anglican Communion site (https://www.anglicancommunion.org/) carries "© 2026 Anglican Consultative Council. All rights reserved." — all three default to all-rights-reserved, which is restricted, never open. The sole explicit grant is the WCC's Conditions for Use (https://www.oikoumene.org/conditions-for-use): "You are free to share, copy and redistribute all or part of our Website for non-commercial use only, provided that (a) no changes are made, (b) the source is acknowledged and (c) you do not suggest that the WCC endorses you or your use" — the no-changes condition is effectively no-derivatives, permitting verbatim citation but forbidding any merge of the member directory into a derived map layer. Net: nothing here is integrate-grade; LDS and Adventist need written permission for any structured use, and the rest are usable only as manually consulted verification references with citation.

### Resolution

Inverse to licence quality. The LDS meetinghouse locator holds true building-level points — the best spatial resolution in the cluster, under the worst terms. GCatholic holds coordinates for notable churches (basilicas, cathedrals) but not ordinary parishes. catholic-hierarchy.org is diocese level only, with no parish coordinates. The Adventist Yearbook holds postal addresses of conferences, missions, and institutions — geocodable, not geocoded — with partial congregation coverage. The WCC member directory is denomination and headquarters level with country membership counts and no site geometry. Anglican provincial directories sit at province/diocese contact level, with parish detail in per-province sites of wildly varying quality.

### Crosswalk

Trivial at the taxonomy level, hard at the entity level. Each directory is single-denomination, so it maps one-to-one onto a LEXICON denominational category with no classification ambiguity — the cluster's one genuine strength. The real work is entity matching: directories list congregations and administrative units, while the master record's unit is the mappable site; congregation-to-building matching is many-to-many (shared buildings, congregations without buildings, mission districts spanning islands) and needs the existing candidate-site review workflow, never a bulk join.

### Access

No APIs, no dumps anywhere in the cluster. catholic-hierarchy.org and GCatholic are plain-HTML browse — scrapeable technically, unlicensed legally. The LDS locator is a JS app whose terms explicitly prohibit automated access. adventistyearbook.org 403s non-browser clients; annual Yearbook PDFs are freely downloadable from documents.adventistarchives.org but carry the no-reproduction clause; eAdventist requires denominational credentials. The WCC directory is browsable HTML under the quoted non-commercial no-changes grant. Anglican provincial data requires per-province navigation — for NZ, the consent-first route through Peter Lineham and the Derbyshire estate, already in motion, supersedes anything on the public sites.

### Value

The cluster's value is as denomination-specific verification baselines, never as data feeds. Three uses. The first use is completeness checking: comparing OSM-derived point counts per denomination per country against Yearbook and directory counts flags under-mapped countries, and Adventist and LDS density in the Pacific (Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, Vanuatu) makes them the most relevant checks for the priority region. The second use is manual RA verification: a directory page is citable source-backed evidence for a single candidate site's existence, denomination, and dates under the existing review workflow, which uses the content as fact-checking — facts are not copyrighted — rather than republishing the compilation. The third use is as permission targets: the Adventist Office of Archives, Statistics, and Research and the LDS Intellectual Property Office are concrete addresses for the outreach lane, and denominational statistics offices are a natural extension of that lane. Nothing here yields a publishable layer. Effort: as an integration, zero — blocked by terms, do not build scrapers; as verification, one to two days per priority country to hand-compare directory counts against map counts and log discrepancies as review tasks; the two permission letters (Adventist ASTR, LDS IP Office) are an hour each on the existing templates, with any structured use gated on a written reply, realistically weeks to months.

## IPUMS International — census microdata with RELIGION variables

**Verdict: needs-permission.** Harmonised census microdata (Minnesota Population Center) with RELIGION/RELIGIOND variables, as a supplement to the statistics-office outreach lane.

### Licensing

The IPUMS International Terms of Use, fetched in full at https://international.ipums.org/international/terms.shtml, bind each registered user individually: "You will not redistribute the data" — extracts are for the licensee only, under IPUMS's agreements with national statistical offices — and use is limited to "scholarly research and educational purposes only", with commercial use "strictly prohibited"; the search-indexed clause adds that extracts "are provided for the exclusive purposes of teaching and scholarly research, and may not be used for any other purposes without explicit written approval from the relevant official statistical authority." Publishing research results is expressly permitted, and IPUMS staff have ruled on the official forum (https://forum.ipums.org/t/reproducibility-and-data-redistribution/3975) that "Sharing descriptive statistics, aggregate data, or analytical results derived from the microdata, however, is permissible." That ruling clearly licenses internal analysis and validation baselines now, and very likely covers publishing derived admin-level aggregates on religionmap.org; but the binding terms document itself never addresses aggregates, a continuously served public map layer is closer to a derivative data product than a scholarly publication, and the terms route "any other purposes" back to the statistical authority. The verdict follows: one confirmation email to ipums@umn.edu — citing the forum ruling, describing the map layer, naming the countries — before any IPUMS-derived aggregate ships on the public map; raw or per-record microdata can never appear or be re-shared regardless.

### Resolution

Admin polygons only — no points, no buildings. First-level administrative units for almost all samples, second-level for most, third-level for a select few; harmonised units are aggregated to meet a minimum-population disclosure threshold (IPUMS's standard is ~20,000 persons per identified unit), so small-area detail is capped. Aggregates tabulated from the microdata inherit that admin geography — one tier below what direct census tables sometimes offer, but sufficient for the region-map runtime's admin-polygon join.

### Crosswalk

Moderate, tending easy. RELIGION carries harmonised general codes (Christian, Muslim, Buddhist, Hindu, Jewish, No religion, Other, and kin) with moderate cross-sample comparability; RELIGIOND adds detailed codes, mainly splitting Christian denominations and the Other grouping, with country-specific depth. Because the codes are already harmonised across censuses, the crosswalk to the project's affiliation categories is a single mapping table plus per-country footnotes for residual-category lumping — easier than crosswalking each statistics office's native tables, at the cost of denominational detail where IPUMS collapses it. Per-country unharmonised source codes are also downloadable when finer fidelity is needed.

### Access

Free individual registration with a stated research purpose; every team member who touches the microdata must hold their own licence — no sharing extracts internally beyond licensees. Extracts are built through the web extract system or programmatically via the IPUMS API (ipumsr/ipumspy), so a reproducible R pipeline is straightforward; some samples require supplementary permissions. RELIGION coverage: 59 countries. Pacific priority hits: Fiji (1966, 1976, 1986, 1996, 2007, 2014) and Papua New Guinea (1980, 1990, 2000). New Zealand is not in IPUMS International, and neither is Australia nor the small Pacific states (Vanuatu, Samoa, Tonga, Solomon Islands) — the top-priority NZ/Pacific gap stays with the statistics-office lane. Other overlaps with the outreach lane: Mongolia and Malaysia (both already answered directly), plus large map countries such as India, Indonesia, Nigeria, Brazil, Egypt, Iran, Vietnam, Canada, the UK, Ireland, and about 45 more.

### Value

Three concrete buys. The first buy is gap-filling aggregates: for countries where direct census tables prove unobtainable from the statistics office, a weighted tabulation of RELIGION by harmonised admin-1/admin-2 unit yields exactly the affiliation-by-region layer the map needs — Fiji 2014 and PNG 2000 are the immediate Pacific candidates (PNG's is dated), and the 59-country list covers a large share of the 100 published pages. The second buy is a validation baseline: where the office lane already supplied tables, IPUMS tabulations independently check totals and category mappings. The third buy is time depth: multi-decade samples (Fiji back to 1966) support change-over-time context no single modern table provides. It complements, never replaces, the outreach lane: microdata are samples (typically 5–10%), so estimates carry sampling error and disclosure-driven geographic coarsening that full-count office tables do not. Effort: registration same-day; one confirmation email before map publication (days-to-weeks turnaround); roughly a week to build a reusable R (ipumsr) extract–tabulate–crosswalk–export pipeline with weighting and minimum-cell suppression, then roughly half a day per additional country; a Fiji-first pilot is 2–3 days end to end once the licence email clears.

## Regional survey programmes as context layers

**Verdict: context-layer-only.** Afrobarometer, Arab Barometer, Latinobarómetro, ISSP religion modules (GESIS), European Values Study (GESIS), Pacific Attitudes Survey.

### Licensing

All five usable programmes permit publishing our own derived national aggregates with citation while prohibiting redistribution of the underlying microdata; none grants an open licence. The load-bearing regime is GESIS, which governs both ISSP and EVS: its Usage Regulations (https://www.gesis.org/fileadmin/user_upload/Usage_regulations.pdf), read in full, release Category A data "for academic research and teaching", state "Un-authorized dissemination of the provided data to third parties is not permitted", and — the operative grant for a context layer — "Summarizing representations of the data typical to scientific works and presentations is allowed"; note also that "Storage and usage of the data by the user is limited to the user's project duration" and publications must cite the DOI and be deposited with GESIS. Afrobarometer (https://www.afrobarometer.org/data/data-usage-and-access-policy/): "Afrobarometer data are protected by copyright" and "Authors of any published work based on Afrobarometer data or papers are required to acknowledge the source", with geocoded data separately restricted. Arab Barometer (https://www.arabbarometer.org/policies/): download is free and published results are the author's responsibility, but "The Arab Barometer does not allow any full text reproduction of any Arab Barometer content on any website other than the Arab Barometer's own website" — our own computed aggregates are results, never reproduced content. Latinobarómetro publishes no formal licence text — its site (https://www.latinobarometro.org/documentacion-datos) states only registration-gated free download and a citation request; read adversarially, that is citation-conditioned tolerance, never a grant, so keep its use to one-line cited statistics. The Pacific Attitudes Survey has no public microdata licence at all; only its published reports in the ANU repository are citable. In every case internal analysis is fine, a one-line published aggregate with citation is defensible — explicitly so under GESIS's summarising-representation clause — and any microdata redistribution or bulk derivative product is off the table.

### Resolution

Country-level is the only design-reliable resolution for a religion context layer. Subnational identifiers exist (Afrobarometer regions, with geocoded enumeration areas under separate restricted access; Arab Barometer governorates; EVS NUTS regions; Latinobarómetro city/region; ISSP region varying by member), but national samples of ~1,000–2,400 do not support subnational religious-composition estimates, and Afrobarometer's fine geography is restricted anyway. Treat all as country polygons — the same tier round 1 assigned WVS.

### Crosswalk

Trivial at the tier the context layer needs: all programmes ask a religious-affiliation question whose major-tradition level maps directly onto LEXICON major categories. Below that it is programme-specific: Afrobarometer carries ~30 denominational codes (named Pentecostal and African-initiated churches) needing a moderate crosswalk if ever used; Arab Barometer distinguishes little beyond Muslim/Christian plus sect, which the LEXICON census taxonomy may not represent; Latinobarómetro's Catholic/Evangelical split and the EVS/ISSP denomination codes map cleanly. For one-line country context, crosswalk effort is near zero.

### Access

All are registration-gated static file downloads (SPSS/Stata/CSV) with no meaningful APIs: Afrobarometer merged-round files free after a short form; Arab Barometer free after a usage-monitoring form; Latinobarómetro free after registration at its Banco de Datos; ISSP (ZA7570, Religion IV 2018, NZ included with a 1991–2018 time series) and EVS (ZA7500, 2017 wave) require a GESIS account with stated academic purpose — and gesis.org sits behind a Cloudflare challenge, so downloads are manual, never scriptable. The Pacific Attitudes Survey (Samoa 2021, Vanuatu 2023) has no archived public microdata; access would mean emailing the ANU/Swinburne/La Trobe investigators, and its near-ceiling Christian religiosity yields little a census would not.

### Value

One-line national religious-composition context rows for country pages where no census religion question exists, exactly as round 1 scored WVS — and these four barometers fill WVS's coverage gaps: Afrobarometer adds ~39 African countries, Arab Barometer covers MENA states that never publish census religion data, Latinobarómetro covers 17–18 Latin American countries (including Argentina, which last asked census religion in 1960), and EVS covers European countries without a census religion question (France, Belgium, the Netherlands). ISSP's NZ Religion module (1991–2018) is a bonus validation series against NZ census target years rather than a gap-filler. The Pacific — the priority region — is the one place this candidate buys nothing: PAS is two countries, politically focused, licence-less, which reinforces rather than relieves the statistics-office outreach lane. Effort: roughly one to two days per programme for a one-off weighted national tabulation (design weights checked, major-tradition recode, citation string stored alongside the value), so about one to two weeks for all five; no pipeline or refresh obligation, since waves arrive every few years; GESIS adds account friction and a publication-deposit obligation worth a calendar note.
