# Pin-to-portal nomination brief (2026-10-15)

Status: DESIGN. Joseph B proposed the idea on 2026-10-15 and ruled the same day on the six questions recorded in section 4 and on the review class in section 7. The design precedes any code. Under AGENTS.md, this brief needs a routine review and a reserved-model review before it merges, and the implementation follows in a separate pull request.

## 1. The problem

A research assistant (RA) browsing religionmap.org who notices a missing place of worship must leave the map they are looking at, open the contributor portal, find the same spot again, and start a nomination there. The public map already holds the spot: a right-click, or a press-and-hold on a phone, drops an amber planning pin at it. Today that pin serves only to measure distances. Here, we let the pin start a nomination in the portal at the pin's position, while the pin continues to measure.

## 2. What exists

Three existing pieces carry most of the design.

The first piece is the planning pin in `apps/regions/_shared/region-map.js`, shared by the global map and every country page. A right-click, a press-and-hold, or a precise address search drops a draggable amber pin; while the pin is down, popups give distances and walking directions from it. A click or tap on the pin removes it, as does the site reset.

The second piece is the public map's hand-off to the portal. Each place's popup carries "Revise this place", a same-tab link to `apps/regions/nz/verification.html?country=xx&revise=1&lat=..&lng=..` with the place's name and OSM identity. Pages whose configuration names their own portal link there instead, without a `country` parameter. The portal parses the link in `deepLinkContextFromParams` and holds it as `pendingDeepLink`; the sign-in card names the place, and `applyPendingDeepLink` opens the revise card once a contributor is signed in. Sign-in uses Google Identity Services rendered in the page, so the page stays loaded and the link's parameters survive sign-in.

The third piece is the portal's own nomination flow. `enterPinMode` opens an entry, `placePin` drops a draggable pin, and the RA drags the pin onto the building, zooms to at least `PIN_MIN_PLACEMENT_ZOOM` (15), and confirms the location. After confirmation, the portal lists existing tasks within the proximity radius and asks whether the pin marks one of them, before the entry form opens. In the quick-entry lane (`RAPID_NOMINATION_ENTRY`), the portal keeps a confirmed pin entry on the device and resumes it after a reload (`resumeRapidPinFromDevice`).

## 3. The design

The design adds a third hand-off mode, `nominate`, beside `revise` and `report_issue`. The public map builds the link; the portal opens its existing nomination flow at the linked point. All entry, proximity checking and review stay in the portal, as AGENTS.md requires: a nomination is a provisional claim, and the public map writes nothing.

### 3.1 The pin card on the public map

The pin card gives the pin a second use and keeps its first. We make six changes in `region-map.js`.

1. **Dropping the pin is unchanged.** A right-click, a press-and-hold, or a precise address search drops the pin, and distances and directions are measured from it.
2. **A click or tap on the pin opens a small card at the pin.** The card shows the pin's coordinates to five decimal places (formatted with `toFixed(5)`), a link labelled **Nominate missing PoW here**, and a **Remove pin** button. Escape, a click on the map, or the start of a drag closes the card; a click on the pin after a drag reopens it with the new position. **Remove pin** takes over from the click on the pin as the way to clear it, and the site reset still clears it too.
3. **The card stays closed when the pin drops.** Most visitors use the pin to measure, and a card on every drop would cover the map. Both drop hints change from "tap it to remove" to "tap it for options": the hint after a right-click or press-and-hold, and the hint after a precise address search.
4. **The card and the link use a wrapped longitude.** MapLibre reports unwrapped longitudes after a long pan (Wellington can read 534.7762), so the card and the link use the pin's longitude wrapped into the range −180 to 180 (`LngLat.wrap()`), as the country lookup already normalises it.
5. **The link names the country as the revise link does.** On a page whose configuration names its own portal, the link goes to that portal without a `country` parameter. Otherwise, on a country page the link uses the page's country, and on the global map it uses the country under the point (the page manifest, then the world outlines). Where that lookup finds no country, as over the sea, the card shows only the coordinates and **Remove pin**.
6. **One function builds the revise and nominate links.** `portalReviseHref` becomes a shared builder that takes the mode as an argument. The nominate link is `…/nz/verification.html?country=<code>&nominate=1&lat=<6 dp>&lng=<6 dp>`, or the configured portal with `nominate`, `lat` and `lng`. The link opens in the same tab, as the revise link does; after the browser's back button, the public map reloads without its pin.

The card leaves the public page's security as it stands. The card's DOM nodes carry only numbers and fixed text, set through `textContent`. The link is a same-origin navigation, which no directive of the content security policy governs. The policy itself is unchanged, and `scripts/stamp_csp.mjs --check` must pass without restamping, because the stamp hashes inline scripts only and the change touches none. The sign-in token stays with the portal.

### 3.2 The portal's handling of the link

The portal treats a nominate link as a held request to open its nomination flow at a point. We make six changes, in `apps/regions/nz/js/verification-map.js` and, for the notice in change 5, `apps/regions/nz/verification.html`.

1. **The parser learns the mode.** `deepLinkContextFromParams` returns `mode: "nominate"` whenever `nominate=1` is present, with `latitude`, `longitude` and `hasCoords` as for revise links. For nominate links, each coordinate must be present and, after trimming, match `^-?\d+(\.\d+)?$` before it is converted with `Number` and checked against the range; absent, empty, whitespace-only, hexadecimal, exponent and partly numeric values fail, and `hasCoords` is then false. The link carries only the point, since the place is unrecorded. When several mode flags are present, revise wins, then report-issue, then nominate.
2. **Sign-in holds the link.** The constructor keeps a nominate link as `pendingDeepLink`, as it keeps a revise link. Signed out, the sign-in card reads "Sign in to nominate the place at the pin (-41.28650, 174.77620); it opens here after sign-in", with the coordinates formatted by `toFixed(5)` from the parsed numbers. When `hasCoords` is false, the card reads "Sign in to nominate a missing place of worship; the link held no usable location, so you will drop the pin yourself." Sign-in and the access check stay as they are: access still comes only from an RA account, and a visitor without one stops at sign-in, as on the portal's front page. `pendingDeepLink` holds one request: a later "Sign in to revise" in the portal replaces a held nominate link, the sign-in card then names the place to revise, and `history.replaceState` removes `nominate`, `lat` and `lng` from the address so that a reload cannot restore the replaced link.
3. **Held links are applied in one place.** `maybeOpenIssueDeepLink` centres the map for any link with coordinates, as now, and returns for both revise and nominate links without applying them. `onBackendSignedIn` alone applies a held link, after its own repaint, whether the RA pressed the Google button or arrived with a sign-in kept on the device. Today a kept sign-in applies a revise link inside `maybeOpenIssueDeepLink`, before `onBackendSignedIn` repaints the detail panel and wipes the revise card; the change fixes that case too.
4. **The nominate branch decides precedence first.** `applyPendingDeepLink` gains a nominate branch, which runs in assignment mode only. Outside assignment mode (`?full=1` or `?batch=all`, which the public map never sets, or a deployment without a backend, where nobody can sign in), the link is not applied; where a contributor has signed in, the branch drops the link and `#copyStatus` says that nominations from the map open in the standard portal. In assignment mode, the branch first checks for a saved entry (change 5), before calling `setPortalMode("add")`, because `setPortalMode` itself resumes a saved entry when the mode changes to add. With no saved entry, the branch calls `setPortalMode("add")`. If the mode is not add afterwards, because the RA kept unsaved changes at the portal's existing prompt, the branch stops and shows the notice of change 5. Once `applyPendingDeepLink` has cleared `pendingDeepLink`, the branch keeps the link's point in its own field, `mapPinRequest` (the coordinates, `hasCoords`, and whether the link has placed a pin), which the notice and change 6 read; `pendingDeepLink` stays free for `requestSignInToRevise`. Otherwise it centres the map on the point at zoom `PIN_MIN_PLACEMENT_ZOOM` or above, calls `enterPinMode`, and calls `placePin` at the point. The pin is unconfirmed: the public map passes no placement zoom, so the RA drags the pin onto the building and confirms it, and the proximity check then runs as for any nomination. A nominate link without usable coordinates opens pin mode with no pin, and the status line asks the RA to drop the pin.
5. **A saved entry comes first, and one notice offers the link.** A saved entry is one that `resumeRapidPinFromDevice` would resume on this page: the device record `powRapidDraft:<CC>:rapid-pin` with a `pin` whose coordinates are finite, while the quick-entry lane (`RAPID_NOMINATION_ENTRY`) is active. In the detailed lane, where the resume never runs, the branch applies the link without the check and leaves any quick-entry record on the device for the next quick-entry visit. The record is keyed by the page's country, so an entry saved under another country is neither seen nor touched. Typed values without a pin are not a saved entry, and they are restored into the new entry as the portal restores them today. When a saved entry exists, the branch lets it resume (through `setPortalMode("add")`, or through `resumeRapidPinFromDevice` when the mode is already add) and shows a notice. The notice sits in a new element, `#mapPinNotice`, in the sidebar outside `#detailPanel`, so later repaints of the detail panel leave it in place. It reads "A nomination from the map is waiting at (-41.28650, 174.77620)", or, when `hasCoords` is false, "A nomination from the map is waiting; the link held no usable location", and offers **Nominate at the map pin** and **Dismiss**. **Nominate at the map pin** calls the existing `discardEntryAttempt`, which asks for confirmation when there is something to lose, clears the `rapid-pin` record and the entry's period cards (`clearGuidedPeriods(RAPID_PIN_PERIODS_KEY)`), exits pin mode and repaints the detail panel; the button then runs the branch again. If the RA cancels the confirmation, both stores stay as they were. The same notice serves the unsaved-changes case of change 4, where the button runs the branch again and `setPortalMode` asks its usual question.
6. **A link is applied once.** `history.replaceState` removes `nominate`, `lat` and `lng` from the address, and keeps every other parameter (`country`, `batch`, `detailed`, `email` and the rest), at four moments: when the RA confirms the pin the link placed, when the RA leaves the entry the link started, when the RA dismisses the notice, and when a revise request replaces the held link (change 2). Each moment also clears `mapPinRequest`. Before then, a reload applies the link again, which is safe because nothing has been saved. After confirmation, the pin is saved on the device in the quick-entry lane, so a reload resumes the entry and starts no second one. Revise links keep their current behaviour.

The rest of the flow is the portal's existing behaviour. The entry form is the form of the country's lane: the quick-entry form, or the detailed form when `detailed=1` or the country has no quick-entry lane. In the quick-entry lane, the entry follows the pin's country, so a link that named the wrong country corrects itself once the pin is placed; the detailed lane records the page's country, as it does today. A pin placed by the branch skips the offer to revise an existing place that a click on a recorded dot gives while aiming, as the resume path already does; the proximity check after confirmation still lists nearby tasks, and we accept the difference. The nomination goes through the existing submission mutations: quick-entry nominations keep their per-user and global intake limits, and the detailed lane's `createManualCandidateTask`, which has no intake limiter, stays as it is. Convex functions, schema and deployment stay as they are.

## 4. Rulings recorded

Joseph B ruled on six questions on 2026-10-15.

1. **Who may nominate from the pin:** signed-in RAs only. Every visitor sees the action, and the portal's existing sign-in gate applies. The public tip lane in the contributor-access ruling is a separate piece of work.
2. **The measuring pin stays.** The pin keeps measuring, and the card adds the nomination action beside **Remove pin**.
3. **The card stays closed on drop.**
4. **An unfinished entry saved on the device comes before a held link**, and is discarded only on the RA's confirmed choice.
5. **The hand-off is a link with a new mode** (section 3), chosen over two alternatives. Passing the point through `sessionStorage` would fail across tabs and duplicate the link mechanism. A nomination form on the public map would load sign-in and backend code on every public page, against the content security policy work of 2026-10-15, and would create an entry route outside the portal.
6. **Nominations carry no route tag.** A tag in `clientContext` would let us count nominations started from the map, but the quick-entry mutation validates `clientContext` against a closed object (`rapidClientContext` in `convex/rapidEntry.ts`), so the tag would need a Convex change and a deployment. Joseph B ruled against the tag, and the implementation leaves `convex/` untouched.

## 6. Tests

Node tests run in CI for four units.

1. **The parser:** a nominate link with valid coordinates; an absent `lat`, an empty `lat=`, whitespace, hexadecimal (`0x10`), exponent (`1e1`), out-of-range and partly numeric (`12abc`) coordinates, each giving `hasCoords` false; combined mode flags; revise and report-issue links unchanged. These cases extend `apps/regions/nz/js/deep-link-context.test.cjs`.
2. **The link builder:** a configured portal; the page's own country; the country under the point on the global map; a point at sea; an unwrapped longitude (534.7762) written as its wrapped value. `region-map.js` runs only in a page, so the test lifts the builder into a `vm` context, the convention of `counts-label.test.cjs`.
3. **The precedence check:** a saved entry with finite coordinates, a record with typed values only, an entry saved under another country, a quick-entry record on a `detailed=1` page, and no record; and a check that only the confirmed button discards a saved entry. The check is a pure function exposed on `NzVerificationMap`, as `deepLinkContextFromParams` is.
4. **The card:** lifted into a `vm` context with a minimal `document` stub; text set through `textContent`.

DOM tests in the style of `portal-walkthrough-dom.test.cjs` cover the call order, which the pure function cannot see:

- arrival with a sign-in kept on the device, for a nominate link and, as a regression check, a revise link;
- a saved entry when the portal mode changes to add, and when the mode is already add;
- unsaved changes kept at the mode prompt;
- a nominate link replaced by an in-portal "Sign in to revise", followed by a reload, after which the replaced link stays gone;
- the discard button with populated period cards: confirming clears the `rapid-pin` record and the period cards, and cancelling clears neither;
- a saved entry with a nominate link whose `hasCoords` is false;
- `detailed=1`, and `full=1` outside assignment mode.

Browser checks run in two passes. The headless pass uses Chromium with the working tree routed under `https://religionmap.org`, and the live content security policy therefore applies, as in the policy checks of 2026-10-15. We require zero policy violations. On the public map, the pass covers a right-click, the card, the card's link, the link after a drag, a press-and-hold and a tap in phone emulation, both drop hints, pins on a country page, on a page with a configured portal, on the global map and at sea, and a pin dropped after panning across the antimeridian. In the portal, signed out, it covers the sign-in card for a nominate link with and without usable coordinates. The signed-in pass is run by hand in a real browser, because the backend accepts Google sign-in only: the portal is served from a localhost origin that the Google OAuth client allows, against an isolated local Convex backend so that nothing reaches the live database, and Joseph B signs in. The committed policy's `connect-src` names the hosted backend only, so the served copy, and only the served copy, adds the local backend's origin to `connect-src` and points the client configuration at it; neither change is committed, and the headless pass checks the committed policy. It covers the unconfirmed pin, the proximity check, confirmation, a submitted quick-entry nomination, the saved-entry notice and its button, the unsaved-changes notice, arrival with a kept sign-in, and a reload before and after confirmation.

## 8. Scope limits

The design covers RAs with portal accounts. Public tips from visitors without an account belong to the contributor-access lane. The pin card offers nomination only; revising a recorded place stays with the place's own popup. The portal's nomination flow, proximity check and review queue are used as they stand, with one repair: a revise link applied after a kept sign-in (section 3.2, change 3).
