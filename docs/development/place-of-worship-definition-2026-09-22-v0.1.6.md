# Operational definition of a place of worship

Version: 0.1.6. Date: 2026-09-22. Status: adopted working operational definition. This dated snapshot preserves version 0.1.6. The [canonical operational definition](../operational-definition.md) presents the current version.

**At a specified time, a place of worship is a reproducibly mappable site for which source evidence supports recurring religious worship by or for a community.**

## Purpose and entity

A definition serves a purpose. This project asks what sites of collective worship do for the communities who express religious piety there, in one form or another, and through them for the societies around them. Those are functional questions, and answering them requires consistent counting of such sites across places and times. Over-counting and under-counting are themselves vague standards; what the project can secure is a stated rule, applied the same way to every case, with examples that show how the edge cases count.

A place of worship is an entity described by six kinds of attribute. Spatial attributes say where it is and at what precision. Temporal attributes say when it existed, when worship recurred, and when it changed. Worship attributes say what recurring worship occurs and how often. Community attributes say by or for whom. Functional attributes say what the site does for that community, the matter the project exists to study. Relational attributes link it to organisations and congregations, to the places of worship that preceded and succeeded it, and to the components of a complex. Every attribute has two kinds of imprecision, which the record keeps apart: uncertainty, where evidence about a matter of fact is incomplete, and vagueness, where the boundary of the concept itself is indistinct, as in how often worship must recur or where a compound ends. Better evidence reduces uncertainty; a stated rule settles vagueness.

The identifier tracks the identity of this entity through time. Attributes change and the identifier stays; the identifier changes only when the entity does. The section on identity states when that happens, and the [2026-09-22 discussion of counting places and coherent worship complexes](pow-counting-and-complexes-discussion-2026-09-22.md) works through the examples. Version 0.1.6 adopted that discussion's rule for replacement venues and shared rooms; the boundary of a coherent complex remains open there.

## Criteria

A site meets the definition when source evidence supports all three criteria:

1. **Reproducibly mappable site.** Preserved location evidence allows investigators to identify the same physical place at the spatial precision required by the study. The site may be a building, parcel, compound, room, adapted or shared venue, temporary structure, or natural place.
2. **Recurring religious worship.** Evidence supports repeated or established worship use. Weekly services, seasonal practice, and an established annual pilgrimage may each qualify. Uncertain recurrence remains provisional. For the census-year derivation, a recorded period of worship at any known frequency establishes the place as in use for the years it covers, and each such year also carries a level of use: regular (weekly or more, monthly, or several times a year) or intermittent (annual or occasional). Only a period whose frequency of use is uncertain covers its years without establishing use; those years derive as uncertain until a reviewer decides on the evidence (rulings R-F1 and R-F1′, 2026-09-03).
3. **Worship by or for a community.** Worship occurs by or for a congregation, resident religious community, or defined institutional population. *For* admits worship conducted on a community's behalf: a chaplaincy serving a hospital, prison, or military population, or a ritual specialist performing worship for a community that does not assemble as a congregation. The criterion therefore does not presuppose that the community is the agent of worship. Restricted access is compatible with community use.

Private devotion, religious affiliation, sacred significance, religious architecture, an organisation's address, and a one-off ceremony are candidate evidence assessed against all three criteria. Privacy, access, and cultural rules govern collection, storage, and publication.

## Identity over time

A place of worship is one entity at one site, and its identifier follows the place of worship as a place of worship. It keeps its identifier through change in its fabric, its organisations, its communities, and its activity. A building burnt down and rebuilt, restored, enlarged, or moved within the site is the same place of worship, with the change dated in its spatial and temporal attributes. The site is the unit at the spatial precision the study requires (criterion 1). A building shifted within its parcel or compound therefore stays within the same site. A change of religion, denomination, congregation, or organisation while worship continues at the site is a dated change in its community attributes. Inactivity is an attribute too: a place whose worship has ceased while its site stands and no other use has claimed it is inactive, keeps its identifier, and switches back to active if worship resumes.

A place of worship ends when its recurring worship ceases and the site is destroyed, repurposed, or abandoned, with no community continuing or succeeding to its worship use there. Whether a long gap is an inactive period or an end is a reviewer's recorded judgement on the evidence, with the basis and the alternative kept. The definition sets no duration threshold. A later recurrence of worship at the same location, by a community with no succession to the earlier use, is a new place of worship with its own identifier, linked to the earlier one by location. Location alone never establishes identity: the ground is a spatial attribute, and a place of worship is more than its ground (ruling, 2026-09-22).

A move to a different site begins a new place of worship. The place at the new site has its own identifier, linked to its predecessor as a relational attribute, and the entity at the old site stays in the record, inactive or ended (ruling, 2026-09-22). The community's continuity lives in the link; the identifier stays with the site.

A place of worship is neither a building nor an organisation. An organisation may conduct worship in many places; each mappable place of collective worship is its own unit, and organisations and congregations are recorded as linked entities. A building or compound may hold several places of worship, each at its own site within it, as a hospital with a chapel and a separate prayer room does. A room shared by several communities, at the same hours or at different ones, is one place of worship with several community attributes.

Identity is not observable at a site visit. Whether worship at a location continues an earlier place of worship is an inference from evidence about the site, the community, and its practice. The project therefore records identity as an explicit, revisable review decision with a stated basis — same place, conversion, successor at a new site, end and new foundation, split, merge, or uncertain — and no import pipeline or proximity heuristic settles identity silently.

A shared representative point is evidence about location alone. Two distinct hospital chapels known only to the same hospital centroid remain two places of worship drawn at the same point; identity requires evidence about the places the point represents (recorded 2026-09-22).

### Worked examples

Each example states the arrangement, the count of identifiers, and the reason. These are the cases reviewers meet, and the ones most often counted wrongly.

- **Christ Church Cathedral and the Transitional Cathedral, Christchurch.** Two. The cathedral is one place of worship, inactive since the 2011 earthquakes, active again if its community returns after the reinstatement, and ended if they never return or the site is destroyed. The Transitional Cathedral, opened in 2013 on another site and known as the Cardboard Cathedral, is a second place with its own identifier, linked to the first. Both count while both are in use, and the second counts for as long as worship continues there, whether or not the first reopens.
- **Hagia Sophia, Istanbul.** One. A church from the fourth century, a Latin church from 1204 to 1261, Orthodox again to 1453, a mosque to 1935, a museum to 2020, and a mosque since. Each conversion continued worship at the site under a new community and is a dated change of community and worship attributes; the museum years are an inactive period; one identifier throughout.
- **The Templo Mayor and the Metropolitan Cathedral, Mexico City.** Two. The Templo Mayor was enlarged and rebuilt over its earlier stages some seven times between the fourteenth century and 1521: one place of worship with dated structures, orientations, and rituals. Its worship ended with its destruction after the conquest. The cathedral, begun in 1573 within the Aztec sacred precinct and built in part from the temple's stone, is a new place of worship with no succession to the earlier use, born across the decades of its construction and dated as an interval. The reused fabric and the shared ground are relational attributes, and neither makes the two places one.
- **A building moved within its site.** One. A church shifted within its parcel is the same place of worship; the move is a dated spatial attribute.
- **A room that passes from Catholic to shared use.** One. The change of community is dated; the identifier stays.
- **A hospital prayer room shared by Muslims and Catholics, at the same hours or at different ones.** One, multi-faith, with the communities, rituals, and schedules as attributes. Private prayer and last rites conducted there are worship attributes of that place. An isolated bedside rite elsewhere in the hospital is evidence of activity to assess against criterion 2, and by itself no place.
- **A hospital with a distinct Catholic chapel and a distinct Muslim prayer room.** Two. Each room is its own site within the building; the hospital's roof and ownership unite nothing.
- **A cathedral with several altars, a convent with several worship rooms, a worship campus with several buildings, or three adjacent temple buildings forming one coherent worship complex.** One each. The components and their differing rituals and recurrence are attributes of the complex, recorded when the source distinguishes them and never required at entry. Recorded component activity keeps its scope and is never summed, since one ceremony can appear under several components; the complex counts at most once at an observation time.
- **Two adjacent, independently established places of worship.** Two. Adjacency, shared ownership, and a common roof establish no complex. Where a coherent complex ends remains open, and the [counting discussion](pow-counting-and-complexes-discussion-2026-09-22.md) works through it.
- **Two distinct hospital chapels known only to the same hospital centroid.** Two, drawn at one point, as above.

## Measurement over time

The analytical unit is a place of worship's time-indexed **worship-function state**. Record physical existence, worship use, location, organisations, congregations, timing, and uncertainty as distinct claims. The `site_id` identifies the place of worship as an entity; dated occupancy records give the years its site was in use, and geometry history dates changes within the site.

Source records, OpenStreetMap objects, and field observations provide evidence about the worship-function state. Reviewer acceptance establishes supported claims as recorded states and longitudinal events. An accepted record may preserve a time-bounded history after worship use ends. Preserve the source or community description alongside any project classification.

Each study states its target population, observation times, evidence standard, and filters.

Counting follows from the recorded attributes and a study's declared rule. Contributors estimate the frequency of worship in the existing categories, state the years, decade, or bounded period the estimate covers, and record their confidence; entry asks for no judgement about any study's threshold. A statement that places worship somewhere within a decade differs from one that places it throughout the decade, and the record keeps the difference. Confidence and frequency are distinct: a confidently evidenced annual service is certain knowledge of rare use, and an uncertain weekly estimate is another evidential state. An investigator declares an activity threshold and an uncertainty treatment, and each place of worship, a complex included, counts at most once at an observation time. Unknown activity stays unknown and never derives as zero.

A country is an index derived from location, never a claim about the place. Each record carries the present-day country under its point, resolved from the location at entry and recoverable from the geometry at any later time; the contributor's own position or the page they opened plays no part. Where a study needs the polity a place stood in at a given date, that is a time-indexed claim recorded with the place's history, distinct from the index (recorded 2026-09-23).

## Revision note (0.1.5 → 0.1.6)

This revision follows the project lead's rulings of 2026-09-22, given while reviewing the [discussion of counting places and coherent worship complexes](pow-counting-and-complexes-discussion-2026-09-22.md). The lead defined a place of worship as "an entity with spatial attributes, temporal attributes, worship attributes, community attributes, functional attributions, and relational attributions", each with "degrees of uncertainty" and "degrees of inherent vagueness", tracked by identifiers over time; stated that the project's interests "derive from functional questions about what sites of collective worship do for communities who express religious piety, in one form or another, there"; and asked for consistent counting with a clear definition and examples for the edge cases. The new section on purpose and entity records this.

On identity the lead ruled in the morning: "new identifier at a new site. This allows us to count the Cardboard Cathedral, which may persist after the old cathedral is rebuilt", and "movement of a building on the same site would not count" as a new place. Christ Church Cathedral "counts as one, and its attributes reveal it has perished for now. If the community returns, its attributes will switch to active. If they never return, or if the site is destroyed, it ends. If a mosque is built over it, we would provide a new identifier, because spatial attributes are insufficient to establish that an entity is a PoW; there is a new recurrence of religious worship by and for a different community." In the afternoon the lead tested the rule against site repurposing: "practically it would not matter to counting. We just have a new site born and an old site lost." The Cathedral of Mexico "was born in the late 16th century (here there is inherent vagueness as it was built over many years), and it is a new site with a new id." Hagia Sophia is entered as one place with dated status attributes, "Eastern Orthodox church (c. 360–1204; 1261–1453), Catholic church (1204–1261), Mosque (1453–1935; 2020–present), Museum (1935–2020)", which "is how I would enter it into our data too."

The two afternoon cases fixed the rule's form. A first draft of this revision stated identity as the joint continuity of site and community; that form gives Hagia Sophia five identifiers, so it was dropped. Version 0.1.6 states identity as continuity of the place of worship as a place of worship at its site. A conversion in which worship continues under a new community is a dated attribute change. A place ends when worship ceases and the site is destroyed, repurposed, or abandoned with no community continuing or succeeding to its use; a later recurrence there without succession is a new place linked by location, which is the mosque-over-the-cathedral case and the Templo Mayor and the Metropolitan Cathedral. A move to a new site is a new place linked to its predecessor, which is the Transitional Cathedral. Whether a gap is inactivity or an end is a reviewer's recorded judgement; the definition sets no duration threshold. The identifier decision changes no cross-sectional count; it changes site age, duration, and turnover, which are plausible exposures for the project's questions.

Versions 0.1.0 to 0.1.5 let one identifier follow a community to a new site and treated the temporary venue as an occupancy of the same place. Version 0.1.6 reverses that. The [country backend scheme's relocation rule](../country-backend-scheme.md) already gave a relocating congregation a new destination `site_id`, and JW's working definition of 2026-09-21 already made the site the persistent entity, so the definition now agrees with both. The review basis vocabulary gains "conversion" and "end and new foundation", and "relocation" becomes "successor at a new site".

The worked examples were recovered from the lead's conversation with Codex on 2026-09-22 and its private record, `pow-research/docs/development/pow-counting-ruling-2026-09-22.md`, together with the cases the lead raised in this revision: the two Christchurch cathedrals; Hagia Sophia; the Templo Mayor and the cathedral of Mexico; the building moved within its site; the room passing from Catholic to shared use; the shared multi-faith prayer room with private prayer and last rites as attributes and the isolated bedside rite as activity evidence; the distinct chapel and prayer room in one hospital; altars, convent rooms, a campus, and adjacent temples as one complex with component activity kept in scope and never summed; independent neighbours as two; and two chapels at one centroid. The counting paragraph under Measurement over time records the lead's instruction that entry takes a frequency estimate with coverage and confidence, "no settling at entry", and that investigators choose thresholds, with a decade's coverage kept distinct from a year's.

The revision also brings the canonical page level with its dated versions. Amendment R-F1′, recorded in version 0.1.5 but absent from the canonical page since 2026-09-03, now stands in criterion 2. The country-index paragraph (recorded 2026-09-23) and the shared-point sentence (recorded 2026-09-22), added to the canonical page without a snapshot, are now versioned here. The shared-room and distinct-chapel examples follow the discussion's table; the boundary of a coherent complex, changes to that boundary, component activity, and unresolved membership remain the discussion's four open questions.

Implementation now lags the definition and is follow-up work, listed so no reader takes this text as a description of the running system. The occupancy validator in `convex/lib/occupancies.ts` still rejects a successor identifier on a relocated period and requires a relocated period to be followed by a period at a different place; both checks encode the superseded rule. The [portal location and occupancy plan](../portal-location-and-occupancy-plan.md) allows `successor_site_id` only for a split. The review-decision vocabulary in the task layer lists `relocation` as an identity basis. Accepted identifiers, observations, and derived states are unchanged by this revision; a study that adopted the earlier rule states which version it used.
