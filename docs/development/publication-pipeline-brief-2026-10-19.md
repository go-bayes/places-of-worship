# Publication pipeline brief: from a batch release to a public release, with Kendall as the fixture (2026-10-19)

Status: DESIGN. This brief designs the path from a principal investigator's (PI) batch release to a public release, as the amended section 7 of the [vocabulary reconciliation](c2-vocabulary-reconciliation-2026-08-14.md) requires, with the Kendall church of [EC-001](../edge-cases.md) as the end-to-end fixture, and it extends that path backwards to the intake of corrections by an agent, with Kendall as the first AI-filed correction and the research assistant (RA) sheet kept as the fallback. The requirements it implements are recorded in the project's decisions register as ruling D25 and its eleven-item amendment; the register is private, and the brief cites its rulings by number and states their content. The brief adopts nothing by itself: under AGENTS.md a design brief needs a routine review and a reserved-model review before it merges, and every pull request it proposes needs its own authority. Section 12 lists what we verified in code and what we infer.

## 1. The problem

The project can review a place of worship, accept the review and freeze the accepted evidence, and there the governed chain stops. Nothing turns a frozen export into an accepted event, a rebuilt master or a public map. The one bridge that exists, `scripts/build_occupancy_dated_places.py`, reads a Convex export directly, draws a dated point only where a reviewer confirmed a derived census-year location, and takes religion and denomination from the matched OpenStreetMap (OSM) feature, so a reviewed denomination stays off the map and the OSM dot a correction replaces stays on it. Kendall shows every gap at once: the OSM node lies 87 m from the building with religion recorded and denomination blank; the correction is a change in our knowledge, which the 2026-08-22 census design would have counted as a change in the world; and the public map lacks any means of replacing the dot.

D25 settles the model. Accepted claims enter a continuous log at each PI batch release; the master is a replay of that log; once a year the project pins an edition at a knowledge cut-off and publishes a census of change, a revision ledger and an onset-unknown class. Its amendment settles the operating terms: a public release follows each batch release and is published automatically with a receipt and a withdrawal path; a reviewed record replaces the OSM dot it corrects in the default view, with grid-thinned groups recomputed before publication; the event contract gains a correction scope and an accession intent; the ODbL question gates the first public reviewed release; each edition carries a provisional OSM tier; outputs are called an annual register. Here, we design the machinery those rulings depend on, in the order the data flow: the contract change, agent intake of corrections, `pow accept`, replay, `pow export` and the release, the annual edition, the Kendall fixture, the pull requests, and the rulings JB still owes.

Write state(*S*, *T*) for the state of the world on date *S* as reconstructed under D3 from events accepted by audit time *T*. Let *S*<sub>Y</sub> be 1 September of year Y and *T*<sub>Y</sub> the cut-off of edition Y, 1 December under amendment item 1. The brief uses these symbols throughout.

## 2. What exists

We read the code and contracts on branch `main` at `78bcaa0e`. Eight facts set the design.

1. **The `pow` verb set stops before acceptance.** `crates/pow-cli/src/main.rs` defines `validate`, `validate-agent`, `stage`, `propose`, `diff`, `object` (hash and verify) and `export verify`. The verbs `accept` and `rebuild-master` are absent, and `export` only verifies. `pow propose` translates staged RA rows into `worship_function_observed` events with deterministic identifiers (`deterministic_uuid` over a seed; verified), which is the pattern `pow accept` can reuse. The staging database holds `stage_batches`, `stage_records` and `validation_diagnostics` only. Of the seven D9 gates, `pow validate` implements schema (gate 1), source presence (gate 5) and the idempotency key (gate 7), plus coordinate ranges and date ordering; country containment and relocation distance (gate 2), future-date and source-coverage checks (gate 3), the 50 m duplicate-risk flag (gate 4) and taxonomy membership (gate 6) remain unimplemented.
2. **The canonical serialisation D15 names exists.** `crates/pow-cli/src/canonical.rs` implements `pow-canonical-json.v1` (RFC 8785) and the `pow-object.v1` hash, checked against `schemas/fixtures/pow-canonical-json.v1.json` and shared with `convex/lib/canonicalJson.ts`. The change-event schema's `payload_hash` is described as "hash of the normalised payload" without naming a canonical form; section 3 pins it.
3. **The frozen bundle is verifiable, with one known limit.** `pow export verify` checks a materialised `pow-export-bundle.v1` directory against its own manifest in six groups (manifest, files, record counts, membership, pins, acceptance). The bundle carries sixteen files, including `tasks.jsonl`, `review_decisions.jsonl`, `evidence_versions.jsonl`, `review_snapshots.jsonl`, `site_occupancies.jsonl` and `derived_year_locations.jsonl`. The review snapshot's content hash uses an older canonical form the Rust crate has never implemented, so its content is checked by presence and cross-reference only. A withdrawn or superseded batch still serves its bytes, and `getExportBundle` reports `disposition.processing_allowed`; the materialised directory omits that disposition, and `getExportBundle` is an action that requires an authenticated, active curator or admin (`requireActingUser`), so a deployment credential alone cannot call it ([frozen exports](frozen-exports.md); verified).
4. **The contract binds `site_created` to `lifecycle`** and the correction types to `correction`; a `site_location_corrected` event carries `effective` and leaves its scope unstated (`schemas/change-event.schema.json`, `allOf`; the sample in `docs/examples/revisions/nz-sample-change-events.jsonl` has `effective_date: 2018-03-06`). `GeometryPayload` carries `previous_geometry_history_id`, an immutable reference; `DenominationPayload` carries `previous_denomination_code` and `StatusPayload` `previous_status`, which are values and can recur across several intervals. `GeometryPayload` carries `uncertainty_radius_m` and no location grade, while `locationConfidenceGrade` (`convex/lib/locationAssertions.ts`) derives `building` from the assertion mode `building_identified`, which carries no radius, and `parcel_or_compound` from a radius of 100 m or less. `EventTarget` offers `matched_current_site_id` as a temporary reference until accepted `site_id`s exist.
5. **The public map's dots come from two grid-thinned PMTiles tiers**, `places-overview-v2-20260722-grid` (zooms 0 to 5) and `places-v2-20260722-grid` (zooms 6 to 18), built by `tools/tiles-r2/build_tiles_grid.py` from a 22 July 2026 extract of 2,072,349 features, of which 701,349 carry no OSM key. At zooms 0 to 6 a dot carries `religion`, `denomination`, `country_code` and `n`, the number of places it stands for; `osm_id` and `osm_type` appear from zoom 7; zooms 8 to 18 are the July tiles unchanged (`ZOOM_SPEC`, `THIN_ATTRS`, `SLIM_ATTRS`; verified). The Worker serves versioned names matching one pattern, `places-overview-v2|places-v2|ra-dots-<cc>` followed by a date and one optional suffix (`tools/tiles-r2/worker/src/index.js`, `VERSIONED`), with a year-long immutable cache, and a versioned key, once written, is immutable.
6. **The runtime already filters both dot layers.** `applyFilters()` sets one filter expression on `LAYERS.places` and `LAYERS.overview` from `currentFilterClauses()` (`apps/regions/_shared/region-map.js`, lines 2844 to 2874), and `syncPlaceDotEra()` hides or fades the snapshot tiers by points mode (lines 5192 to 5235). The dated tier shows only in period mode, or in all mode when the census year lies more than `PLACE_SNAPSHOT_HORIZON` (15) years back.
7. **Agent intake accepts a research dossier, and nothing else.** `convex/internalAgentIntake.ts` `ingestBundle` validates a bundle with `validateAgentReviewBundle` (`convex/lib/agentIntake.ts`), writes a task in batch `internal-agent-research` with country `NZ`, geometry from `dossier.candidate_location`, `matched_osm_id` parsed from `place_ref`, and an evidence draft marked `agent_intake_only`, plus the advisory review, claim-grain judgments and an immutable receipt. The bundle schema (`scripts/agent_research/schemas/agent-review-bundle.v1.json`) lacks every field a correction needs: a location assertion, an original point, a denomination relation, a label basis and a change class; the task's `source_context` carries origin, bundle hash and submission key only, without an `issue_report`. The validator requires `place.country_code === "NZ"` and an allowlist version it knows (`nz-v1`); a parish's own domain is refused until an allowlist names it. The submit command accepts the `dev` and `local` selectors only. An `agent_intake_only` draft cannot be accepted for export (`convex/reviews.ts`, line 270) and is excluded from the external AI review queue (`convex/claudeReviews.ts`, line 155). All verified.
8. **A task's location assertion is written at creation only.** `initial_location_assertion` is set by `createIssueTask`, `createManualCandidateTask`, rapid entry and batch import; `evidence:saveEvidenceDraft` takes a task id, a draft and client context, and no mutation updates the assertion on an existing task (verified by search). PI acceptance (`convex/acceptances.ts`) refuses the PI's own submission and permits the PI to ratify a decision the PI recorded, recording `self_decided: true` on the row (`isSelfDecided`, `convex/lib/acceptance.ts`).

Two standing designs frame the work. The [content-addressed review contract](content-addressed-review.md) (approved 2026-09-11) lists what an accepted event must reference: proposal hash, accepting decision hash, PI batch-release hash, evidence-version hashes, payload hash, source-manifest references, schema and taxonomy versions; it requires that withdrawal and acceptance share a processing guard; its third step (proposal pinning), fourth step (export queue and PI batch release) and fifth step (`pow` verification, acceptance and replay) are unbuilt. The [human review and release design](human-review-and-release.md) (2026-09-23) names the stages after a freeze, accepted master change and published output, as planned. The live gate is per-item PI acceptance plus a curator freeze.

## 3. The change-event contract, version 2

Items 4 and 5 of the amendment change the contract, so version 2 of the schema is the first pull request. We propose a new file, `schemas/change-event.v2.schema.json`, beside the current file, which stays as version 1 (the repository already keeps `indicator.schema.json` beside `indicator.v2.schema.json`). Events declare `schema_version: "change-event.v2"`. `pow validate` accepts both versions by the declared version; `pow accept` writes version 2 only; the version 1 examples stay valid under version 1. Version 2 makes seven changes.

**Change 1: `correction_scope`.** A top-level property, `correction_scope`, with the values `whole_validity` and `interval` and the schema default `whole_validity`, permitted only when `event_intent` is `correction` and refused on every other intent. An accepted event (`review.review_status: accepted`) must carry the value explicitly: `pow accept` writes the default into the event it appends, so the log is complete without any reader applying a default; a reader that meets an accepted correction without a scope is reading a defective log. A staged or proposed event may omit it. When the scope is `interval`, a required companion object `correction_interval` carries `valid_from` and `valid_to` (dates; `valid_to` null for open-ended), and `pow validate` refuses `interval` without it.

**Change 2: the state a correction rewrites is named, immutably.** A previous value (`previous_denomination_code`, `previous_status`) identifies a state ambiguously, because the same value can hold in several intervals of one site's history. Version 2 therefore adds `corrects_state_ref`, required on every accepted correction and refused on every other intent: `{ opened_by_event_id }`, the `event_id` of the live event that opened the state being corrected (for the registered location state, the `site_registered` event; for a later denomination state, the `denomination_changed` event that opened it). `previous_geometry_history_id` and the previous-value members stay as human-readable context and must agree with the referenced state, or validation refuses. Under `whole_validity`, replay rewrites the referenced state across its whole valid interval; under `interval`, replay splits the state at `valid_from` and `valid_to` and rewrites the middle piece. A correction is therefore confined to the state it names: a later observed change or lifecycle event opens a new state with its own interval, and a successor site is a different `site_id` under the operational definition (version 0.1.6). Two live corrections of the same state without a supersession link between them are a conflict: `pow accept` refuses the bundle with `adjudication_required`, and the resolution is a reviewed event that supersedes one of them (D12), never the newer one winning by date.

What `effective` means on a correction. D3 defines `effective` on a correction as the time the corrected fact became true. On a `whole_validity` correction replay reads `effective` for the D9 date-plausibility gate and for nothing else: the correction contributes no transition at any *S*, and the rewritten state keeps the interval the referenced state had. For Kendall, the corrected fact (the church stands on its building) held before any evidence we hold, so the fixture records `not_later_than` equal to the earliest evidence date with `date_precision: bounded`. The sample event's `effective_date: 2018-03-06` becomes harmless under version 2 because the scope says what the date is for.

**Change 3: accession and within-site movement as paired types.** D4 pairs event types with intents so that a contributor must choose (`site_location_corrected` against `site_relocated`). We follow that pattern twice. Version 2 adds the event type `site_registered`, bound to a new intent value `accession`, with the existing `site_created` payload (`payload_type: site_created`, carrying `site_id`, `country_code`, `geometry`, `status`). `site_created` stays bound to `lifecycle` and means founding or opening. Replay reads `site_registered` as the site entering the record with presence bounded by its `effective`: with `not_later_than` set, the site is present at every anchor *S* at or after that date; at anchors before it, with `not_earlier_than` absent, the bound straddles *S* and the site contributes uncertainty, which is the onset-unknown class of D25. An accession contributes no transition to the census of change. Version 2 also adds `site_location_changed`, intent `observed_change`, payload `geometry_update`: a building moved or rebuilt elsewhere within its site, which the operational definition keeps under one identifier and which is a change in the world, so the census counts it. The alternative to a paired accession type, widening the intents `site_created` may carry, would leave the type name saying "created" on a place that existed for a century, and we recommend against it.

**Change 4: the location grade travels with the geometry.** `GeometryPayload` and `SiteCreatedPayload` gain `location_confidence`, the grade `locationConfidenceGrade` derives from the confirmed assertion (`exact`, `building`, `parcel_or_compound`, `street`, `locality`, `area`), and `location_assertion_mode`. `uncertainty_radius_m` is carried only when the assertion carried one. Replay copies the grade into the state table and D13's publication gate reads it; nothing downstream infers a grade from a distance.

**Change 5: `payload_hash` pinned.** Version 2 defines `payload_hash` as the `pow-object.v1` hash (`sha256:` followed by the hex digest) of the `pow-canonical-json.v1` bytes of the `payload` object. Version 1 left the normalisation unnamed. The content-addressed contract requires the same canonical form for every review object, and D15 needs a hash whose inputs are defined.

**Change 6: provenance on the review reference.** `ReviewRef` gains the members the content-addressed contract lists for an accepted event: `export_batch_id`, `export_manifest_hash`, `evidence_version_hash`, `review_snapshot_hash`, `review_decision_hash`, `proposal_hash` and `batch_release_hash`. The first five are required on an accepted event. `proposal_hash` and `batch_release_hash` are nullable until the contract's third and fourth steps ship, and become required for events accepted after each ships. Accepting events while those two steps are unbuilt is an exception to the approved contract, and section 11 puts it to JB as a ruling rather than treating it as compliance. Within the exception, translation is versioned (`pow-translate.v1`, section 5), so the events are a mechanical function of the reviewed evidence version and the decision, and the receipt carries a `pow diff` of the translated events for the PI's record.

**Change 7: retraction of an accepted event.** The log is append-only, so a withdrawal after acceptance cannot edit `review.review_status`. Version 2 adds the event type `event_retracted`, intent `review_state`, payload `proposal_state` with `reason`, naming the retracted events in `supersedes_event_ids`. Replay treats an event as live at *T* when its `recorded_at` is at or before *T* and no retraction recorded at or before *T* names it; when a superseding event is retracted, the event it superseded becomes live again. A state computed at an earlier *T* therefore contains the event still, which is what an audit of an earlier release needs. A correction that replaces a wrong event continues to use the D12 pattern, a new accepted event whose `supersedes_event_ids` names the old one, and replay applies the newest live event in a supersession chain. A retraction enters the log through `pow retract --event <id> --instruction <reference>` under JB's written instruction, recorded in the event's `review.rationale`; it is the one first-year event the review queue does not produce.

Fixtures ship with the schema: the Kendall events of section 9 as positive cases; and negative cases for `correction_scope` on an `observed_change` event, `interval` without `correction_interval`, an accepted correction without an explicit scope or without `corrects_state_ref`, a `corrects_state_ref` disagreeing with the previous-value member, two live corrections of one state, a denomination value recurring in two intervals with a correction naming the second, `site_created` with intent `accession`, `site_registered` with intent `lifecycle`, an accepted event whose `payload_hash` disagrees with its payload, a geometry payload with a radius and the grade `building`, and a `site_registered` event with no `not_later_than` and no `effective_date`. The same pull request wires D9 gates 2, 3 and 6 into `pow validate`: country containment and relocation distance, future effective dates on completed events and effective dates outside the source's coverage, and taxonomy membership against the pinned instance (`schemas/denomination-taxonomy.json`, version `2026-06-12.1`, which carries `christian.baptist`; verified). Gate 4 belongs to acceptance (section 5).

## 4. The intake end: an agent files a correction

Today the agent lane carries a research dossier about a place: claims with cited locators, an advisory review by a second provider, run manifests, a quarantine block for personal details, and a deterministic submission key. The dossier has a `candidate_location` with a basis and radius, but nothing says "the record's point is wrong and here is where the place is", nothing records which denomination relation or change class the claims support, and intake writes a plain task, with no original point for a reviewer to rule between. We verified each absence in section 2, fact 7. The design below adds a correction proposal to the dossier, lets intake create a revision task of the same shape the portal's revise flow creates, adds the one write path a human preparer needs, and keeps every safeguard the lane has. A dossier without the new block behaves as today.

### 4.1 What the portal records for a revision

The portal's revise flow (`enterReviseMode` and `createRevisionTask` in `apps/regions/nz/js/verification-map.js`) calls `tasks:createIssueTask` with the confirmed point as the task geometry, the record's point as `originalLatitude` and `originalLongitude`, the OSM identifier as `osmId`, and a `locationAssertion` of `contract_version: location_assertion_v1` with `mode` (`building_identified` or `approximate_area`), `basis`, `confidence`, an optional radius and wording, and `contributor_confirmed: true`. The server stores `source_context.issue_report.original_point` as `[longitude, latitude]` and `initial_location_assertion` (`convex/tasks.ts`, `createIssueTaskHandler`), checks the assertion against the task point (`assertAssertionMatchesTaskPoint`), and derives the master-data location grade from mode and radius (`locationConfidenceGrade`). The contributor then submits an observation whose draft carries `change_class` (`genuine_change`, `map_correction`, `uncertain`), `denomination_or_tradition_raw`, `denomination_label_basis` and `denomination_relation` (`label_only`, `record_correction`, `historical_change`, `shared_or_concurrent_use`, `uncertain`) from `convex/model.ts`. At review, `assertLocationOutcome` (`convex/lib/locationOutcome.ts`) requires a `location_outcome` of `accept_moved_point`, `keep_original_point` or `uncertain` whenever the task has an original point and the pin moved. All verified.

### 4.2 The correction proposal in the dossier

We add one optional object to the dossier, `correction_proposal`, in a new schema version `agent-dossier.v2` with the bundle at `agent-review-bundle.v2`; the three validators (Python `lib.py`, TypeScript `agentIntake.ts`, Rust `validate-agent`) pin the schema file, so a version bump is the honest form of an additive change. The object has five parts.

1. `target`: `place_ref` (the OSM object the correction concerns, which must equal `dossier.place.place_ref`), `original_latitude` and `original_longitude` (the record's point as the source edition shows it), and `source_edition` (the tileset or extract name, such as `places-v2-20260722-grid`).
2. `moved_point`: `latitude`, `longitude`, `basis` from the dossier's existing location bases (`osm_object`, `address_geocode`, `source_map_or_photo`, `imagery`, `reader_inference`), an optional `outline_ref` (an OSM way or relation whose outline the point lies within), and `supporting_claim_ids`. Absent when the proposal corrects attributes only.
3. `location_assertion_proposed`: `mode`, `basis` (from the portal's assertion bases, which for an agent will be `named_source_description` or `other`), `confidence`, `uncertainty_radius_m` and `source_wording`. The object omits `contributor_confirmed`, because confirmation is a human act. The human preparer confirms in step 4.4.
4. `denomination`: `denomination_raw`, `label_basis` (one of the portal's bases, which for a website is `current_self_description` or `named_documentary_source`), `denomination_relation`, `taxonomy_code_suggested` (checked against the pinned taxonomy, advisory only) and `supporting_claim_ids`.
5. `change_class`: `map_correction`, `genuine_change` or `uncertain`, with a `basis` string and `supporting_claim_ids`.

Every `supporting_claim_ids` entry must name a claim in the dossier, every cited claim must have a source on the allowlist, and the advisory reviewer's `claim_checks` must cover them; a proposal whose supporting claims the reviewer marked `not_supported` or `not_checked` is admitted with the reviewer's outcome attached, for the human to see, because the lane records disagreement rather than resolving it. The runner's personal-details screen already walks every string in the dossier, so the new strings are screened without new code; the hex-digest rule applies to them as to every other field.

### 4.3 What intake writes

When a bundle carries `correction_proposal`, `ingestBundle` creates the task as a revision of the existing record, with the same fields the portal's revise flow produces: `geometry` at the moved point (or the original point when there is none), `matched_osm_id` and `osm_object_type` from `place_ref`, `source_context.issue_report` with `original_point`, `source_edition`, `evidence_lane: "agent_correction"`, `source_title` and `source_url` of the first supporting claim, and `automated_checks` carrying the proposal summary. It leaves `initial_location_assertion` empty, because the contract requires a confirmed assertion and confirmation waits for the preparer; it stores the proposed assertion inside `source_context.issue_report.location_assertion_proposed` for the preparer. The evidence draft stays `agent_intake_only`, and gains `change_class`, `denomination_or_tradition_raw`, `denomination_label_basis` and `denomination_relation` copied from the proposal, so the preparer sees them in the draft they will clone. The receipt, the submission key, the bundle hash, the advisory review and the judgments are written as today. Two new judgment rows record the researcher's correction proposal and the reviewer's verdict on it (`judgment_kind: correction_proposal`), so later calibration can count how often a model's moved point survived human review.

Three safeguards need extension rather than preservation. First, the validator's country check and the allowlist are New Zealand-only; a United States run needs `country_code` generalised to the registry and an allowlist version `us-v1` whose domains include `openstreetmap.org` and, for the pilot, the church's own domain, since the lane refuses a parish domain until an allowlist names it. Second, the batch `internal-agent-research` and the fixed `target_years: [2013, 2018, 2023]` become per-country, read from `convex/lib/countryYears.ts` as the portal does. Third, the submit command's deployment selector admits `dev` and `local` only, so a run against the deployment the live portals use needs either an explicit allowance for a named deployment under JB's instruction or a run on a personal development deployment; section 11 puts the choice to JB. The ingest flag (`POW_INTERNAL_AGENT_INGEST_ENABLED`) stays per deployment and per authorised run.

### 4.4 Preparation, review, and the humans the Kendall run needs

The lane's authority boundary stands: an `agent_intake_only` draft cannot be accepted for export, and the ordinary path is a human-owned draft prepared from the dossier ([internal agent review](internal-agent-review.md), "Workflow and authority"). Preparation needs one write path that does not exist (section 2, fact 8). We add `tasks:confirmLocationAssertion({ taskId, locationAssertion, proposedAssertionHash })` for the roles `ra`, `reviewer`, `curator` and `admin`: it requires a task whose `issue_report` carries `location_assertion_proposed` and whose `initial_location_assertion` is empty, requires `proposedAssertionHash` to equal the hash of the stored proposal (so the preparer confirms what they read), runs `assertCountryAllowsAssertionMode` and `assertAssertionMatchesTaskPoint`, stores `initial_location_assertion` with `contributor_confirmed: true` and the preparer as `confirmed_by`, appends a task event `location_assertion_confirmed`, and refuses a second confirmation. The task row is covered by review snapshots, so any snapshot taken before confirmation goes stale and the reviewer decides on the confirmed state. The portal's task panel gains a control for the preparer, and the RA guide gains the step.

The preparer then opens the task, reads the dossier and the advisory review, confirms the assertion, creates the ordinary draft with `evidence:saveEvidenceDraft` carrying `change_class: map_correction`, the denomination fields and the receipt identifier in its source notes, and submits it. The task is then in the same queue as any RA revision, with the original point and the moved point for the reviewer to rule between. The reviewer records `accepted_for_export` with `location_outcome: accept_moved_point`, `identity_decision: same_place` and `denomination_relation` as filed, on the current snapshot. The PI accepts. A curator or admin composes and freezes the batch.

The code enforces a two-person minimum: the submission's author may not review it, and the PI may not accept the PI's own submission, while the PI may ratify a decision the PI recorded, with `self_decided: true` recorded on the acceptance (section 2, fact 8). For Kendall the roles are an operator who runs the agent and submits the bundle (JB, or an agent under his written instruction, since the flag and the deployment are his); a preparer who owns the ordinary draft and may be any RA, reviewer or curator; a reviewer other than the preparer; JB as PI; and a curator or admin who freezes, who may be the preparer or the reviewer. JB cannot be the preparer, because acceptance refuses his own submission. Under the code as it stands JB may be both reviewer and PI, with the self-ratification recorded; whether the first AI-filed correction should instead require three distinct people (preparer, reviewer, PI) is a policy choice we put to JB in section 11. The RA sheet and the portal's revise flow remain the fallback: an RA who files the same correction by hand produces the same task shape, and nothing downstream distinguishes the two routes except `evidence_lane`.

### 4.5 The Kendall bundle

The bundle an agent would submit for Kendall, abbreviated and illustrative in its identifiers, carries a dossier for `osm:node/358729928` ("Wayside Church", country `US`, seed from the public map) with four claims and a correction proposal. The claims: a `name` claim, "Wayside Baptist Church", from the church's visit page (`https://waysidemiami.org/visit/`, `source_type: church_website`, retrieved 2026-10-19, `evidential_weight: primary_institutional`); an `address` claim, "7701 SW 98th Street, Miami", from the same page; a `denomination` claim, "Baptist", from the same page, with the quoted support being the congregation's own name; and a `service_pattern` claim, Sunday worship at 11:00, from the same page, which supports `status_assessment.current_status: active` as of 2026-10-19. The `osm_version_chain` records node 358729928's versions from the OSM API with each version's tags summary, from which section 9 takes the onset bound: the earliest version whose tags carry `amenity=place_of_worship`, which is worship evidence, rather than the node's creation, which need not be. The correction proposal: `target` node 358729928 at its tile position, `source_edition: places-v2-20260722-grid`; `moved_point` at the centroid of relation 6474428 (`basis: osm_object`, `outline_ref: osm:relation/6474428`), with the claim that the relation is tagged `building=church` at 7701 SW 98th Street; `location_assertion_proposed` with `mode: building_identified`, `basis: named_source_description`, `confidence: high`, wording "the church's visit page gives 7701 SW 98th Street; OSM relation 6474428 is the church building at that address"; `denomination` with `denomination_raw: Baptist`, `label_basis: current_self_description`, `denomination_relation: record_correction`, `taxonomy_code_suggested: christian.baptist`; `change_class: map_correction`, basis "the congregation has not moved; the source point sits beside its building". Private correspondence stays outside every citation: the correspondent's confirmation identified the case and appears nowhere in the bundle, as EC-001 records.

## 5. `pow accept`

`pow accept <bundle_dir> --log <log_dir> --lease <lease_file>` turns a verified frozen bundle into accepted events and appends them to the master log. It has no network access and reads no clock except once, to stamp `recorded_at` on the events it writes and to compare with the lease; the stamp is recorded in the segment manifest. Its steps are fixed.

1. **Verify.** Run the six check groups of `export verify` on the directory and refuse on any error. The review snapshot's content hash, which the crate cannot recompute today, is recomputed under the older canonical form implemented in Rust in the same pull request, so the pins check becomes complete.
2. **Hold the processing guard.** The content-addressed contract requires acceptance and withdrawal to share a guard. Two additive Convex mutations provide it. `exports:claimForProcessing({ exportBatchId, processorId })` (role `curator` or `admin`, or the recorded curator's user id for a service call, as the freeze chain already does) moves a `frozen` batch to `processing` with a lease (`processing_lease: { processor_id, expires_at }`, fifteen minutes, renewable), and refuses a batch in any other status; `exports:withdrawExportBatch` keeps requiring status `frozen`, so a batch under lease or already accepted cannot be withdrawn, and a batch withdrawn first cannot be claimed. `exports:recordAccepted({ exportBatchId, processorId, segmentManifestHash })` moves `processing` to `accepted`, stores the segment manifest hash, and is idempotent for the same hash. The publication job claims, writes `lease.json` (batch id, processor id, expiry), runs `pow accept`, which refuses when the lease file is absent, names another batch or has expired by the run stamp, and then records acceptance. An interrupted worker leaves a lease that expires; the next run claims again, finds the segment already written for that manifest hash (step 3) and records acceptance without rewriting. A lapsed lease with no segment is a clean retry. After `accepted`, the only reversal is a retraction event (section 3, change 7).
3. **Idempotence.** Compute the bundle's `manifest_hash` and look for a segment already named by it in the log manifest chain. If one exists, print its receipt and exit 0 without writing: the same bundle accepted twice is one acceptance, which is D9 gate 7 at bundle grain. A bundle whose `supersedes_export_batch_id` names a batch already accepted is accepted with supersession by claim: each new event supersedes the earlier live event of the same type for the same site and attribute, and only that one. `site_registered` is never superseded by a replacement bundle, so identity and the registry survive a re-export that changes one attribute; identity ends only through `duplicate_merged` or a retraction. Independent facts the replacement leaves unchanged stay live.
4. **Replay the current log** for the bundle's country (section 6) to build the identity index: source key to `site_id`, and the registry's positions. At first-year volumes the replay is cheap; it also keeps acceptance reading the log itself rather than a registry file that replay could contradict (D5).
5. **Associate before minting (D9 gate 4).** For each task, resolve identity in this order: a source key already in the index; else the decision's `identity_decision` (`same_place` names a site, `duplicate` names a survivor, `successor_at_new_site` names a predecessor, `new_candidate` asserts a new place); else, when no decision speaks, look for registered sites within 50 m of the task point (country-configurable) and for other tasks in the bundle sharing a key or a point. A neighbour found with no identity decision naming the relation refuses the bundle with `adjudication_required`, naming the task and the neighbour; the case returns to review for a decision. An unseen source key alone never establishes a new site.
6. **Translate.** For each included task, take the decision `pow export verify` identified as authoritative (the latest accepted acceptance's decision), the evidence version it pinned, the task row with its confirmed assertion, the occupancies and derived year locations, and produce events by the rules of table 1 under the translation contract `pow-translate.v1`, whose rules are versioned so that the same inputs always give the same events. Every identifier is deterministic: `event_id` is `deterministic_uuid` over `(manifest_hash, task_id, evidence_version_hash, event kind, index)` and `client_event_id` is the same seed in clear, so a re-run on the same bundle writes the same bytes. `recorded_at` is the run's single stamp. `review` carries `review_status: accepted` and the provenance members. `payload_hash` is computed under `pow-object.v1`. `taxonomy_version` is the pinned taxonomy's version. The location grade and mode come from the confirmed assertion through `locationConfidenceGrade`. Translation refuses the whole bundle, naming the task, when any event fails `pow validate` under version 2, when a denomination code fails gate 6, when a correction would conflict with a live correction of the same state, or when a culturally sensitive payload lacks `sensitivity_basis`. Sensitive payloads enter the log (D18 allows it); the public projection gates them (section 7).
7. **Mint identity.** A task resolved in step 5 as a new place receives a `site_id`, and the translation emits `site_registered` as the first event for that site. The `site_id` is `deterministic_uuid` over `("site", country_code, source key or candidate id, manifest_hash)`, so the same bundle mints the same identifier on a re-run, and the identifier lives in the event payload, so replay reads identifiers and mints none. OSM identifiers become the crosswalk: the `site_registered` event's `source_refs` carry `source_record` entries with `source_dataset_id` (the extract, `osm-places-20260722`) and `source_record_id` (`node/358729928`), and the registry replay derives (section 6) records each key with a role, `point_source` for the object whose position the record had, `outline_source` for a building outline cited as geometry basis, `address_source` for an address point. The public form is `sameAs` on the site record, as `schemas/site.schema.json` already allows. Convex mints no `site_id`; a task's `candidate_site_id` is a Convex locator and stays out of every public product.
8. **Append.** Write one segment file, `events/<cc>/<recorded_at>-<manifest_hash:12>.jsonl`, one canonical JSON event per line in `event_id` order, and a segment manifest under `schemas/data-manifest.schema.json` (`dataset_role: accepted_export`, `pipeline_stage: accepted`, `input_manifests` naming the bundle manifest hash, `supersedes_manifest_id` naming the previous segment manifest, every durable file hashed). The log is the chain of segment manifests; nothing edits a segment once written. Print a receipt: bundle id, manifest hash, segment path and hash, event count, sites minted, run stamp, and the `pow diff` of the translated events.

## 6. Replay: `pow rebuild-master`

`pow rebuild-master --log <log_dir> --country <cc> --as-of <T> --anchors <first>..<last> --out <dir>` computes state(*S*, *T*) for every anchor *S* in the range and for *S* equal to the date of *T*, and writes the per-release site-state tables. It is a pure function of the log segments, the pinned taxonomy file and its arguments: it reads only the files whose hashes the rebuild manifest records, and it uses neither clock, network nor geocoder (D15). Where a product needs an area assignment (section 8), the boundary file is an input with a recorded hash, and point-in-polygon runs deterministically inside the rebuild.

The algorithm has four passes.

1. **Select.** Read every segment of the country's log whose manifest `recorded_at` is at or before *T*; keep events with `recorded_at` at or before *T*; drop events named by an `event_retracted` event recorded at or before *T*, reviving any event those dropped events superseded; in a supersession chain keep the newest live event.
2. **Timeline.** For every site and attribute, build state intervals from the non-correction events. Each interval carries four dates: the opening bound (`open_not_earlier_than`, `open_not_later_than`; an exact date sets both) and the closing bound (`close_not_earlier_than`, `close_not_later_than`; an open interval has none). An event with only `not_earlier_than` opens an interval whose `open_not_later_than` is unknown, so the interval is certain nowhere until a later event or assertion bounds it; a lower bound is never read as an occurrence date. Events are ordered for interval construction by `open_not_later_than`, then `open_not_earlier_than`, then `recorded_at`, then `event_id`, which is a total order. A `worship_function_observed` event contributes two things: a sighting, which asserts presence of the observed attribute at its `effective` date and tightens the bounds of the interval containing that date; and its `target_year_affects`, which are reviewer-approved assertions about named anchors (basis `reviewer_inference` or `source_statement`) and set the attribute's status at that anchor directly, whatever the observation's date. Without such an assertion an observation says nothing about an earlier anchor.
3. **Corrections.** Apply correction events in `recorded_at` order to the state each `corrects_state_ref` names (section 3), rewriting under `whole_validity` or splitting under `interval`.
4. **Evaluate.** For each anchor *S*, for each site and attribute, the state is `present` (or the attribute's value) when `open_not_later_than` is at or before *S* and either no closing bound exists or `close_not_earlier_than` is after *S*; `absent` when *S* precedes `open_not_earlier_than` or follows `close_not_later_than`; and `uncertain` otherwise, with the straddling bound recorded. A site whose presence is `uncertain` at *S* because its opening bound straddles *S* is in the onset-unknown class for *S*. Transitions between two anchors are `certain` when both bounds of the transition fall inside the interval between the anchors and `possible` when a bound straddles an anchor; the census reports the two counts apart.

Outputs, all canonical JSON lines sorted by `site_id` then anchor, so that two runs produce identical bytes: `site_state.jsonl` with one row per site and anchor (`site_id`, `country_code`, `anchor`, `presence`, the four onset and closing bound dates, geometry, `geometry_role`, `location_confidence`, `location_assertion_mode`, `uncertainty_radius_m`, `denomination_codes`, `religion`, `taxonomy_version`, `worship_use_status`, `status`, `name`, `culturally_sensitive`, `display_clearance`, `last_event_id`); `sites_current.jsonl`, the same row at *S* equal to the date of *T*; `transitions.jsonl` (site, attribute, from, to, bound dates, certainty); `site_registry.jsonl` (`site_id`, source keys with roles, `minted_by_event_id`, `minted_at`); and `rebuild-manifest.json` (data manifest, `dataset_role: accepted_export`, `pipeline_stage: accepted`, inputs with hashes, *T*, the anchor list, the code commit, every output hashed). GeoParquet for R users is a derived product written by a pinned Python tool from the JSONL and hashed in the release manifest; the D15 byte-identity gate applies to the JSONL, because Parquet writers vary their bytes across versions and the gate must be exact.

The acceptance gate (D15) is a test in CI: replay the fixture log twice, once from a fresh checkout and once after deleting `.pow/staging.sqlite`, and compare every output byte for byte with pinned hashes; replay at *T* one second before the Kendall acceptance and assert the site is absent. Beyond Kendall, the fixture log carries synthetic sites for the bounded cases: an opening known only by a lower bound, a closure bounded across an anchor, a same-year opening and closure, a correction of a state that a later observed change follows, a retracted superseding event, and a replacement bundle changing one attribute. A test that fails here blocks every later pull request.

## 7. `pow export` and the release

`pow export release --rebuild <dir> --country <cc> --release-id <id> --out <dir>` turns the current state table into the public artefacts of one release. A release is per country, and every artefact but one is versioned and immutable.

**One public projection.** Every public artefact, whatever its form, passes through one projection defined in `pow export` and tested once: the overlay, the downloads, the edition tables, the census and ledger rows, the manifests and the receipts. The projection drops a site's point geometry below the D13 grades (`exact`, `building`, `parcel_or_compound`), carrying the site into area products only; drops every culturally sensitive site without `display_clearance: cleared_for_public_display` (D18) from every public artefact, counts included; carries citations only when their `licence_status` is `accepted` and their text passes the personal-details screen the agent lane already uses, since an accepted licence says nothing about D24; and replaces Convex identifiers, evidence row identifiers, contributor identifiers and job identities with nothing. Receipts therefore have two forms: the full execution record (bundle identifiers, segment paths, retrieval times, the operator, the job run) stays in project storage, and the public receipt carries the release id, the manifest hashes, the artefact hashes, the pointer change and its time. The fixture asserts that a sensitive site and a street-grade site are absent from every public artefact of a release that contains them, and that no public artefact contains a Convex identifier.

**The reviewed overlay.** `reviewed-<cc>-<release>.geojson` carries one point feature per site in `sites_current.jsonl` that passes the projection, with presence `present` or `uncertain` at the current date. Properties: `site_id`, `name`, `religion`, `denomination` code and its label from the pinned taxonomy, `taxonomy_version`, `status`, `location_confidence`, `radius_m` where the assertion carried one, `onset_not_later_than`, `reviewed_at` (the `recorded_at` of the latest live event), `release_id`, `supersedes_osm` (the list of OSM keys the site's `point_source` roles name, as `node/358729928`), `osm_type` and `osm_id` of the first point source for the popup's link, and the permitted citations. Where a country has tens of thousands of reviewed sites the overlay becomes a PMTiles tier under the Worker; the first years are GeoJSON.

**The suppression list.** `suppress-<cc>-<release>.json`: `{ release_id, base_places_tileset, ids }`, the sorted union of every feature's `supersedes_osm`, plus `site:<site_id>` entries for reviewed sites that an earlier base build baked in and this release replaces or retracts (below). A reviewed record replaces the dot it corrects (amendment item 6), and the list is how the runtime and the tile build know which dots.

**Recomputing the grid-thinned groups, in one global composition.** From zoom 8 the base tiles carry `osm_type` and `osm_id`, so one filter clause hides a suppressed dot and the overlay draws the reviewed one. At zooms 0 to 7 a dot stands for a group and carries `n`, and hiding the representative would hide its neighbours' count, which item 6 forbids. The coarse tiers are global, so they are rebuilt as one *composition*: `composition-<id>.json` names, for every country, the release id whose overlay and suppression list it folds in, and the coarse archives are named by the composition id (`places-overview-v2-20260722-grid-c<id>`, `places-coarse-v2-20260722-grid-c<id>`). `build_tiles_grid.py` gains `--composition <file>`, removes every suppressed key from the thin input, adds every reviewed site at its accepted position with OSM-style attributes (religion, and the denomination's first `osm_aliases` entry from the taxonomy, so the group keys agree) and with its `site_id`, and runs its `thin`, `build` and `validate` stages for zooms 0 to 7 only; the validation's conservation check becomes input count minus suppressed plus reviewed, per country and in total. The zoom 8 to 18 rows are untouched, so the places tier is split into a coarse archive (zooms 6 and 7) and a detail archive (zooms 8 to 18), and the overview archive (zooms 0 to 5) is rebuilt whole; the overview tier is 10.6 MB, and the size of the zoom 6 and 7 tier and the rebuild time remain to be measured. Below zoom 8 the overlay layer is hidden, because the reviewed site is already a member of its group; from zoom 8 the overlay shows and the filter applies. Every country pointer names the composition it was published with, and a composition change (a new release, or a withdrawal) switches every pointer that names the old composition in the same publication run, under one concurrency lock, so no pointer ever serves a coarse archive that folds in a release another pointer has withdrawn. The archive names need the Worker's `VERSIONED` pattern to admit the composition suffix, a Worker change JB deploys, since tile configuration is outside pull-request closure.

**The release manifest and the pointer.** `release-<cc>-<release>.manifest.json` is a data manifest (`dataset_role: public_product`, `pipeline_stage: public`) naming the rebuild manifest hash, the log segment hashes, the taxonomy hash, the composition id, the base and coarse tileset names and their manifests, every artefact with its SHA-256, *T*, the generator commit, counts (sites, suppressed keys), `licence_basis` and `licence_status`. `current-<cc>.json` is the pointer, the one mutable object per country: `{ country, release_id, composition_id, base: { overview, places_coarse, places_detail }, overlay_url, suppression_url, manifest_url, manifest_sha256, published_at }`, served with `Cache-Control: max-age=300`. Every other object is written under a versioned name with the existing refusal (`If-None-Match: *`; the README's rule) and served immutable. The runtime reads the pointer at load, adds the overlay source and layer, adds the suppression clause to `currentFilterClauses()`, and shows the release id beside the attribution with a link to the manifest. Artefacts live in the project's R2 bucket under the Worker, in a new path family (`/releases/<cc>/…`, `/compositions/…`, `/current/<cc>.json`), because publication by Git commit would make every release a pull request, which is the hand path the amendment retires. Data leave Git; manifests and public receipts are also committed to the repository by the job, so the public record of what was published is in version control.

**The publication protocol and its commit point.** A publication run has one commit point, the conditional pointer switch, and durable state on both sides of it. Before anything public changes, the job writes `publication-<run>.pending.json` to project storage: the batches claimed, the segment written, the release and composition ids, the artefacts to upload with their hashes, the pointers to switch and the ETag each pointer is expected to have. It then uploads every immutable object (an upload that finds its key present with the same hash is a no-op; a different hash is a refusal). The switch writes each pointer with `If-Match` on the recorded ETag, in a fixed order, and records each success in the pending file; a precondition failure stops the run and leaves the remaining pointers unchanged, for an operator to read. After the last switch the job writes the public receipt, finalises the pending file as `publication-<run>.json`, and commits manifests and receipts to Git; each of those steps is retried until it succeeds and none of them changes what the public sees. Recovery on restart reads the pending files: a run that stopped before any switch is discarded (its uploaded objects are harmless and reused); a run that stopped mid-switch resumes with the pointers that remain; a run that stopped after the switches completes its receipt and commit. Acceptance precedes publication, so a run that fails after acceptance leaves accepted events in the log and no public change; the next run publishes them. A failure record names the step that failed.

**Receipts and withdrawal (item 3).** Each publication writes the public receipt described above and the full record in project storage. `pow export withdraw --country <cc> --release <id> --reason <text>` withdraws a release by publishing: it builds a new composition without that release's overlay and suppression list, points the country at its previous release and every affected country at the new composition under the same protocol, and writes `withdrawal-<cc>-<release>.json` with a public-safe reason. A pointer is never rolled back to an older ETag, so withdrawing a release that a later release already replaced changes the composition only. The withdrawn objects stay in the bucket, immutable, unless JB orders their deletion for a disclosure, and copies a browser has cached cannot be recalled. Withdrawal of the release leaves the log intact; if the fault is in an accepted event, a retraction event (section 3, change 7) follows under its own authority and the next release replays without it. A `releases-<cc>.jsonl` index lists every receipt and withdrawal.

**Automatic publication (items 2 and 3).** A workflow in CI runs the chain: retrieve the frozen bundle, claim it, materialise, `pow accept`, record acceptance, `pow rebuild-master`, `pow export release`, the composition rebuild, upload, pointer switch, receipt, commit of manifests and receipts. Retrieval cannot use `getExportBundle`, which requires an authenticated curator (section 2, fact 3). We add an internal action, `internal.exports.getExportBundleForProcessing({ exportBatchId, userId })`, callable with a deployment key and acting for a recorded curator who must be active, on the pattern the scheduled freeze chain already uses; the receipt records that curator as the acting user. This is an authorisation change and takes the reserved review AGENTS.md requires for security-sensitive changes. The trigger is the PI batch release once it exists; until then, JB names the frozen batch or batches in writing and the job runs on `workflow_dispatch` with those ids. The job needs tippecanoe and the extract (`slim.ndjson`) as a hashed input fetched from project storage, and secrets JB provisions; the build machine with a cron is the fallback runtime if CI proves too slow for the composition rebuild. Every release still has a named human: the person whose instruction, or whose batch release, triggered it, recorded in the full receipt.

**The ODbL gate and keyless features (item 7).** `pow export release --public` refuses unless the release configuration carries a `licence_basis` JB sets after advice and the manifest's `licence_status` is `accepted`; without `--public`, the job builds and verifies everything and uploads nothing, which is how the fixture and the dry runs proceed before the ruling. OSM-derived content carries the OSM attribution. On keyless features we read item 7 as follows: the 701,349 features stay in the base tiles; the provisional tier of the annual edition and every download exclude them; a reviewed release cites source records by key, so a keyless feature can be neither a `point_source` nor a suppression entry; a place that exists on the map as a keyless dot enters a reviewed release only through review as a place with its own evidence, and its duplicate dot stays until the next base-tile build. We ask JB in section 11 to confirm this reading.

**Contributing corrections to OSM (item 11).** Offering a reviewed correction to OpenStreetMap is a separate human act, case by case, outside the publication job; the job never writes to OSM. When a mapper, or a project member, later edits the OSM object, the next provisional edition shows the change and the site's `point_source` key may change; the base-tile validator reports suppression entries absent from the new base, and each opens an `osm_identity_link` task.

## 8. The annual edition

The edition is a rebuild at *T*<sub>Y</sub> plus three queries, packaged under the name the amendment chose. `pow rebuild-master --as-of 2026-12-01T00:00:00Z` with anchors from the earliest event bound to 2026-09-01 gives the site-state tables of edition 2026; `pow export edition --edition 2026.1` passes them through the public projection and writes them under `annual-register-2026.1-<cc>-…` with a data manifest and `scope.snapshot_anchor: 09-01`, kept immutable; a reprocessing under a changed rule is `2026.2`. The edition identifier format and the clock of the cut-off (we propose UTC) are rulings for JB.

The three reports are queries over rebuilds rather than stored tables, and each follows D25 as ruled.

- **The census of change** compares state(*S*<sub>Y</sub>, *T*<sub>Y</sub>) with state(*S*<sub>Y−1</sub>, *T*<sub>Y</sub>) per site and attribute, within one rebuild, and counts the transitions of `transitions.jsonl` whose bounds fall in (*S*<sub>Y−1</sub>, *S*<sub>Y</sub>], certain and possible apart, so a place that opened and closed between the anchors is counted although the endpoint states agree. Corrections and accessions contribute no transition.
- **The revision ledger** compares state(*S*, *T*<sub>Y</sub>) with state(*S*, *T*<sub>Y−1</sub>) for every anchor *S*, which needs the previous edition's tables (pinned) or a second rebuild at *T*<sub>Y−1</sub>; the job does both and asserts they agree, a determinism check for free. The inaugural baseline is the empty accepted state: before the first edition no site had any accepted state at any anchor, so in the first edition every site's rows are accession revisions, from no record to the reconstructed state, and the ledger is as full as the log. A newly recorded place with evidence of presence before an anchor enters that anchor's state, and its arrival is such a revision (D25, point 3). The job tests the identity D25 states, that the net change between consecutive editions at the latest anchors equals the census plus the ledger's entry for *S*<sub>Y−1</sub>, on the fixture and on every edition. The (draft id, hash) set difference of section 7 survives as the ledger's audit trail.
- **The onset-unknown class** lists sites whose presence bound straddles *S*<sub>Y−1</sub>, apart from the places that appeared.

A fourth output is a diagnostic, outside D25's three reports: the *reconciliation report* compares each reviewed site's state at *S*<sub>Y</sub> with the provisional record it supersedes in the pinned OSM edition (location distance, religion and denomination, presence), through the crosswalk. Kendall's 87 m and its `christian` to `christian.baptist` refinement appear there, labelled as a comparison of the reviewed record with its source, which is a different quantity from a revision of the project's own record.

Each report publishes review coverage by area beside its counts (D25, point 4): reviewed sites and provisional places per country and, where the public map already carries a census boundary layer for the country, per area, assigned inside the rebuild from a pinned boundary file. The provisional tier (item 8) is the 1 September OSM edition, pulled through ohsome in mid-October (the lag the audit scoping measured), strict `amenity=place_of_worship` nodes and ways, keyless features excluded, written as `annual-register-2026.1-<cc>-provisional.csv` with its source manifest and counted apart from reviewed sites in every table. Whether the 1 September 2026 pull has been run we could not verify; the edition depends on it.

**The annual base build and what it bakes (item 9).** Each year the base tiles are built from the 1 September edition with the current composition folded in: suppressed keys removed, reviewed sites inserted with their `site_id` as an attribute at every zoom from 7, and a `baked_composition` entry in the base manifest naming the composition and every release it contained. The build and upload under new versioned names are standing authority; switching the live map to the new base is JB's word, so the client's suppression list shrinks to the delta since the bake only once JB switches, and until then it carries every release since the previous base. A reviewed site baked into the base is replaced or withdrawn through the same mechanism as an OSM dot: the suppression list names it by `site:<site_id>`, the runtime filter reads both key forms, the composition rebuild removes it from the coarse groups, and the overlay carries the current record; a retracted site appears as a tombstone in the suppression list until the next bake omits it. Reviewed nominations with no OSM key are therefore handled by `site_id` throughout.

## 9. Kendall as the end-to-end fixture

The fixture is one bundle, four events, two rebuilds and one release, committed under `schemas/fixtures/publication-pipeline/kendall/` and run by CI; its assertions land with the pull request that delivers each stage (section 10).
