# Publication pipeline brief: from a batch release to a public release, with Kendall as the fixture (2026-10-19)

Status: DESIGN. Joseph B (JB) asked on 2026-10-19 for the design brief for `pow accept`, replay, `pow export` and the reviewed overlay, with the Kendall church of [EC-001](../edge-cases.md) as the fixture. The same day he extended the brief to agent intake of corrections, with Kendall as the first AI-filed correction and the research assistant (RA) sheet kept as the fallback. The brief implements ruling D25 of the private decisions register and its eleven-item amendment, whose public counterpart is section 7 of the [vocabulary reconciliation](c2-vocabulary-reconciliation-2026-08-14.md) as amended. It adopts nothing by itself: under AGENTS.md a design brief needs a routine review and a reserved-model review before it merges, and every pull request it proposes needs its own authority. Section 12 lists what we verified in code and what we infer.

## 1. The problem

The project can review a place of worship, accept the review and freeze the accepted evidence, and there the governed chain stops. Nothing turns a frozen export into an accepted event, a rebuilt master or a public map. The one bridge that exists, `scripts/build_occupancy_dated_places.py`, reads a Convex export directly, draws a dated point only where a reviewer confirmed a derived census-year location, and takes religion and denomination from the matched OpenStreetMap (OSM) feature, so a reviewed denomination stays off the map and the OSM dot a correction replaces stays on it. Kendall shows every gap at once: the OSM node lies 87 m from the building with religion recorded and denomination blank; the correction is a change in our knowledge, which the 2026-08-22 census design would have counted as a change in the world; and the public map lacks any means of replacing the dot.

D25 settles the model. Accepted claims enter a continuous log at each PI batch release; the master is a replay of that log; once a year the project pins an edition at a knowledge cut-off and publishes a census of change, a revision ledger and an onset-unknown class. Its amendment settles the operating terms: a public release follows each batch release and is published automatically with a receipt and a withdrawal path; a reviewed record replaces the OSM dot it corrects in the default view, with grid-thinned groups recomputed before publication; the event contract gains a correction scope and an accession intent; the ODbL question gates the first public reviewed release; each edition carries a provisional OSM tier; outputs are called an annual register. Here, we design the machinery those rulings depend on, in the order the data flow: the contract change, agent intake of corrections, `pow accept`, replay, `pow export` and the release, the annual edition, the Kendall fixture, the pull requests, and the rulings JB still owes.

Write state(*S*, *T*) for the state of the world on date *S* as reconstructed under D3 from events accepted by audit time *T*. Let *S*<sub>Y</sub> be 1 September of year Y and *T*<sub>Y</sub> the cut-off of edition Y, 1 December under amendment item 1. The brief uses these symbols throughout.

## 2. What exists

We read the code and contracts on branch `main` at `78bcaa0e`. Seven facts set the design.

1. **The `pow` verb set stops before acceptance.** `crates/pow-cli/src/main.rs` defines `validate`, `validate-agent`, `stage`, `propose`, `diff`, `object` (hash and verify) and `export verify`. The verbs `accept` and `rebuild-master` are absent, and `export` only verifies. `pow propose` translates staged RA rows into `worship_function_observed` events with deterministic identifiers (`deterministic_uuid` over a seed; verified), which is the pattern `pow accept` can reuse. The staging database holds `stage_batches`, `stage_records` and `validation_diagnostics` only.
2. **The canonical serialisation D15 names exists.** `crates/pow-cli/src/canonical.rs` implements `pow-canonical-json.v1` (RFC 8785) and the `pow-object.v1` hash, checked against `schemas/fixtures/pow-canonical-json.v1.json` and shared with `convex/lib/canonicalJson.ts`. The change-event schema's `payload_hash` is described as "hash of the normalised payload" without naming a canonical form; section 3 pins it.
3. **The frozen bundle is verifiable.** `pow export verify` checks a materialised `pow-export-bundle.v1` directory against its own manifest in six groups (manifest, files, record counts, membership, pins, acceptance). The bundle carries sixteen files, including `tasks.jsonl`, `review_decisions.jsonl`, `evidence_versions.jsonl`, `review_snapshots.jsonl`, `site_occupancies.jsonl` and `derived_year_locations.jsonl`. A withdrawn or superseded batch still serves its bytes, and `getExportBundle` reports `disposition.processing_allowed`; the materialised directory omits that disposition ([frozen exports](frozen-exports.md)).
4. **The contract binds `site_created` to `lifecycle`** and the correction types to `correction`; a `site_location_corrected` event carries `effective` and leaves its scope unstated (`schemas/change-event.schema.json`, `allOf`; the sample in `docs/examples/revisions/nz-sample-change-events.jsonl` has `effective_date: 2018-03-06`). `GeometryPayload` carries `previous_geometry_history_id` and `DenominationPayload` carries `previous_denomination_code`, which section 3 uses to name the state a correction rewrites. `EventTarget` offers `matched_current_site_id` as a temporary reference until accepted `site_id`s exist.
5. **The public map's dots come from two grid-thinned PMTiles tiers**, `places-overview-v2-20260722-grid` (zooms 0 to 5) and `places-v2-20260722-grid` (zooms 6 to 18), built by `tools/tiles-r2/build_tiles_grid.py` from a 22 July 2026 extract of 2,072,349 features, of which 701,349 carry no OSM key. At zooms 0 to 6 a dot carries `religion`, `denomination`, `country_code` and `n`, the number of places it stands for; `osm_id` and `osm_type` appear from zoom 7; zooms 8 to 18 are the July tiles unchanged (`ZOOM_SPEC`, `THIN_ATTRS`, `SLIM_ATTRS`; verified). The Worker serves versioned names matching one pattern, `places-overview-v2|places-v2|ra-dots-<cc>` followed by a date and one optional suffix (`tools/tiles-r2/worker/src/index.js`, `VERSIONED`), with a year-long immutable cache, and a versioned key, once written, is immutable.
6. **The runtime already filters both dot layers.** `applyFilters()` sets one filter expression on `LAYERS.places` and `LAYERS.overview` from `currentFilterClauses()` (`apps/regions/_shared/region-map.js`, lines 2844 to 2874), and `syncPlaceDotEra()` hides or fades the snapshot tiers by points mode (lines 5192 to 5235). The dated tier shows only in period mode, or in all mode when the census year lies more than `PLACE_SNAPSHOT_HORIZON` (15) years back.
7. **Agent intake accepts a research dossier, and nothing else.** `convex/internalAgentIntake.ts` `ingestBundle` validates a bundle with `validateAgentReviewBundle` (`convex/lib/agentIntake.ts`), writes a task in batch `internal-agent-research` with country `NZ`, geometry from `dossier.candidate_location`, `matched_osm_id` parsed from `place_ref`, and an evidence draft marked `agent_intake_only`, plus the advisory review, claim-grain judgments and an immutable receipt. The bundle schema (`scripts/agent_research/schemas/agent-review-bundle.v1.json`) lacks every field a correction needs: a location assertion, an original point, a denomination relation, a label basis and a change class; the task's `source_context` carries origin, bundle hash and submission key only, without an `issue_report`. The validator requires `place.country_code === "NZ"` and an allowlist version it knows (`nz-v1`); a parish's own domain is refused until an allowlist names it. The submit command accepts the `dev` and `local` selectors only. An `agent_intake_only` draft cannot be accepted for export (`convex/reviews.ts`, line 270) and is excluded from the external AI review queue (`convex/claudeReviews.ts`, line 155). All verified.

Two standing designs frame the work. The [content-addressed review contract](content-addressed-review.md) (approved 2026-09-11) lists what an accepted event must reference: proposal hash, accepting decision hash, PI batch-release hash, evidence-version hashes, payload hash, source-manifest references, schema and taxonomy versions; its fifth implementation step is `pow` verification, acceptance and replay. The [human review and release design](human-review-and-release.md) (2026-09-23) names the stages after a freeze, accepted master change and published output, as planned. PI batch release itself is approved and unbuilt; the live gate is per-item PI acceptance plus a curator freeze.

## 3. The change-event contract, version 2

Items 4 and 5 of the amendment change the contract, so version 2 of the schema is the first pull request. We propose a new file, `schemas/change-event.v2.schema.json`, beside the current file, which stays as version 1 (the repository already keeps `indicator.schema.json` beside `indicator.v2.schema.json`). Events declare `schema_version: "change-event.v2"`. `pow validate` accepts both versions by the declared version; `pow accept` writes version 2 only; the version 1 examples stay valid under version 1. Version 2 makes five changes.

**Change 1: `correction_scope`.** A top-level property, `correction_scope`, with the values `whole_validity` and `interval` and the schema default `whole_validity`, permitted only when `event_intent` is `correction` and refused on every other intent. An accepted event (`review.review_status: accepted`) must carry the value explicitly: `pow accept` writes the default into the event it appends, so the log is complete without any reader applying a default; a reader that meets an accepted correction without a scope is reading a defective log. A staged or proposed event may omit it. When the scope is `interval`, a required companion object `correction_interval` carries `valid_from` and `valid_to` (dates; `valid_to` null for open-ended), and `pow validate` refuses `interval` without it.

What a correction rewrites. A correction names the prior state it replaces: a geometry correction through `previous_geometry_history_id`, a denomination correction through `previous_denomination_code`, a status correction through `previous_status`. When the payload names no prior state, replay takes the state of that attribute in force at the correction's `effective` time. Under `whole_validity`, replay rewrites that state across its whole valid interval; under `interval`, replay splits the state at `valid_from` and `valid_to` and rewrites the middle piece. A correction is therefore confined to the state it names: a later observed change or lifecycle event opens a new state with its own interval, and a successor site is a different `site_id`, under the operational definition (version 0.1.6).

What `effective` means on a correction. D3 defines `effective` on a correction as the time the corrected fact became true. On a `whole_validity` correction replay reads `effective` for the D9 date-plausibility gate and for nothing else: the correction contributes no transition at any *S*, and the rewritten state keeps the interval the prior state had. For Kendall, the corrected fact (the church stands on its building) held before any evidence we hold, so the fixture records `not_later_than` equal to the earliest evidence date with `date_precision: bounded`. The sample event's `effective_date: 2018-03-06` becomes harmless under version 2 because the scope says what the date is for.

**Change 2: accession as a paired type.** D4 pairs event types with intents so that a contributor must choose (`site_location_corrected` against `site_relocated`). We follow that pattern: version 2 adds the event type `site_registered`, bound to a new intent value `accession`, with the existing `site_created` payload (`payload_type: site_created`, carrying `site_id`, `country_code`, `geometry`, `status`). `site_created` stays bound to `lifecycle` and means founding or opening. Replay reads `site_registered` as the site entering the record with presence bounded by its `effective`: with `not_later_than` set, the site is present at every anchor *S* at or after that date; at anchors before it, with `not_earlier_than` absent, the bound straddles *S* and the site contributes uncertainty, which is the onset-unknown class of D25. An accession contributes no transition to the census of change. The alternative, widening the intents `site_created` may carry, would leave the type name saying "created" on a place that existed for a century, and we recommend against it.

**Change 3: `payload_hash` pinned.** Version 2 defines `payload_hash` as the `pow-object.v1` hash (`sha256:` followed by the hex digest) of the `pow-canonical-json.v1` bytes of the `payload` object. Version 1 left the normalisation unnamed. The content-addressed contract requires the same canonical form for every review object, and D15 needs a hash whose inputs are defined.

**Change 4: provenance on the review reference.** `ReviewRef` gains six optional members an accepted event fills: `export_batch_id` and `export_manifest_hash` (the frozen bundle), `evidence_version_hash` (the version the decision pinned), `review_snapshot_hash`, `review_decision_hash`, and `batch_release_hash`, which stays null until PI batch release exists and then becomes required for events accepted after it ships. These are the references the content-addressed contract lists for an accepted event. They let an auditor walk from an event to the exact reviewed bytes without the staging database.

**Change 5: retraction of an accepted event.** The log is append-only, so a withdrawal after acceptance cannot edit `review.review_status`. Version 2 adds the event type `event_retracted`, intent `review_state`, payload `proposal_state` with `reason`, naming the retracted events in `supersedes_event_ids`. Replay treats an event as live at *T* when its `recorded_at` is at or before *T* and no retraction recorded at or before *T* names it. A state computed at an earlier *T* therefore contains the event still, which is what an audit of an earlier release needs. A correction that replaces a wrong event continues to use the D12 pattern, a new accepted event whose `supersedes_event_ids` names the old one, and replay applies the newest live event in a supersession chain.

Fixtures ship with the schema: the four Kendall events of section 9 as positive cases; and negative cases for `correction_scope` on an `observed_change` event, `interval` without `correction_interval`, an accepted correction without an explicit scope, `site_created` with intent `accession`, `site_registered` with intent `lifecycle`, an accepted event whose `payload_hash` disagrees with its payload, and a `site_registered` event with no `not_later_than` and no `effective_date`. The same pull request wires D9 gate 6, taxonomy membership, into `pow validate`, because `denomination_corrected` cannot be accepted without it and the taxonomy instance (`schemas/denomination-taxonomy.json`, version `2026-06-12.1`) carries `christian.baptist` (verified).

## 4. The intake end: an agent files a correction

JB extended the brief to the intake of corrections by an agent. Today the agent lane carries a research dossier about a place: claims with cited locators, an advisory review by a second provider, run manifests, a quarantine block for personal details, and a deterministic submission key. The dossier has a `candidate_location` with a basis and radius, but nothing says "the record's point is wrong and here is where the place is", nothing records which denomination relation or change class the claims support, and intake writes a plain task, with no original point for a reviewer to rule between. We verified each absence in section 2, fact 7. The design below adds a correction proposal to the dossier, lets intake create a revision task of the same shape the portal's revise flow creates, and keeps every safeguard the lane has. It is additive, and a dossier without the new block behaves as today.

### 4.1 What the portal records for a revision

The portal's revise flow (`enterReviseMode` and `createRevisionTask` in `apps/regions/nz/js/verification-map.js`) calls `tasks:createIssueTask` with the confirmed point as the task geometry, the record's point as `originalLatitude` and `originalLongitude`, the OSM identifier as `osmId`, and a `locationAssertion` of `contract_version: location_assertion_v1` with `mode` (`building_identified` or `approximate_area`), `basis`, `confidence`, an optional radius and wording, and `contributor_confirmed: true`. The server stores `source_context.issue_report.original_point` as `[longitude, latitude]` and `initial_location_assertion` (`convex/tasks.ts`, `createIssueTaskHandler`), checks the assertion against the task point (`assertAssertionMatchesTaskPoint`), and derives the master-data location grade from mode and radius (`locationConfidenceGrade`, `convex/lib/locationAssertions.ts`). The contributor then submits an observation whose draft carries `change_class` (`genuine_change`, `map_correction`, `uncertain`), `denomination_or_tradition_raw`, `denomination_label_basis` and `denomination_relation` (`label_only`, `record_correction`, `historical_change`, `shared_or_concurrent_use`, `uncertain`) from `convex/model.ts`. At review, `assertLocationOutcome` (`convex/lib/locationOutcome.ts`) requires a `location_outcome` of `accept_moved_point`, `keep_original_point` or `uncertain` whenever the task has an original point and the pin moved. All verified.

### 4.2 The correction proposal in the dossier

We add one optional object to the dossier, `correction_proposal`, in a new schema version `agent-dossier.v2` with the bundle at `agent-review-bundle.v2`; the three validators (Python `lib.py`, TypeScript `agentIntake.ts`, Rust `validate-agent`) pin the schema file, so a version bump is the honest form of an additive change. The object has five parts.

1. `target`: `place_ref` (the OSM object the correction concerns, which must equal `dossier.place.place_ref`), `original_latitude` and `original_longitude` (the record's point as the source edition shows it), and `source_edition` (the tileset or extract name, such as `places-v2-20260722-grid`).
2. `moved_point`: `latitude`, `longitude`, `basis` from the dossier's existing location bases (`osm_object`, `address_geocode`, `source_map_or_photo`, `imagery`, `reader_inference`), an optional `outline_ref` (an OSM way or relation whose outline the point lies within), and `supporting_claim_ids`. Absent when the proposal corrects attributes only.
3. `location_assertion_proposed`: `mode`, `basis` (from the portal's assertion bases, which for an agent will be `named_source_description` or `other`), `confidence`, `uncertainty_radius_m` and `source_wording`. The object omits `contributor_confirmed`, because confirmation is a human act. The human preparer confirms in step 4.4.
4. `denomination`: `denomination_raw`, `label_basis` (one of the portal's bases, which for a website is `current_self_description` or `named_documentary_source`), `denomination_relation`, `taxonomy_code_suggested` (checked against the pinned taxonomy, advisory only) and `supporting_claim_ids`.
5. `change_class`: `map_correction`, `genuine_change` or `uncertain`, with a `basis` string and `supporting_claim_ids`.

Every `supporting_claim_ids` entry must name a claim in the dossier, every cited claim must have a source on the allowlist, and the advisory reviewer's `claim_checks` must cover them; a proposal whose supporting claims the reviewer marked `not_supported` or `not_checked` is admitted with the reviewer's outcome attached, for the human to see, because the lane records disagreement rather than resolving it. The runner's personal-details screen already walks every string in the dossier, so the new strings are screened without new code; the hex-digest rule applies to them as to every other field.

### 4.3 What intake writes

When a bundle carries `correction_proposal`, `ingestBundle` creates the task as a revision of the existing record, with the same fields the portal's revise flow produces: `geometry` at the moved point (or the original point when there is none), `matched_osm_id` and `osm_object_type` from `place_ref`, `source_context.issue_report` with `original_point`, `source_edition`, `evidence_lane: "agent_correction"`, `source_title` and `source_url` of the first supporting claim, and `automated_checks` carrying the proposal summary. It leaves `initial_location_assertion` empty, because the contract requires a confirmed assertion and confirmation waits for the preparer; it stores the proposed assertion inside `source_context.issue_report.location_assertion_proposed` for the preparer. The evidence draft stays `agent_intake_only`, and gains `change_class`, `denomination_or_tradition_raw`, `denomination_label_basis` and `denomination_relation` copied from the proposal, so the preparer sees them in the draft they will clone. The receipt, the submission key, the bundle hash, the advisory review and the judgments are written as today. Two new judgment rows record the researcher's correction proposal and the reviewer's verdict on it (`judgment_kind: correction_proposal`), so later calibration can count how often a model's moved point survived human review.

Three safeguards need extension rather than preservation. First, the validator's country check and the allowlist are New Zealand-only; a United States run needs `country_code` generalised to the registry and an allowlist version `us-v1` whose domains include `openstreetmap.org` and, for the pilot, the church's own domain, since the lane refuses a parish domain until an allowlist names it. Second, the batch `internal-agent-research` and the fixed `target_years: [2013, 2018, 2023]` become per-country, read from `convex/lib/countryYears.ts` as the portal does. Third, the submit command's deployment selector admits `dev` and `local` only, so a run against the deployment the live portals use needs either an explicit allowance for a named deployment under JB's instruction or a run on a personal development deployment; section 11 puts the choice to JB. The ingest flag (`POW_INTERNAL_AGENT_INGEST_ENABLED`) stays per deployment and per authorised run.

### 4.4 Review, and the humans the Kendall run needs

The lane's authority boundary stands: an `agent_intake_only` draft cannot be accepted for export, and the ordinary path is a human-owned draft prepared from the dossier ([internal agent review](internal-agent-review.md), "Workflow and authority"). The preparer opens the task, reads the dossier and the advisory review, confirms the location assertion with `contributor_confirmed: true` (which the server then checks against the task point and stores on the task), creates the ordinary draft with `evidence:saveEvidenceDraft` carrying `change_class: map_correction`, the denomination fields and the receipt identifier in its source notes, and submits it. The task is then in the same queue as any RA revision, with the original point and the moved point for the reviewer to rule between. The reviewer records `accepted_for_export` with `location_outcome: accept_moved_point`, `identity_decision: same_place` and `denomination_relation` as filed, on the current snapshot. The PI accepts; `isSelfDecided` (`convex/lib/acceptance.ts`) records whether the PI ratified a decision the PI made, and the acceptance mutation refuses the PI's own submission. A curator or admin composes and freezes the batch.

The Kendall run needs, therefore, four human roles held by at least three people: an operator who runs the agent and submits the bundle (JB or an agent under his written instruction, since the flag and the deployment are his); a preparer who owns the ordinary draft and may be any RA, reviewer or curator; a reviewer who is neither the preparer nor the PI; and JB as PI, who may hold the operator role and no other, because the acceptance path refuses his own submission and `isSelfDecided` would otherwise record a self-ratified decision. The curator or admin who freezes may be the preparer or the reviewer. The RA sheet and the portal's revise flow remain the fallback: an RA who files the same correction by hand produces the same task shape, and nothing downstream distinguishes the two routes except `evidence_lane`.

### 4.5 The Kendall bundle

The bundle an agent would submit for Kendall, abbreviated and illustrative in its identifiers, carries a dossier for `osm:node/358729928` ("Wayside Church", country `US`, seed from the public map) with four claims and a correction proposal. The claims: a `name` claim, "Wayside Baptist Church", from the church's visit page (`https://waysidemiami.org/visit/`, `source_type: church_website`, retrieved 2026-10-19, `evidential_weight: primary_institutional`); an `address` claim, "7701 SW 98th Street, Miami", from the same page; a `denomination` claim, "Baptist", from the same page, with the quoted support being the congregation's own name; and a `service_pattern` claim, Sunday worship at 11:00, from the same page, which supports `status_assessment.current_status: active` as of 2026-10-19. The `osm_version_chain` records node 358729928's versions from the OSM API, which gives the onset bound section 9 uses. The correction proposal: `target` node 358729928 at its tile position, `source_edition: places-v2-20260722-grid`; `moved_point` at the centroid of relation 6474428 (`basis: osm_object`, `outline_ref: osm:relation/6474428`), with the claim that the relation is tagged `building=church` at 7701 SW 98th Street; `location_assertion_proposed` with `mode: building_identified`, `basis: named_source_description`, `confidence: high`, wording "the church's visit page gives 7701 SW 98th Street; OSM relation 6474428 is the church building at that address"; `denomination` with `denomination_raw: Baptist`, `label_basis: current_self_description`, `denomination_relation: record_correction`, `taxonomy_code_suggested: christian.baptist`; `change_class: map_correction`, basis "the congregation has not moved; the source point sits beside its building". Private correspondence stays outside every citation: the correspondent's confirmation identified the case and appears nowhere in the bundle, as EC-001 records.

## 5. `pow accept`

`pow accept <bundle_dir> --log <log_dir>` turns a verified frozen bundle into accepted events and appends them to the master log. It has no network access and reads no clock except once, to stamp `recorded_at` on the events it writes; the stamp is recorded in the segment manifest. Its steps are fixed.

1. **Verify.** Run the six check groups of `export verify` on the directory and refuse on any error. Read `disposition.json`, a small file the materialiser writes from the `disposition` block `getExportBundle` returns (`status`, `processing_allowed`, `retrieved_at`); refuse when the file is absent or `processing_allowed` is false. The withdrawal race the content-addressed contract names (a withdrawal between retrieval and acceptance) is closed by the publication job of section 7, which retrieves and accepts in one run and records the retrieval time in the receipt; a withdrawal after acceptance proceeds by retraction (section 3, change 5).
2. **Idempotence.** Compute the bundle's `manifest_hash` and look for a segment already named by it in the log manifest chain. If one exists, print its receipt and exit 0 without writing: the same bundle accepted twice is one acceptance, which is D9 gate 7 at bundle grain. A bundle whose `supersedes_export_batch_id` names a batch already accepted is accepted with supersession: every event it produces for a task the earlier batch also exported carries `supersedes_event_ids` naming the earlier events for that task (D12).
3. **Replay the current log** for the bundle's country (section 6) to build the identity index: source key to `site_id`. At first-year volumes the replay is cheap; it also keeps acceptance reading the log itself rather than a registry file that replay could contradict (D5).
4. **Translate.** For each included task, take the decision `pow export verify` identified as authoritative (the latest accepted acceptance's decision), the evidence version it pinned, the task row, the occupancies and derived year locations, and produce events by the rules of table 1. Every identifier is deterministic: `event_id` is `deterministic_uuid` over `(manifest_hash, task_id, evidence_version_hash, event kind, index)` and `client_event_id` is the same seed in clear, so a re-run on the same bundle writes the same bytes. `recorded_at` is the run's single stamp. `review` carries `review_status: accepted` and the six provenance members. `payload_hash` is computed under `pow-object.v1`. `taxonomy_version` is the pinned taxonomy's version. Translation refuses the whole bundle, naming the task, when any event fails `pow validate` under version 2, when a denomination code fails gate 6, when identity is ambiguous (two sites hold the same source key, or the decision's `identity_decision` is `uncertain`), or when a culturally sensitive payload lacks `sensitivity_basis`. Sensitive payloads enter the log (D18 allows it); the export gates them.
5. **Mint identity.** A task whose source key (`osm:<type>/<id>` from `matched_osm_id` and `osm_object_type`, or the `candidate_site_id` for a nomination) has no `site_id` in the index receives one, and the translation emits `site_registered` as the first event for that site. The `site_id` is `deterministic_uuid` over `("site", country_code, source key, manifest_hash)`, so the same bundle mints the same identifier on a re-run, and the identifier lives in the event payload, so replay reads identifiers and mints none. OSM identifiers become the crosswalk: the `site_registered` event's `source_refs` carry `source_record` entries with `source_dataset_id` (the extract, `osm-places-20260722`) and `source_record_id` (`node/358729928`), and the registry replay derives (section 6) records each key with a role, `point_source` for the object whose position the record had, `outline_source` for a building outline cited as geometry basis, `address_source` for an address point. The public form is `sameAs` on the site record, as `schemas/site.schema.json` already allows. Convex mints no `site_id`; a task's `candidate_site_id` is a Convex locator and stays out of every public product.
6. **Append.** Write one segment file, `events/<cc>/<recorded_at>-<manifest_hash:12>.jsonl`, one canonical JSON event per line in `event_id` order, and a segment manifest under `schemas/data-manifest.schema.json` (`dataset_role: accepted_export`, `pipeline_stage: accepted`, `input_manifests` naming the bundle manifest hash, `supersedes_manifest_id` naming the previous segment manifest, every durable file hashed). The log is the chain of segment manifests; nothing edits a segment once written. Print a receipt: bundle id, manifest hash, segment path and hash, event count, sites minted, run stamp.

| Decision and evidence | Events emitted | Intent | Effective |

## 6. Replay: `pow rebuild-master`

`pow rebuild-master --log <log_dir> --country <cc> --as-of <T> --anchors <first>..<last> --out <dir>` computes state(*S*, *T*) for every anchor *S* in the range and for *S* equal to the date of *T*, and writes the per-release site-state tables. It is a pure function of the log segments, the pinned taxonomy file and its arguments: it reads only the files whose hashes the rebuild manifest records, and it uses neither clock, network nor geocoder (D15). Where a product needs an area assignment (section 8), the boundary file is an input with a recorded hash, and point-in-polygon runs deterministically inside the rebuild.

The algorithm has four passes.

1. **Select.** Read every segment of the country's log whose manifest `recorded_at` is at or before *T*; keep events with `recorded_at` at or before *T*; drop events named by an `event_retracted` event recorded at or before *T*; in a supersession chain keep the newest live event.
2. **Timeline.** For every site, order the non-correction events by their latest admissible effective date (`effective_date`, else `not_later_than`, else `not_earlier_than`), then `recorded_at`, then `event_id`, and build per-attribute state intervals: presence (opened by `site_registered` or `site_created`, closed by `site_closed` or `worship_use_disappeared` with no later reopening), location (geometry history states), denomination, worship use, status, name. Each interval carries the bound that opened it, so a bound straddling an anchor can be reported.
3. **Corrections.** Apply correction events in `recorded_at` order to the state each names (section 3), rewriting under `whole_validity` or splitting under `interval`.
4. **Evaluate.** For each anchor *S*, for each site, read the interval containing *S* for each attribute; where the opening bound straddles *S*, the attribute is `uncertain` and the bound is recorded; where the site's presence bound straddles *S*, the site is in the onset-unknown class for *S*.

Outputs, all canonical JSON lines sorted by `site_id` then anchor, so that two runs produce identical bytes: `site_state.jsonl` with one row per site and anchor (`site_id`, `country_code`, `anchor`, `presence` as present, absent or uncertain, `onset_not_earlier_than`, `onset_not_later_than`, geometry, `geometry_role`, `location_confidence` derived from `uncertainty_radius_m`, `denomination_codes`, `religion`, `taxonomy_version`, `worship_use_status`, `status`, `name`, `culturally_sensitive`, `display_clearance`, `last_event_id`); `sites_current.jsonl`, the same row at *S* equal to the date of *T*; `site_registry.jsonl` (`site_id`, source keys with roles, `minted_by_event_id`, `minted_at`); and `rebuild-manifest.json` (data manifest, `dataset_role: accepted_export`, `pipeline_stage: accepted`, inputs with hashes, *T*, the anchor list, the code commit, every output hashed). GeoParquet for R users is a derived product written by a pinned Python tool from the JSONL and hashed in the release manifest; the D15 byte-identity gate applies to the JSONL, because Parquet writers vary their bytes across versions and the gate must be exact.

The acceptance gate (D15) is a test in CI: replay the fixture log twice, once from a fresh checkout and once after deleting `.pow/staging.sqlite`, and compare every output byte for byte with pinned hashes; replay at *T* one second before the Kendall acceptance and assert the site is absent. A test that fails here blocks every later pull request.

## 7. `pow export` and the release

`pow export release --rebuild <dir> --country <cc> --release-id <id> --out <dir>` turns the current state table into the public artefacts of one release. A release is per country, and every artefact but one is versioned and immutable.

**The reviewed overlay.** `reviewed-<cc>-<release>.geojson` carries one point feature per site in `sites_current.jsonl` that passes the public gates: `location_confidence` in `exact`, `building` or `parcel_or_compound` (D13; street, locality and area placements go to area products only), `display_clearance: cleared_for_public_display` where `culturally_sensitive` is true (D18), and presence `present` or `uncertain` at the current date. Properties: `site_id`, `name`, `religion`, `denomination` code and its label from the pinned taxonomy, `taxonomy_version`, `status`, `location_confidence`, `radius_m`, `onset_not_later_than`, `reviewed_at` (the `recorded_at` of the latest live event), `release_id`, `supersedes_osm` (the list of OSM keys the site's `point_source` roles name, as `node/358729928`), `osm_type` and `osm_id` of the first point source for the popup's link, and the citations whose `licence_status` is `accepted`. Convex identifiers appear nowhere. Where a country has tens of thousands of reviewed sites the overlay becomes a PMTiles tier under the Worker; the first years are GeoJSON.

**The suppression list.** `suppress-<cc>-<release>.json`: `{ release_id, base_places_tileset, ids }`, the sorted union of every feature's `supersedes_osm`. A reviewed record replaces the dot it corrects (amendment item 6), and the list is how the runtime and the tile build know which dots.

**Recomputing the grid-thinned groups.** From zoom 8 the base tiles carry `osm_type` and `osm_id`, so one filter clause hides a suppressed dot and the overlay draws the reviewed one. At zooms 0 to 7 a dot stands for a group and carries `n`, and hiding the representative would hide its neighbours' count, which Astra's condition forbids. The release job rebuilds, therefore, the two coarse tiers from the extract: `build_tiles_grid.py` gains `--suppress <list…>` and `--reviewed <overlay…>`, removes every suppressed key from the thin input, adds every reviewed site at its accepted position with OSM-style attributes (religion, and the denomination's first `osm_aliases` entry from the taxonomy, so the group keys agree), and runs its `thin`, `build` and `validate` stages for zooms 0 to 7 only; the validation's conservation check becomes input count minus suppressed plus reviewed. The zoom 8 to 18 rows are untouched, so the places tier is split into a coarse archive (zooms 6 and 7) and a detail archive (zooms 8 to 18), and the overview archive (zooms 0 to 5) is rebuilt whole; the coarse archives are small (the overview tier is 10.6 MB; the size of the zoom 6 and 7 tier remains to be measured). The coarse tiers are global, so one publication run rebuilds them once from the union of every country's current suppression list and overlay, and each country's pointer names the global coarse version. Below zoom 8 the overlay layer is hidden, because the reviewed site is already a member of its group; from zoom 8 the overlay shows and the filter applies. The archive names need the Worker's `VERSIONED` pattern to admit a second suffix (`places-overview-v2-20260722-grid-<release>`), a Worker change JB deploys, since tile configuration is outside pull-request closure.

**The release manifest and the pointer.** `release-<cc>-<release>.manifest.json` is a data manifest (`dataset_role: public_product`, `pipeline_stage: public`) naming the rebuild manifest hash, the log segment hashes, the taxonomy hash, the base and coarse tileset names and their manifests, every artefact with its SHA-256, *T*, the generator commit, counts (sites, suppressed keys), `licence_basis` and `licence_status`. `current-<cc>.json` is the pointer, the one mutable object: `{ country, release_id, base: { overview, places_coarse, places_detail }, overlay_url, suppression_url, manifest_url, manifest_sha256, published_at }`, served with `Cache-Control: max-age=300`. Every other object is written under a versioned name with the existing refusal (`If-None-Match: *`; the README's rule) and served immutable. The runtime reads the pointer at load, adds the overlay source and layer, adds the suppression clause to `currentFilterClauses()`, and shows the release id beside the attribution with a link to the manifest. Artefacts live in the project's R2 bucket under the Worker, in a new path family (`/releases/<cc>/…`, `/current/<cc>.json`), because publication by Git commit would make every release a pull request, which is the hand path the amendment retires. Data leave Git; manifests and receipts are also committed to the repository by the job, so the public record of what was published is in version control.

**Receipts and withdrawal (item 3).** Each publication writes `receipt-<cc>-<release>.json`: the bundles accepted (ids and manifest hashes), the retrieval time of each disposition check, the log segment written, the rebuild and release manifest hashes, every uploaded object with its hash, the pointer's previous and new release ids, the time of the switch, and the job identity. `pow export withdraw --country <cc> --release <id> --reason <text>` reverts the pointer to the previous release and writes `withdrawal-<cc>-<release>.json` with a public-safe reason; the withdrawn objects stay in the bucket, immutable, unless JB orders their deletion for a disclosure, and copies a browser has cached cannot be recalled. Withdrawal of the release leaves the log intact; if the fault is in an accepted event, a retraction event (section 3, change 5) follows under its own authority and the next release replays without it. A `releases-<cc>.jsonl` index lists every receipt and withdrawal.

**Automatic publication (items 2 and 3).** A workflow in CI runs the chain: fetch the frozen bundle (`getExportBundle` with a deploy key), materialise with `disposition.json`, `pow accept`, `pow rebuild-master`, `pow export release`, the coarse rebuild, upload, pointer switch, receipt, commit of manifests and receipts. The trigger is the PI batch release once it exists; until then, JB names the frozen batch or batches in writing and the job runs on `workflow_dispatch` with those ids. A run that fails at any step leaves the pointer where it was and writes a failure record; nothing partial is pointed at. The job needs tippecanoe and the extract (`slim.ndjson`) as a hashed input fetched from project storage, and secrets JB provisions; the build machine with a cron is the fallback runtime if CI proves too slow for the coarse rebuild. Every release still has a named human: the person whose instruction, or whose batch release, triggered it, recorded in the receipt.

**The ODbL gate and keyless features (item 7).** `pow export release --public` refuses unless the release configuration carries a `licence_basis` JB sets after advice and the manifest's `licence_status` is `accepted`; without `--public`, the job builds and verifies everything and uploads nothing, which is how the fixture and the dry runs proceed before the ruling. OSM-derived content carries the OSM attribution. On keyless features we read item 7 as follows: the 701,349 features stay in the base tiles; the provisional tier of the annual edition and every download exclude them; a reviewed release cites source records by key, so a keyless feature can be neither a `point_source` nor a suppression entry; a place that exists on the map as a keyless dot enters a reviewed release only through review as a place with its own evidence, and its duplicate dot stays until the next base-tile build. We ask JB in section 11 to confirm this reading.

## 8. The annual edition

The edition is a rebuild at *T*<sub>Y</sub> plus three queries, packaged under the name the amendment chose. `pow rebuild-master --as-of 2026-12-01T00:00:00Z` with anchors from the earliest event bound to 2026-09-01 gives the site-state tables of edition 2026; `pow export edition --edition 2026.1` writes them under `annual-register-2026.1-<cc>-…` with a data manifest and `scope.snapshot_anchor: 09-01`, kept immutable; a reprocessing under a changed rule is `2026.2`. The edition identifier format and the clock of the cut-off (we propose UTC) are rulings for JB.

The three reports are queries over rebuilds rather than stored tables.

- **The census of change** compares state(*S*<sub>Y</sub>, *T*<sub>Y</sub>) with state(*S*<sub>Y−1</sub>, *T*<sub>Y</sub>) per site and attribute, within one rebuild, and counts transitions from the live events whose latest admissible effective date lies in (*S*<sub>Y−1</sub>, *S*<sub>Y</sub>], so a place that opened and closed between the anchors is counted although the endpoint states agree. Corrections and accessions contribute no transition.
- **The revision ledger** compares state(*S*, *T*<sub>Y</sub>) with state(*S*, *T*<sub>Y−1</sub>) for every anchor *S*, which needs the previous edition's tables (pinned) or a second rebuild at *T*<sub>Y−1</sub>; the job does both and asserts they agree, a determinism check for free. The (draft id, hash) set difference of section 7 survives as the ledger's audit trail. For a site with no reviewed state at *T*<sub>Y−1</sub>, which is every site in the first edition, we propose that the ledger row compare the reviewed state with the provisional record the site supersedes, read from the pinned OSM edition through the crosswalk, and mark the row `reference: provisional`; otherwise the first edition's ledger is empty and Kendall's correction appears in no report, which misstates what the project did.
- **The onset-unknown class** lists sites whose presence bound straddles *S*<sub>Y−1</sub>, apart from the places that appeared.

Each report publishes review coverage by area beside its counts (D25, point 4): reviewed sites and provisional places per country and, where the public map already carries a census boundary layer for the country, per area, assigned inside the rebuild from a pinned boundary file. The provisional tier (item 8) is the 1 September OSM edition, pulled through ohsome in mid-October (the lag the audit scoping measured), strict `amenity=place_of_worship` nodes and ways, keyless features excluded, written as `annual-register-2026.1-<cc>-provisional.csv` with its source manifest and counted apart from reviewed sites in every table. Whether the 1 September 2026 pull has been run we could not verify; the edition depends on it. The base tiles of the following year are built from the same edition with the year's suppression lists and overlays folded in (item 9), so the client's suppression list carries one year's delta at most.
