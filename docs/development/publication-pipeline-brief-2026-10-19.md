# Publication pipeline brief: the change-event contract and agent-filed corrections, with Kendall as the fixture (2026-10-19)

Status: DESIGN, narrowed. The amended section 7 of the [vocabulary reconciliation](c2-vocabulary-reconciliation-2026-08-14.md) requires a path from a principal investigator's (PI) batch release to a public release: a continuous accepted log, replay, a reviewed overlay that replaces the OpenStreetMap (OSM) dot it corrects, automatic publication with receipts, and an annual edition. This brief designs in full the two pull requests that carry the Kendall church of [EC-001](../edge-cases.md), filed by an agent, as far as a frozen export: version 2 of the change-event contract (PR A) and agent intake of corrections, in which the agent authors the ordinary evidence and a named reviewer judges it on the model of a pull request (PR B). The seven later pull requests, from `pow accept` to the annual edition, appear in outline only (section 5), each with the design questions its own brief must answer before it is built. Rulings cited by number (D3, D4, D9, D12, D13, D18, D24, D25) are the project's standing decisions; the brief states their content where it relies on them. The brief adopts nothing by itself, and every pull request it proposes needs its own authority. Section 8 lists what we verified in code and what we infer.

## 1. The problem

The project can review a place of worship, accept the review and freeze the accepted evidence, and there the governed chain stops. Nothing turns a frozen export into an accepted event, a rebuilt master or a public map. The one bridge that exists, `scripts/build_occupancy_dated_places.py`, reads a Convex export directly, draws a dated point only where a reviewer confirmed a derived census-year location, and takes religion and denomination from the matched OSM feature, so a reviewed denomination stays off the map and the OSM dot a correction replaces stays on it. Kendall shows every gap at once: the OSM node lies 87 m from the building with religion recorded and denomination blank; the correction is a change in our knowledge, which the 2026-08-22 census design would have counted as a change in the world; and the public map lacks any means of replacing the dot.

D25 settles the model. Accepted claims enter a continuous log at each PI batch release; the master is a replay of that log; once a year the project pins an edition at a knowledge cut-off and publishes a census of change, a revision ledger and an onset-unknown class. Its amendment settles the operating terms: a public release follows each batch release and is published automatically with a receipt and a withdrawal path; a reviewed record replaces the OSM dot it corrects in the default view, with grid-thinned groups recomputed before publication; the event contract gains a correction scope and an accession intent; the ODbL question gates the first public reviewed release; each edition carries a provisional OSM tier; outputs are called an annual register. Two of those terms, the correction scope and the accession intent, change the contract every later stage reads, and the agent lane is where the Kendall correction will be filed. Here, we design those two pieces, and we outline the rest.

One constraint governs every stage, in the wording JB adopted on 2026-11-16: "Every published value traces to evidence, to a recorded review by a named reviewer, and to a named human who authorised its release, case by case or under a dated policy for its class." The named reviewer may be a person or an agent (section 4.5). The human who authorises release is always a person, and in the stages this brief designs that person authorises each case, or each batch once batch release exists, after reading it. Release under a dated policy for a class is a later step that the brief names and leaves undesigned.

Write state(*S*, *T*) for the state of the world on date *S* as reconstructed under D3 from events accepted by audit time *T*: an event replays into *S* when its effective time is at or before *S* and its `recorded_at` is at or before *T*, a bounded effective time orders by its latest admissible date, and a bound that straddles *S* contributes uncertainty. Let *S*<sub>Y</sub> be 1 September of year Y.

## 2. What exists

We read the code and contracts on branch `main` at `78bcaa0e`. Seven facts set the design.

1. **The `pow` verb set stops before acceptance.** `crates/pow-cli/src/main.rs` defines `validate`, `validate-agent`, `stage`, `propose`, `diff`, `object` (hash and verify) and `export verify`. Of the seven D9 gates, `pow validate` implements schema (gate 1), source presence (gate 5) and the idempotency key (gate 7), plus coordinate ranges and date ordering; country containment and relocation distance (gate 2), future-date and source-coverage checks (gate 3), the 50 m duplicate-risk flag (gate 4) and taxonomy membership (gate 6) remain unimplemented. `pow propose` mints deterministic identifiers (`deterministic_uuid` over a seed).
2. **The canonical serialisation D15 names exists.** `crates/pow-cli/src/canonical.rs` implements `pow-canonical-json.v1` (RFC 8785) and the `pow-object.v1` hash, checked against `schemas/fixtures/pow-canonical-json.v1.json` and shared with `convex/lib/canonicalJson.ts`. The change-event schema's `payload_hash` is described as "hash of the normalised payload" without naming a canonical form.
3. **The contract binds `site_created` to `lifecycle`** and the correction types to `correction`; a `site_location_corrected` event carries `effective` and leaves its scope unstated (`schemas/change-event.schema.json`, `allOf`; the sample in `docs/examples/revisions/nz-sample-change-events.jsonl` has `effective_date: 2018-03-06`). `GeometryPayload` carries `previous_geometry_history_id`, an immutable reference; `DenominationPayload` carries `previous_denomination_code` and `StatusPayload` `previous_status`, which are values and can recur across several intervals. `GeometryPayload` carries `uncertainty_radius_m` and no location grade. `TargetYearAffect.target_year` is a date (`2018-09-01`); the Convex validator `targetYearAffect.target_year` is a number (`2018`). `supersedes_event_ids` is a free list with no rule about what a superseding event may name.
4. **The location grade is derived from the assertion.** `locationConfidenceGrade` (`convex/lib/locationAssertions.ts`) returns `building` for mode `building_identified`, which carries no radius, and `parcel_or_compound`, `street`, `locality` or `area` from the radius of an `approximate_area` assertion. It never returns `exact`; `geometry-history.schema.json` lists `exact` and `unknown` as further grades.
5. **Agent intake accepts a research dossier, and nothing else.** `convex/internalAgentIntake.ts` `ingestBundle` validates a bundle with `validateAgentReviewBundle` (`convex/lib/agentIntake.ts`), writes a task in batch `internal-agent-research` with country `NZ`, geometry from `dossier.candidate_location`, `matched_osm_id` parsed from `place_ref`, and an evidence draft marked `agent_intake_only`, plus the advisory review, claim-grain judgments and an immutable receipt. The bundle schema (`scripts/agent_research/schemas/agent-review-bundle.v1.json`) lacks every field a correction needs: a location assertion, an original point, a denomination relation, a label basis and a change class; the task's `source_context` carries origin, bundle hash and submission key only, without an `issue_report`. The validator requires `place.country_code === "NZ"` and an allowlist version it knows (`nz-v1`); a parish's own domain is refused until an allowlist names it. The submit command accepts the `dev` and `local` selectors only. An `agent_intake_only` draft cannot be accepted for export (`convex/reviews.ts`, line 270) and is excluded from the external AI review queue (`convex/claudeReviews.ts`, line 155). The personal-details screen (`hasPersonalDetails`, mirrored in `lib.py` and the Rust validator) detects telephone numbers in New Zealand formats, e-mail addresses and honorific-led names; a bare name such as "John Smith" passes it. All verified.
6. **A task's location assertion is written at creation only.** `initial_location_assertion` is set by `createIssueTask`, `createManualCandidateTask`, rapid entry and batch import; `evidence:saveEvidenceDraft` takes a task id, a draft and client context, checks `assertOwnsOrCanReview` and `evidenceIntakeRefusal` (which refuses decided statuses until a reopen), and no mutation updates the assertion on an existing task. Review decisions carry `identity_decision` as an enum (`same_site`, `conversion`, `successor_at_new_site`, `end_and_new_foundation`, `new_candidate`, `duplicate`, `split`, `merge`, `relocation`, `uncertain`), and at `78bcaa0e` they had no field for the linked place, which the PI guide asked the reviewer to name in the decision note. Main has since added `linked_site_ids` to the decision and its input and refuses `relocation` on new decisions (verified at `98b74334`; section 4.2). Idempotent submissions use `client_submission_receipts`, keyed by member and submission id with a request digest. PI acceptance refuses the PI's own submission and permits the PI to ratify a decision the PI recorded, recording `self_decided: true` (`convex/lib/acceptance.ts`).
7. **The portal's revise flow** (`enterReviseMode` and `createRevisionTask` in `apps/regions/nz/js/verification-map.js`) calls `tasks:createIssueTask` with the confirmed point as the task geometry, the record's point as `originalLatitude` and `originalLongitude`, the OSM identifier, and a `locationAssertion` with `contributor_confirmed: true`; the server stores `source_context.issue_report.original_point` and `initial_location_assertion`, checks the assertion against the task point, and at review `assertLocationOutcome` requires `location_outcome` whenever the pin moved. Drafts carry `change_class`, `denomination_or_tradition_raw`, `denomination_label_basis` and `denomination_relation` (`convex/model.ts`).

The [content-addressed review contract](content-addressed-review.md) (approved 2026-09-11) lists what an accepted event must reference, requires acceptance and withdrawal to share a processing guard, and leaves its third to fifth steps (proposal pinning, PI batch release, `pow` acceptance and replay) unbuilt. The [human review and release design](human-review-and-release.md) names the stages after a freeze as planned. The live gate is per-item PI acceptance plus a curator freeze.

## 3. PR A: the change-event contract, version 2

Items 4 and 5 of the amendment change the contract, so version 2 of the schema comes first. We propose a new file, `schemas/change-event.v2.schema.json`, beside the current file, which stays as version 1 (the repository already keeps `indicator.schema.json` beside `indicator.v2.schema.json`). Events declare `schema_version: "change-event.v2"`. `pow validate` accepts both versions by the declared version; the version 1 examples stay valid under version 1; every later stage writes version 2 only. Version 2 makes eight changes.

**Change 1: `correction_scope`.** A top-level property, `correction_scope`, with the values `whole_validity` and `interval` and the schema default `whole_validity`, permitted only when `event_intent` is `correction` and refused on every other intent. An accepted event (`review.review_status: accepted`) must carry the value explicitly, so the log is complete without any reader applying a default; a staged or proposed event may omit it. Under `whole_validity` the correction rewrites the targeted state across its whole valid interval; under `interval` it rewrites the part of that state within the bounds change 2 names.

**Change 2: the state a correction rewrites is bound to an attribute, an opening event and, where needed, an interval.** A previous value (`previous_denomination_code`, `previous_status`) identifies a state ambiguously, because the same value can hold in several intervals of one site's history, and a date alone chooses nothing after a within-site move. Version 2 therefore adds `corrects`, required on every accepted correction and refused on every other intent:

```json
"corrects": {
  "attribute": "location",
  "opened_by_event_id": "<event_id of the live event that opened the state>",
  "interval": { "valid_from": "1987-01-01", "valid_to": null }
}
```

`attribute` is one of `location`, `denomination`, `status`, `name`, `worship_use`, `attributes`, `identity`, and must agree with the event type (a `site_location_corrected` event corrects `location`). `opened_by_event_id` names the live event whose payload opened the state being corrected: for the first location state, the `site_registered` or `site_created` event; after a within-site move, the `site_location_changed` event; for a denomination state, the `denomination_changed` or registration event that set it. `interval` is required when `correction_scope` is `interval` and refused otherwise; its `valid_to` is null for open-ended. The previous-value members and `previous_geometry_history_id` stay as human-readable context and must agree with the referenced state, or validation refuses. The *conflict key* of a correction is (`site_id`, `attribute`, affected interval), where the affected interval is the referenced state's whole interval under `whole_validity` and the named interval otherwise. Two live corrections whose conflict keys share a site and attribute and whose affected intervals intersect are a conflict, and the resolution is a reviewed adjudication (D12), never the newer winning by date. A new correction that would create a conflict is refused with `adjudication_required`. A conflict can arise in the log in one way only, when an adjudication is retracted (change 7): the retraction validates, and the conflict key takes the state `adjudication_required`, which blocks that site and attribute. While the state stands, replay selects neither correction by date and export of that site refuses, until a reviewed adjudication lands. An adjudication is a new correction of the same site and attribute that names the conflicting corrections in `supersedes_event_ids`, whatever their intervals, and states its own scope and interval. Supersession is whole-event: a superseded correction contributes nothing at any *S*, so the portions of its interval the adjudication leaves uncovered revert to the underlying state, and a reviewer who wants part of a superseded correction kept re-emits that part as a further correction. Corrections of 1980 to 2000 and 1990 to 2010, say, are resolved by one adjudication covering 1980 to 2010, or by one covering 1990 to 2010 beside a re-emitted 1980 to 1990, and the fixture carries both shapes. A correction is thereby confined to the state it names: a later observed change or lifecycle event opens a new state with its own opening event, and a successor site is a different `site_id` under the operational definition (version 0.1.6).

What `effective` means on a correction. D3 defines `effective` on a correction as the time the corrected fact became true. On a `whole_validity` correction, replay reads `effective` for the D9 date-plausibility gate and for nothing else: the correction contributes no transition at any *S*, and the rewritten state keeps the interval the referenced state had. For Kendall, the corrected fact (the church stands on its building) held before any evidence we hold, so the fixture records `not_later_than` equal to the earliest evidence date with `date_precision: bounded`. The sample event's `effective_date: 2018-03-06` becomes harmless under version 2 because the scope and the target say what the date is for.

**Change 3: accession and within-site movement as paired types.** D4 pairs event types with intents so that a contributor must choose (`site_location_corrected` against `site_relocated`). We follow that pattern twice. Version 2 adds `site_registered`, bound to a new intent value `accession`, with the existing `site_created` payload (`payload_type: site_created`, carrying `site_id`, `country_code`, `geometry`, `status`). `site_created` stays bound to `lifecycle` and means founding or opening. Replay reads `site_registered` as the site entering the record with presence bounded by its `effective`: with `not_later_than` set, the site is present at every anchor *S* at or after that date; at anchors before it, with `not_earlier_than` absent, the bound straddles *S* and the site contributes uncertainty, which is the onset-unknown class of D25. An accession contributes no transition to the census of change. A `site_registered` or `site_created` event opens a state for every correctable attribute of the site: `location` and `status` from its payload, and `denomination`, `name`, `worship_use` and `attributes` with the value `unknown` where the payload omits them, so that a later correction of any attribute has an opening event to name and `previous_denomination_code: null` agrees with an `unknown` state. Version 2 also adds `site_location_changed`, intent `observed_change`, payload `geometry_update`: a building moved or rebuilt elsewhere within its site, which the operational definition keeps under one identifier and which is a change in the world, so the census counts it. The alternative to a paired accession type, widening the intents `site_created` may carry, would leave the type name saying "created" on a place that existed for a century, and we recommend against it.

**Change 4: the location grade travels with the geometry, and `exact` has no assertion source.** `GeometryPayload` and `SiteCreatedPayload` gain `location_confidence` and `location_assertion_mode`. The grade is the value `locationConfidenceGrade` derives from a confirmed assertion (`building`, `parcel_or_compound`, `street`, `locality`, `area`), or `unknown` where no reviewed assertion exists, which is the case for a `site_registered` event whose geometry is the source's own coordinate: a precisely recorded OSM point says how the source drew the place, and nothing about how confidently the project locates it. `exact` is reserved for a reviewed evidence rule that no current contract defines, so version 2 lists it and refuses it on every event until such a rule exists. `uncertainty_radius_m` is carried only when the assertion carried one. Replay copies the grade into the state table and D13's publication gate reads it; nothing downstream infers a grade from a distance.

**Change 5: `payload_hash` pinned.** Version 2 defines `payload_hash` as the `pow-object.v1` hash (`sha256:` followed by the hex digest) of the `pow-canonical-json.v1` bytes of the `payload` object. Version 1 left the normalisation unnamed. The content-addressed contract requires the same canonical form for every review object, and D15 needs a hash whose inputs are defined.

**Change 6: provenance on the review reference.** `ReviewRef` gains the members the content-addressed contract lists for an accepted event: `export_batch_id`, `export_manifest_hash`, `evidence_version_hash`, `review_snapshot_hash`, `review_decision_hash`, `proposal_hash` and `batch_release_hash`. The first five are required on an accepted event. The schema makes `proposal_hash` and `batch_release_hash` nullable, because the contract's third and fourth steps are unbuilt; whether any event may be accepted while they are null is a decision for the acceptance brief (section 5, PR C) and for JB (section 7, ruling 12). Version 2 only makes the members exist.

**Change 7: supersession and retraction at contract level.** `supersedes_event_ids` gains rules. A superseding event names explicit events, never "every event of a task", and each named event must share the superseder's `target` and attribute: a correction may supersede only a correction with the same conflict key, an observed change only an observed change of the same attribute, and so on; a `site_registered` event may be superseded only by `duplicate_merged`. A correction that adjudicates a conflict (change 2) names the corrections it resolves, whatever their intervals, and a correction that reanchors a dependant (below) names the correction it replaces; those are the two cases in which a superseder's conflict key may differ from the superseded event's. The dependants rule applies to supersession as well as to retraction. A superseding event is refused when, in the live set after it, a live event would reference a suppressed or retracted event through `corrects.opened_by_event_id` or a relocation link. Two cases arise: an observed change superseding a `site_location_changed` that a live correction names as its opening event, and a `duplicate_merged` superseding a `site_registered` whose site carries live corrections. Each dependant must be replaced in the same acceptance by a reviewed correction that names a live opening event, such as the superseding event itself or the surviving site's registration, and supersedes the dependant. Validation reads the live set after every event of one acceptance, so the new opening event and the reanchored corrections land together, and PR C's brief defines the acceptance as a unit. A new event type `event_retracted`, intent `review_state`, payload `proposal_state` with `reason`, names the retracted events in `supersedes_event_ids`. Dependants are refused or adjudicated, never orphaned: validation of a retraction against the log refuses it when any live event references a retracted event through `corrects.opened_by_event_id`, `supersedes_event_ids` or a relocation link, unless the retraction names those dependants too (an explicit cascade, listed in the payload's `reason`) or a reviewed replacement has superseded them first. Liveness at audit time *T* is defined over the events recorded at or before *T*, and suppression runs along the lineage rather than being recomputed from the newest member. An event is *retracted at T* when an `event_retracted` recorded at or before *T* names it. An event is *suppressed at T* when some event recorded at or before *T* that is not retracted at *T* names it in `supersedes_event_ids`; whether that superseder is itself superseded makes no difference, because replacement of a replacement leaves the first replacement's judgement standing against its own predecessor. An event is *live at T* when it is recorded at or before *T*, is not retracted at *T* and is not suppressed at *T*. Only an explicit retraction restores anything: when a superseder is retracted, the suppression edges it carried lapse, and each event it named becomes live again at every later *T* unless another unretracted superseder still names it. The dependants rule therefore reads the live set a retraction would leave, revivals included (JB, 2026-11-16): a revival that would leave a live event referencing a retracted event is refused unless the cascade names it. Suppose C1 and C2 correct the location state opened by O, and C2 supersedes C1. A retraction naming O and C2 names the only live dependant of O, yet retracting C2 would revive C1, which references O, so the retraction is refused until it names C1 as well. Naming a revived event can revive that event's own predecessors, so validation recomputes the live set after the whole cascade and refuses the retraction while any live event in that set references a retracted event. Four consequences follow. In a chain where E2 supersedes E1 and E3 supersedes E2, at a *T* after E3 only E3 is live, at a *T* between E2 and E3 only E2 is live, and at a *T* before E2 only E1 is live. Retracting E3 revives E2 and leaves E1 suppressed by E2; retracting E2 while E3 is live is refused by the dependants rule above unless E3 is retracted with it, since E3 names E2. Retracting an adjudication that named several corrections revives every one of them that no other unretracted superseder names, and the conflict those corrections had returns with them. The retraction validates, the conflict key takes the state `adjudication_required` (change 2), and export of that site refuses until a reviewed adjudication lands; neither revived correction wins by date, and the retraction's `reason` must say how the conflict is to be resolved next. A suppressed event contributes nothing to any state at *T*, so the conflict check of change 2 ignores it and an ordinary third replacement validates. A state computed at an earlier *T* still contains every event live at that *T*, which is what an audit of an earlier release needs.

**Change 8: a retrospective assertion within D3.** A reviewer who reads an October observation and concludes that worship was in use at the 1 September anchor is asserting when a fact held, which is what `effective` records. Version 2 therefore requires that a `worship_function_observed` (or `site_attributes_observed`) event whose `target_year_affects` names an anchor *A* carry an `effective` bound that covers *A*: `not_later_than` at or before *A* (the fact held by *A*), `date_precision: bounded`, `basis: reviewer_inference` or `source_statement`, and a new payload member `observed_on`, the date of the observation itself, so the observation date is kept apart from the asserted validity. The event then replays into *A* under D3's own rule and needs no special reading. An observation with no retrospective assertion carries its observation date as `effective_date` and says nothing about earlier anchors. `TargetYearAffect.target_year` stays a date in the event contract; the Convex decision's numeric `target_year` converts to the country's anchor date (`2026` to `2026-09-01`) at translation, and the conversion rule is part of the translation contract PR C will define. A retrospective assertion that contradicts a live lifecycle event (presence asserted at *A*, a closure certain before *A*) is a temporal conflict and is refused for adjudication, like a correction conflict.

Fixtures ship with the schema, under `schemas/fixtures/change-event.v2/`. The positive cases are the Kendall events of section 4.7, and a denomination value recurring in two intervals with a correction whose `opened_by_event_id` names the second interval's opening event, which validates and rewrites the second interval alone. The negative cases are `correction_scope` on an `observed_change` event, `interval` without `corrects.interval`, an accepted correction without an explicit scope or without `corrects`, a `corrects.attribute` disagreeing with the event type, a `corrects` whose previous-value member disagrees with the referenced state, a new correction whose affected interval intersects that of a live correction, a correction after a `site_location_changed` that names the registration instead of the move, `site_created` with intent `accession`, `site_registered` with intent `lifecycle`, a superseder naming an event of another attribute, a retraction of a registration with a live dependant and no cascade, a retrospective assertion whose `effective` fails to cover its anchor, a retrospective assertion contradicting a certain closure, a geometry payload with a radius and the grade `building`, any event carrying `exact`, an accepted event whose `payload_hash` disagrees with its payload, and a `site_registered` event with no `not_later_than` and no `effective_date`. The supersession cases of change 7 each state their outcome: an ordinary supersession (the superseded correction contributes nothing and the conflict check passes); a three-event chain evaluated at audit times before E2, between E2 and E3, and after E3, with exactly one live correction at each; retraction of the chain's tip, reviving E2 and not E1; a refused retraction of E2 while E3 is live, and the same retraction accepted as a cascade naming E3; a cascade naming O and C2, where C2 supersedes C1 and both correct the state O opened, refused because it would revive C1 with a reference to O, and the same cascade accepted when it also names C1; an observed change superseding a `site_location_changed` that a live correction names as its opening event, refused, and the same supersession accepted when one acceptance also carries a correction that names the superseding event as its opening event and supersedes the old correction; a `duplicate_merged` superseding a `site_registered` whose site carries a live correction, refused unless the correction is reanchored to the surviving site in the same acceptance; an adjudication of two corrections with unequal overlapping intervals in both shapes of change 2; retraction of that adjudication, a positive case that validates, revives both corrections and puts their conflict key into `adjudication_required`; and, as its negative counterpart, an export of that site while the conflict stands, which PR E's export gate refuses. The same pull request wires D9 gates 2, 3 and 6 into `pow validate`: country containment and relocation distance, future effective dates on completed events and effective dates outside the source's coverage, and taxonomy membership against the pinned instance (`schemas/denomination-taxonomy.json`, version `2026-06-12.1`, which carries `christian.baptist`; verified). Gate 4 belongs to acceptance. Enforcing gate 6 would reject the existing version 1 examples, which still carry bare codes (`anglican`, `methodist`) and the placeholder `denomination-taxonomy.draft`, and the Rust fixtures in `main.rs` that do the same; PR A migrates them to dotted codes under `2026-06-12.1`, keeping their version 1 event shapes, which closes the debt D2 named.

## 4. PR B: an agent files a correction

Today the agent lane carries a research dossier about a place: claims with cited locators, an advisory review by a second provider, run manifests, a quarantine block for personal details, and a deterministic submission key. The dossier has a `candidate_location` with a basis and radius, but nothing says "the record's point is wrong and here is where the place is", nothing records which denomination relation or change class the claims support, and intake writes a plain task, with no original point for a reviewer to rule between. The design below adds a correction proposal to the dossier and lets intake create a revision task of the same shape the portal's revise flow creates. It then converts the dossier into ordinary evidence by a deterministic, validated rule, so that the agent authors the draft; withholds free text from the public unless it is chosen for publication; and reviews the result on the model of a pull request, with a named reviewer from a provider other than the author's, findings with recorded dispositions, required checks, and release by a person. The lane's other safeguards stay. A dossier without the new block behaves as today.

### 4.1 The correction proposal in the dossier

We add one optional object to the dossier, `correction_proposal`, in a new schema version `agent-dossier.v2` with the bundle at `agent-review-bundle.v2`; the three validators (Python `lib.py`, TypeScript `agentIntake.ts`, Rust `validate-agent`) pin the schema file, so a version bump is the honest form of an additive change. The object has six parts.

1. `target`: `place_ref` (the OSM object the correction concerns, which must equal `dossier.place.place_ref`), `original_latitude` and `original_longitude` (the record's point as the source edition shows it), and `source_edition` (the tileset or extract name, such as `places-v2-20260722-grid`).
2. `moved_point`: `latitude`, `longitude`, `basis` from the dossier's existing location bases (`osm_object`, `address_geocode`, `source_map_or_photo`, `imagery`, `reader_inference`), an optional `outline_ref` (an OSM way or relation whose outline the point lies within), and `supporting_claim_ids`. Absent when the proposal corrects attributes only.
3. `location_assertion_proposed`: the members of `locationAssertionInput` (`convex/model.ts`) except the confirmation, that is `contract_version`, `mode`, `basis` (from the portal's assertion bases, which for an agent will be `named_source_description` or `other`), `latitude` and `longitude` (equal to `moved_point`, or to the original point when there is none), `confidence`, `uncertainty_radius_m` and `source_wording`. The object omits `contributor_confirmed`, because the reviewer's snapshot-linked decision confirms the assertion (section 4.4).
4. `denomination`: `denomination_raw`, `label_basis` (one of the portal's bases, which for a website is `current_self_description` or `named_documentary_source`), `denomination_relation`, `taxonomy_code_suggested` (checked against the pinned taxonomy, advisory only) and `supporting_claim_ids`.
5. `change_class`: `map_correction`, `genuine_change` or `uncertain`, with a `basis` string and `supporting_claim_ids`.
6. `identity_proposed`: one of the existing `identity_decision` literals (`same_site` for Kendall), with an optional `related_ref` (`osm:<type>/<id>` or a task id) where the literal needs a linked place, and `supporting_claim_ids`. The reviewer's decision, never the proposal, settles identity.

A revised bundle, which carries the author's fix, adds a seventh member, `replaces_task_id`, naming the task it replaces (section 4.4).

Every `supporting_claim_ids` entry must name a claim in the dossier, every cited claim must have a source on the allowlist, and the advisory reviewer's `claim_checks` must cover them; a proposal whose supporting claims the reviewer marked `not_supported` or `not_checked` is admitted with the advisory reviewer's outcome attached, for the named reviewer of section 4.5 to see, because the lane records disagreement rather than resolving it.

### 4.2 What intake writes

When a bundle carries `correction_proposal`, `ingestBundle` creates the task as a revision of the existing record, with the same fields the portal's revise flow produces: `geometry` at the moved point (or the original point when there is none), `matched_osm_id` and `osm_object_type` from `place_ref`, `source_context.issue_report` with `original_point`, `source_edition`, `evidence_lane: "agent_correction"`, `source_title` and `source_url` of the first supporting claim, and `automated_checks` carrying the proposal summary. It leaves `initial_location_assertion` empty, because the contract requires a confirmed assertion and the reviewer's decision supplies the confirmation (section 4.4); it stores the proposed assertion, with its hash, inside `source_context.issue_report.location_assertion_proposed`. Before storing and hashing the assertion, intake runs `assertCountryAllowsAssertionMode` and `assertAssertionMatchesTaskPoint` (`convex/lib/locationAssertions.ts`) on it against the task's country and point, and refuses the bundle when either fails, so an assertion accepted as proposed has passed the same checks as one a reviewer tightens (section 4.4). The checks validate the assertion without confirming it: `assertLocationAssertion`, which `assertAssertionMatchesTaskPoint` calls, refuses an assertion without `contributor_confirmed: true` (verified), so PR B factors out a variant that applies every other check, and nothing records `contributor_confirmed` at intake. The intake-only draft, the receipt, the submission key, the bundle hash, the advisory review and the judgement rows are written as today. Two new judgement rows record the researcher's correction proposal and the advisory reviewer's verdict on it (`judgment_kind: correction_proposal`), so later calibration can count how often a model's moved point survived review.

**The agent authors the ordinary draft through a deterministic conversion** (JB, 2026-11-16). In the current lane a person re-keys the dossier into a fresh ordinary draft ([internal agent review](internal-agent-review.md), "Workflow and authority"). PR B replaces that re-keying with a versioned conversion contract, `agent-dossier-to-evidence.v1`, run in the same transaction as intake. The conversion is a pure function of the validated bundle and the task row, implemented in TypeScript for Convex and in Rust for `pow export verify` with shared fixtures, so either implementation reproduces the other's output byte for byte. It writes a second draft, ordinary and submitted, whose author is recorded as the research run (provider, reported model, prompt hash and run identifier) together with the conversion contract's version. Three rules govern the draft's content. First, lineage: every field and historical claim the conversion fills names the dossier claims it came from, and the draft names the intake-only draft, the receipt and the bundle hash. Second, observation stays apart from interpretation: what a source states, with its quoted support, fills the observation fields (`evidence_note` and the historical claims with their timing), while the researcher's status assessment and the proposal's change class, denomination relation and proposed states fill the interpretation fields (`change_class`, `denomination_relation`, `interpretation_note`), each recorded as the author's interpretation. Third, validation: the output passes the ordinary evidence-and-periods validators, and a dossier the contract cannot map without loss (a claim kind with no ordinary counterpart, or a proposal citing a claim the conversion cannot place) produces no ordinary draft, so the task keeps only its intake-only draft, for return or rejection as today. The conversion also writes the *proposal*, the decision fields the author proposes (`location_outcome: accept_moved_point`, `identity_decision`, `target_year_affects` with their bases, and the proposed assertion's hash), as an object `review-proposal.v1` whose `pow-object.v1` hash is the *proposal hash*. The `agent_intake_only` refusal stays on the intake-only draft; the converted draft is ordinary evidence and goes to review.

Three safeguards need extension rather than preservation. First, the validator's country check and the allowlist are New Zealand-only; a United States run needs `country_code` generalised to the registry and an allowlist version `us-v1` whose domains include `openstreetmap.org` and, for the pilot, the church's own domain, since the lane refuses a parish domain until an allowlist names it. Second, the batch `internal-agent-research` and the fixed `target_years: [2013, 2018, 2023]` become per-country, read from `convex/lib/countryYears.ts` as the portal does. Third, the submit command's deployment selector admits `dev` and `local` only, so a run against the deployment the live portals use needs either an explicit allowance for a named deployment under JB's instruction or a run on a personal development deployment; section 7 puts the choice to JB. The ingest flag (`POW_INTERNAL_AGENT_INGEST_ENABLED`) stays per deployment and per authorised run.

**Review decisions already hold the linked-place reference.** The association step the acceptance brief will need (section 5, PR C) must know which place a `successor_at_new_site`, `end_and_new_foundation`, `duplicate`, `split` or `merge` decision refers to. Main now records that place in `linked_site_ids` on `reviewDecisionInput` and the stored decision (`convex/lib/identityLinks.ts`): a successor at a new site, an end and new foundation, a split and a merge each require at least one link; a duplicate may name the record it repeats and an uncertain decision its alternatives; `same_site`, `conversion` and `new_candidate` refuse links. `reviews:applyReviewDecision` stores a link that matches an existing task as that task's site id, and stores a well-formed place identifier that matches no task (`n123`, `osm:node/123`, `candidate:…`) as entered. PR B therefore adds no decision field: an agent-correction task uses the existing control, and Kendall's decision is `same_site` with no link. `linked_site_ids` is stored outside `decision_hash`, and [internal agent review](internal-agent-review.md) states that hashing it needs a version 2 decision envelope. PR B leaves the version 0 and version 1 envelopes unchanged and introduces a version 2 envelope for decisions on agent-correction tasks (section 4.5), which names the links; whether other decisions need that envelope, and whether an accepted event's `review_decision_hash` must cover the links, are questions for PR C's brief.

### 4.3 Person-bearing text: a narrow public projection

D24 rules that a living person named in the project's records appears in public only under a pseudonym, clergy and office holders included, while contributors may be credited. The rule applies whatever the spelling and whatever a detector finds: the regex screen finds a name only behind an honorific, a capitalised-word heuristic misses "john smith", and "Rev. J. Smith preached on 3 May" passes the current screen (verified). Detection therefore flags passages for attention and never exempts a record. JB ruled on 2026-11-16 that free text is withheld from the public by default, so the public sees a narrow projection of each record, and a person clears only the strings chosen for publication.

**The public projection** is a versioned object, `public-projection.v1`, listing everything any public surface may carry for a task's accepted version: `{ task_id, evidence_version_hash, task_row_hash, selection_hash, fields, place_name, chosen_text }`. `fields` holds structured values only: the site identifier, the point and its location grade, the taxonomy codes for religion and denomination, status and worship use, target-year states with their bases, dates with their precisions, the change class, and source types. The point, the grade and the target-year states are the values the decision settles (the location outcome, the confirmed assertion and the target-year affects), so the projection reads them from a set of *decision values*: the proposal's values (section 4.2) before a decision exists, and the decision's own values after it. The identifiers of the review and the release are absent, because those records do not exist when the projection is computed; PR E attaches them in the release manifest and receipts, outside the projection hash. `place_name` is the task's name, or the generic label a person chose in its place (below). `chosen_text` lists the free-text strings chosen for publication, each as `{ field, published_text, published_sha256 }`, where the published text is the original string under the clearance `no_person` and the clearance's replacement otherwise; it is empty by default, and `selection_hash` is null when no selection exists. The original string of a replaced entry, its hash and the clearance action stay in the restricted clearance row, because a public hash of a short string naming a person can be confirmed by guessing. Every other string stays in the restricted record for reviewers: source titles and locators, quotations, notes, address and denomination text, assertion wording and the proposal's basis strings. `task_row_hash` is the `pow-object.v1` hash of the task fields the projection reads, because the evidence version's envelope (`buildEvidenceVersion`, `convex/lib/evidenceVersions.ts`) holds evidence fields and occupancies and none of the task text. One function shared by Convex and the Rust crate computes the projection from five inputs: the version's canonical bytes, the task row, the saved selection, the clearance rows that bind to it, and the decision values. The projection's own `pow-object.v1` hash is the *projection hash*. No input contains the projection hash, so clearing a string or recording a decision never changes a hash that the clearance or the decision is bound to. PR E builds every public artefact from projections alone.

**Free text reaches the public only when chosen, and only chosen text needs a person's clearance.** Selection, clearance and review happen in that order, all while the task is `needs_review` or `in_progress`, before any decision; decided statuses refuse each step until a reopen. A string enters `chosen_text` through a saved selection, recorded with `evidence:selectPublicText({ taskId, evidenceVersionHash, chosen, clientSubmissionId })` by the named reviewer (a person, or an agent through the review principal of section 4.5) or by a person holding `reviewer`, `curator` or `admin`. `chosen` names each string by its field and SHA-256. The mutation writes a `public_text_selections` row holding `public-text-selection.v1`, `{ task_id, evidence_version_hash, task_row_hash, chosen }`, whose `pow-object.v1` hash is the *selection hash*; a later selection on the same version supersedes the earlier one, and with it every clearance bound to the earlier one. A person must then clear the selection with `evidence:clearPublicText({ taskId, evidenceVersionHash, selectionHash, clearances, clientSubmissionId })`. `clearances` covers every chosen string as `{ field, text_sha256, action, replacement? }`, where `action` is `no_person`, `role_only` (the text is rewritten to the person's role, as "the pastor leads the congregation") or `pseudonym` (a stable pseudonym under D24's rules); `replacement` is required for `role_only` and `pseudonym` and refused for `no_person`. A clearer who would withhold a string records a new selection without it, which restarts clearance. The caller is a person holding `reviewer`, `curator` or `admin`, and an agent principal cannot clear. The named version must be the draft's current submitted version; every chosen string must be present with a matching `text_sha256`, and an absent or stale hash refuses the whole call. A `client_submission_receipts` row makes an identical retry return the original receipt and a changed retry return `content_conflict`, for both mutations. `clearPublicText` recomputes the selection hash from the current task row, the named version and the current selection, and refuses when it differs from the `selectionHash` the caller passes; the selection holds no clearance output, so the hash a clearance binds to stays fixed when the clearance is written. It writes one `public_text_clearances` row (task, version hash, selection hash, the cleared strings with their actions, the replacements with their hashes, cleared by, cleared at, receipt), appends a task event `public_text_cleared`, and alters neither the version nor the task. A replacement is screened like any intake string, and the screen refuses a replacement that itself names a person.

**The place name is public by default, and a name that may carry a person's name needs a human disposition.** A place name can carry a living person's name ("John Smith Memorial Chapel", "Pastor John Smith Ministries"), and D24 applies to it like any other string. Three protections cover it. First, the screens run on the place name at intake and again when the projection is computed: the regex screen refuses an honorific-led name, as today, and a name heuristic marks the name `attention: suspected_person` when any of its words, in whatever case, falls outside a vocabulary of place and street names, saints' names in dedications, taxonomy terms, common English words, months and listed organisations. Second, the named reviewer checks the place name against D24 as one of the values to be published (section 4.5), and a doubt is recorded as a finding. Third, a marked name, or a finding about one, is text that may name a person, which always needs a person's disposition before release (section 4.5). The disposition is a clearance: every selection on a task with a marked name or such a finding includes the field `place_name`, and the person clears it with `clearPublicText`, keeping the name (`no_person`), replacing it with a role (`role_only`), or replacing it (`pseudonym`) with a pseudonym under D24 or with a generic label built from the taxonomy ("Baptist church"). An unmarked name that the reviewer passes is published without a clearance row. The residual risk is a name that the screens and the reviewer both miss, such as a surname that is also a common word ("Smith Family Chapel"); the blinded human audit of a later step would measure it, and section 8 records its rate as unmeasured.

**Detectors mark, and only mark.** At intake the existing screen refuses the bundle as today, and for `agent-review-bundle.v2` only it is extended with United States and international telephone formats and with absolute-path patterns (`/Users/`, `/home/`, `C:\`, UNC paths); version 1 bundles keep the version 1 screen unchanged, with compatibility fixtures in all three validators (a version 1 bundle carrying a United States number passes as today; the same bundle under version 2 refuses), so B1 stays additive. The name heuristic marks strings in the restricted record as well, so the reviewer sees each mark beside its string; a mark on a withheld string changes nothing in public, and a chosen string needs clearance whether or not it is marked.

**Binding through review and freeze.** The review snapshot is taken after selection and clearance. For an agent-correction task it reads the current selection and the clearance rows bound to it, which `reviewSnapshot` (`convex/reviews.ts`) does not read today, and it pins the *candidate projection*, computed with the proposal's decision values, and its hash, so the reviewer sees exactly what would be published if the proposal is accepted. A change to the evidence version, to a task field the projection reads, to the selection or to a clearance makes the snapshot stale. The snapshot gains these members for agent-correction tasks only, so the hash inputs of every other task's snapshot stay as they are. A decision `accepted_for_export` carries `projection_hash`, the hash of the projection computed with the decision's own values; the decision values exclude that member, so no hash depends on itself. Accepted as proposed, the decision's projection is the candidate the snapshot pins; a departing decision's projection differs where its note says it departs, and the server recomputes it and refuses a mismatch. A decision `accepted_for_export` is refused in `recordReviewDecision` and `batchRecordReviewDecisions` when the projection holds a chosen string without a matching clearance or a marked place name without a person's disposition. PI acceptance and `assertTaskExportAuthority` at batch creation and freeze recompute the projection from the decision's pinned `evidence_version_hash`, the task row, the selection, the clearances and the decision, refuse when its hash differs from the decision's `projection_hash`, and apply the same refusals. The frozen bundle gains `public_projections.jsonl`, `public_text_selections.jsonl` and `public_text_clearances.jsonl`, which `pow export verify` checks by recomputing each projection from `tasks.jsonl`, `evidence_versions.jsonl`, `review_decisions.jsonl` and those two files. The intake-only draft needs no projection, because export authority attaches to the accepted version alone.

**Retained audit material against publication-eligible content.** The bundle collector keeps every draft and every evidence version of an included task (`convex/exports.ts`), the intake-only draft and superseded versions included, and PR B leaves that collection as it is: those rows are restricted audit history, retrievable by curators and never public, and retaining them discloses nothing. Publication-eligible content is the projection of the accepted version named by the authoritative decision's `evidence_version_hash`. On every agent-correction task, `pow export verify` recomputes that projection, checks each chosen string against a clearance row whose hashes reproduce, and treats every other version as restricted history.

**Fixtures** in the validators and the Convex tests: a note "john smith leads the congregation" (no detector fires; the note is withheld by default, the projection omits it, and no clearance is needed); the same note chosen for publication (clearance required; cleared as `role_only` to "the pastor leads the congregation"); "Rev. J. Smith preached on 3 May" chosen for publication (the regex screen admits it, as verified; clearance pseudonymises it); "Pastor John Smith" (refused by the regex screen at intake, as today); a locator `https://example.org/people/john-smith` (withheld by default and absent from the projection); the place names "Wayside Baptist Church" (unmarked, published without a clearance row), "St John's Anglican Church" (the dedication vocabulary leaves it unmarked) and "John Smith Memorial Chapel" (marked, so release refuses until a person records a disposition); a Miami telephone number and a `/Users/…` path (refused by the extended screen); a replacement text that itself names a person (refused at clearance by the screen); a chosen string changed after clearance (the stale hash refuses); a clearance written after its selection, whose selection hash still reproduces; a `role_only` or `pseudonym` clearance without a replacement (refused); a pseudonymised string, whose projection entry carries only the replacement and its hash, never the original's hash; a new selection after clearance (the earlier clearance lapses and acceptance refuses until the new selection is cleared); a clearance written after the snapshot (the snapshot goes stale); a task field the projection reads, edited after review (the snapshot goes stale and acceptance refuses); a departing decision whose `projection_hash` disagrees with its recomputation (refused); a replacement text whose recorded hash disagrees with the stored text (freeze refuses); an acceptance with a chosen string uncleared (refused in both review routes, at PI acceptance and at freeze); a projection row that disagrees with its recomputation or carries a field outside the allow-list (`pow export verify` refuses); and a bundle holding the intake-only draft and a superseded version beside the accepted version, which `pow export verify` passes. Kendall's projection carries structured fields, the unmarked place name "Wayside Baptist Church" and no chosen text; the fixture asserts the projection hash, a null `selection_hash` and the absence of any selection or clearance row.

### 4.4 The reviewer's decision confirms the location assertion

The location contract requires a confirmed assertion, and the agent's proposed assertion is unconfirmed (section 4.1). JB ruled on 2026-11-16 that the reviewer's snapshot-linked decision stands in for a separate confirmation by a preparer. The review snapshot covers the task row, including `issue_report.location_assertion_proposed` and its stored hash, so the decision pins the proposed assertion, and a changed assertion makes the snapshot, and therefore the decision, stale. PR B adds no confirmation mutation and no write to the task row.

A decision `accepted_for_export` on an agent-correction task carries `confirmed_assertion_hash`. Accepting the assertion as proposed sets that member to the hash stored at intake. A reviewer who tightens the assertion (a radius, a narrower mode, wording) without moving the point records the tightened assertion on the decision, with its hash and a typed note naming the change (section 4.5); `assertCountryAllowsAssertionMode` and `assertAssertionMatchesTaskPoint` run on it as for `createIssueTask`, and `contributor_confirmed` is true. The confirmed assertion lives on the decision and is never copied to the task row, so the decision leaves its own snapshot current; PR C's translation reads the assertion from the decision. A reviewer who would move the point records a finding instead, because a moved point is a new proposal. `accept_moved_point` settles on the task's own point (`acceptedPoint`, `convex/lib/locationOutcome.ts`), drafts carry no point, and the conversion runs only inside intake, so no new evidence version on the existing task can change the point.

**The author's fix is a revised bundle and a replacement task.** Every fix the author makes, to the point or to anything else, is a revised bundle: a new dossier, hence a new submission key and, under intake's existing rule (`agent-research:<submission_key>`, `convex/internalAgentIntake.ts`), a new task, whose `correction_proposal` names the task it replaces in `replaces_task_id`. Intake refuses a replacement unless the replaced task is an agent-correction task for the same `place_ref` whose status is neither `reviewed`, `pi_accepted` nor `exported` (a decided task needs a reopen first). It converts the revised dossier as in section 4.2, writing the corrected point into the new task's `geometry` and the new proposal, stores `replaces_task_id` in the new task's `issue_report`, and appends a task event `replaced_by_revision`, naming the new task, to the replaced task. That event is the only write intake makes to the replaced task, and it leaves the task row unchanged. Because the review snapshot reads every task event, the event makes every snapshot of the replaced task stale, so no approval recorded on it can be used; `assertAgentCorrectionReady` also refuses export authority on any task that carries the event. The replaced task keeps its review passes, findings and decisions as history, the review portal shows the two tasks as a chain, and a reviewer closes the replaced task with the decision `duplicate_task`, naming the replacement in `linked_site_ids`. A finding fixed in this way records the disposition `fixed` with the replacement task and its evidence version.

**Confirmation is required, not advised.** A converted draft on an agent-correction task could otherwise be accepted and frozen with the agent's assertion unconfirmed, because `assertLocationOutcome` reads the two points and never the assertion. A shared check, `assertAgentCorrectionReady(task, version, decision)`, therefore runs wherever export authority is granted or rechecked: in `recordReviewDecision` and `batchRecordReviewDecisions` for a decision `accepted_for_export`, in `recordAcceptance` (the PI), and in `assertTaskExportAuthority` at batch creation and freeze. For a task whose `issue_report.evidence_lane` is `agent_correction` with a `location_assertion_proposed`, the check requires four things: an authoritative decision whose `confirmed_assertion_hash` equals either the stored proposed hash or the hash of the assertion the decision records; a decision snapshot that covers the current task row, selection and clearances; (section 4.3) cleared chosen strings, a person's disposition for any marked place name, and a `projection_hash` that reproduces; and no `replaced_by_revision` event on the task. The frozen bundle carries the decision with its confirmed assertion in `review_decisions.jsonl`, and `pow export verify` checks the hash on every agent-correction task. The fixture records an acceptance without `confirmed_assertion_hash` and asserts refusal at every one of those four points, and it asserts that a decision whose snapshot predates a change to the stored assertion is refused as stale.

### 4.5 Review on the model of a pull request, and the humans the Kendall run needs

JB ruled on 2026-11-16 that review of records follows the model of a pull request. A case is the pull request: the author proposes, a named reviewer examines the case and records findings, required checks pass, and merge is inclusion in a released batch. Six rules carry the model into PR B.

The first rule concerns **identities**. The author and the named reviewer are each recorded with provider, model version as the provider reports it, prompt or policy version and run, or, for a person, by user. For an agent-correction task the author is the research run whose dossier the conversion read (section 4.2).

The second rule concerns **the named reviewer**, who may be a person or an agent. An agent reviewer comes from a provider other than the author's and examines the sources itself: its review records, for every claim that supports a value in the projection, whether it opened the source, saw it only through a search snippet, or did not check it, and it can approve only when it opened every such source. The minimum is one named reviewer, and a class policy may require more through the existing `extra_opinions_required`, counted on the current evidence version (section 6). The advisory review written at intake is an input and does not count as the named review, because it examined the dossier and not the converted version.

The third rule concerns **findings and their dispositions**. Each review pass is an append-only `review_passes` row holding the reviewer's identity, the snapshot hash it examined, its verdict (approve or request changes) and its findings, each with an identifier, the field it concerns and its text. Every finding receives a recorded disposition, under the identity of whoever records it: `fixed` (naming the replacement task and the evidence version that fixes it), `declined` with a reason, or `escalated` to a person. A fix comes from the author as a revised bundle, which intake converts into a replacement task with a new evidence version (section 4.4), and the fix voids every earlier approval, because each approval binds to a snapshot of the task and version it examined and the replaced task's snapshots go stale.

The fourth rule concerns **the decision and its note**. Accepting as proposed records `accepted_as_proposed: true` and the proposal hash (section 4.2) and needs no note; the server checks that the decision's fields equal the proposal. A typed note is required only where the decision departs from the proposal, and the note names each changed field. Each text is recorded under the identity of whoever wrote it: the case author's reasoning under the author's identity, and the decision and any note under the reviewer's, so the record never presents an agent's text as a person's. Today `applyReviewDecision` requires a note of at least eight characters on every decision (`convex/reviews.ts`, verified at `98b74334`), so PR B relaxes that check for decisions accepted as proposed on agent-correction tasks only. Decisions on these tasks use a version 2 decision envelope, `review-decision.v2`, which adds to the version 1 fields the reviewer's identity, `accepted_as_proposed`, the proposal hash, `confirmed_assertion_hash` (section 4.4), `projection_hash` (section 4.3), `evidence_version_hash` and `linked_site_ids`; the version 0 and version 1 envelopes stay unchanged.

The fifth rule lists the **required checks**: the three validators and the personal-details screen at intake; the conversion's reproduction in Rust; the target-year keys against the task's years; a current snapshot; the projection's allow-list, the place-name screen and the clearance of chosen strings (section 4.3); the confirmed assertion (section 4.4); and a computed list of *human disposition reasons*.

The sixth rule names **the cases that always need a human disposition before release**. The server computes the reasons on each case and refuses release while a reason stands without a person's recorded disposition. The reasons are an identity ruling other than `same_site`; an adjudication; text that may name a person (a marked place name, a finding about a person, or a string chosen for publication, which section 4.3 already requires a person to clear); a cultural flag (a `privacy_flag` other than `clear`, an import row marked `culturally_sensitive`, or an advisory `defer_cultural` recommendation); an unresolved finding, meaning one without a disposition or one escalated; and the first use of a source type, a country, a model or a prompt version, judged against the cases already released.

**Release is full stop by default.** Full stop is the only mode PR B supports: review and checks complete, and the case waits for the lead to read and release it. Until batch release passes its tests, release is the existing per-item PI acceptance, `recordAcceptance`. JB ruled on 2026-11-16 that batch release by the lead replaces per-item acceptance once it does (section 5, PR C; section 6), and batch release is designed in its own brief. Full send would release records under a dated policy that the lead signs for a class, naming the reviewers, the country, a volume cap, an audit rate and an expiry, and resting on a blinded human audit within tolerance; the brief names full send as a later step and leaves it undesigned. A case with a human disposition reason never goes full send.

**What the server must change.** Review roles belong to human users today: the review mutations admit users holding `reviewer`, `curator` or `admin` through `requireUser`, and the author exclusion compares the draft's `created_by` with the deciding user (`convex/reviews.ts`, verified at `98b74334`). PR B therefore adds an agent-review principal, admitted through an internal mutation gated per deployment like intake (`POW_INTERNAL_AGENT_REVIEW_ENABLED`), through which the controller records an agent's review pass and decision; model processes receive no deployment credentials, as at intake. The principal is distinct from the service user that holds converted drafts, and the author exclusion is generalised: the reviewer's identity differs from the author's, and an agent reviewer's provider differs from an agent author's. PR B admits the agent principal on agent-correction tasks only, and RA-authored tasks keep today's rules. The review portal shows the proposal, the projection and the human disposition reasons, offers acceptance as proposed, and records a person's dispositions. These additions give a new principal authority to decide, so B1 takes a reserved review as a security-sensitive change (section 6).

**Kendall's retrospective assertion.** The reviewer records `accepted_for_export` on the current snapshot with `location_outcome: accept_moved_point`, `identity_decision: same_site`, and `target_year_affects: [{ target_year: 2026, target_year_status: "present", basis: "reviewer_inference" }]`, all as proposed. That assertion needs an entry path, because today the review portal derives `target_year_affects` from the draft's `target_year_statuses` with a fixed `basis: source_observation` (`targetYearAffectsFromDraft`, `apps/regions/nz/js/review-portal.js`), and a United States task's default `target_years` are 2000, 2010 and 2020 (verified), so the converted draft would yield no 2026 affect and no inference basis. PR B adds three pieces. First, intake sets a correction task's `target_years` to the country's years plus the anchor year of the observation (2026 for Kendall); the conversion writes `target_year_statuses: { "2026": "present" }` with `target_year_evidence` for that year stating the inference (worship documented on 19 October; presence at 1 September inferred), and the proposal carries the affect with basis `reviewer_inference`. For agent-correction tasks only, the server validates `target_year_statuses` keys against the task's years on save and submit (B1). The same check on every task narrows an existing write path, since `targetYearStatusSet` accepts any string key today, so general enforcement belongs to B2, with a compatibility fixture showing an existing task's arbitrary keys accepted under B1. Second, the contributor portal today renders, reads and builds drafts from the country constant `TARGET_YEARS` (`apps/regions/nz/js/verification-map.js`: the status controls, the status map, the wide-row field list, `currentFormValues` and `buildEvidenceDraft`), so a person who opens an agent-correction task in the portal would see no 2026 control. PR B makes the open task's `target_years` the source of years throughout rendering, loading of a saved draft, validation and evidence construction, falling back to the country constant only for a new nomination with no task. Third, the review portal's decision form lists the proposed affects with a basis control per year, defaulting to the proposal's basis, or else `source_observation`, and offering `reviewer_inference` and `source_statement`; a person reviewing may change a status or a basis, which is a departure with its note, and the decision stores what the reviewer chose. ## 6. Sequencing

Two server fixes come first, because the review model of section 4.5 rests on snapshot hashes and on opinions bound to the version they examined; both gaps are verified on `main` at `98b74334`. The first fix, P1, makes `pow export verify` recompute each review snapshot's content hash. Today the command checks that the snapshot hashes the decisions name are present in `review_snapshots.jsonl` and equal the manifest's set, and it reports in an informational note that it does not recompute snapshot content, because that hash is taken over an older canonical form the crate has never implemented (`crates/pow-cli/src/export.rs`). The second fix, P2, binds additional opinions to the current evidence version. Today `applyReviewDecision` (`convex/reviews.ts`) counts as additional opinions the decisions of every other reviewer on the task, with no filter by evidence draft or version, so an opinion recorded on an earlier version satisfies the gate on the current one. P1 and P2 are small pull requests, and both precede B1.

PR A and PR B are designed here. A can be built now, in parallel with P1 and P2; B depends on nothing in A except the fixture's event shapes. Estimates are agent implementation under review: P1 and P2 together about three days; A about one week (eight schema changes, gates 2, 3 and 6, fixtures); B about two and a half weeks, delivered as two pull requests. B1 is additive: the two schema versions in three validators with the version 2 screen beside the unchanged version 1 screen; the intake changes and the conversion with its Rust reproduction; task-specific target years in the contributor portal; the agent-review principal with `review_passes` and the version 2 decision envelope; the public projection, `selectPublicText` and `clearPublicText` for chosen strings, with their receipts and tables; the revised-bundle route with `replaces_task_id`; the review portal's proposal, disposition and basis controls; the guide; and the fixtures. B1 deploys under the ordinary closure rule and, as a new authorised write path and a new principal with authority to decide, takes a reserved review beside the routine one. B2 tightens gates and is non-additive under the project's deployment rule: `recordReviewDecision`, `batchRecordReviewDecisions`, `recordAcceptance` and `assertTaskExportAuthority` gain the readiness, projection and human-disposition refusals. The linked-place rules B2 would otherwise have tightened are already enforced on main through `linked_site_ids` (section 4.2). Legacy behaviour is fixed: decisions recorded before B2 are read as recorded and never re-validated; B2's refusals apply only to tasks whose `evidence_lane` is `agent_correction`, of which none exists before B1; a legacy task is unaffected by B2. B2's deployment is sequenced by JB (ruling 13).

Batch release by the lead replaces per-item PI acceptance once batch release passes its tests (ruling 16). Batch release needs P1, since a release pins snapshot hashes, and is designed in its own brief; until it passes its tests, the Kendall run uses per-item acceptance. ## 7. Rulings for JB

Each ruling is answerable with a yes, a no or a choice. Rulings 1 to 11 and 13 concern PR A and PR B; ruling 12 is the one roadmap decision that shapes A's schema; rulings 14 to 17 set the review model and the governing constraint. JB ruled on rulings 8, 9, 10 and 14 to 17 on 2026-11-16, and those entries record his rulings; ruling 3 includes his direction of the same date on revivals in a cascade; the others remain open.

1. **Accession and within-site movement as paired types.** Version 2 adds `site_registered` (intent `accession`) and `site_location_changed` (intent `observed_change`), and leaves `site_created` bound to `lifecycle`. Recommended: yes.
2. **The correction target and its conflict key.** `corrects` binds a correction to an attribute, an opening event and an interval where scoped; corrections with intersecting affected intervals on one attribute are refused for adjudication. Recommended: yes.
3. **Supersession and retraction rules.** A superseder names explicit events of the same target and attribute; an unretracted superseder suppresses its predecessor along the lineage even when it is itself superseded; a retraction with live dependants is refused unless it names them or a replacement precedes it, a supersession that would leave a live correction anchored to a suppressed opening event is refused unless the same acceptance reanchors that correction, and the check reads the live set the retraction would leave, so a revival that would leave a live event referencing a retracted event is refused unless the cascade names it (JB, 2026-11-16); only a retraction restores, and it restores what no other unretracted superseder names. A retraction of an adjudication validates; the revived corrections' conflict key takes the state `adjudication_required`, which blocks that site and attribute: export of the site refuses until a reviewed adjudication lands, and neither correction wins by date. Recommended: yes.
4. **A retrospective assertion within D3.** A reviewer's assertion about an anchor is carried as an `effective` bound covering the anchor, with the observation date kept in `observed_on`; a contradiction with a lifecycle event is refused for adjudication. Confirm this reading of D3.
5. **The `exact` grade.** `exact` is refused on every event until a reviewed evidence rule defines it; a registered source point carries `unknown`. Recommended: yes.
6. **Agent lane: country and allowlist.** Generalise the validator's country to the registry and add `us-v1` naming `openstreetmap.org` and `waysidemiami.org` for the pilot, with the parish-domain rule kept for every other domain. Recommended: yes.
7. **Agent lane: deployment.** Allow the submit command to target a named deployment under JB's written instruction for an authorised run, or run Kendall on a personal development deployment and accept that its review, acceptance and freeze happen there. Recommended: the named deployment under instruction.
8. **Agent lane: Kendall's humans.** Ruled by JB, 2026-11-16: the Kendall run needs one person, JB, who records the human dispositions the case requires and accepts as PI; the named reviewer may be an agent from a provider other than the author's, and a class policy may require further reviewers (section 4.5).
9. **The reviewer's decision confirms the location assertion.** Ruled by JB, 2026-11-16: the reviewer's snapshot-linked decision stands in for a preparer's confirmation. The decision pins the proposed assertion through its snapshot and carries `confirmed_assertion_hash`; a changed assertion makes the decision stale; and `assertAgentCorrectionReady` enforces the confirmation at review, PI acceptance and freeze (section 4.4).
10. **Free text withheld from the public by default.** Ruled by JB, 2026-11-16: a narrow public projection carries structured fields and the place name; a free-text string reaches the public only when chosen for publication, and only chosen strings need a person's clearance, bound to the saved selection of strings it clears, so that writing a clearance leaves the hash it binds to unchanged, with actions `no_person`, `role_only` and `pseudonym`. A place name that may carry a person's name needs a person's disposition before release; detectors mark passages and exempt nothing; the review snapshot pins the projection (section 4.3).
11. **The linked-place reference on review decisions.** PR B uses main's `linked_site_ids` as the linked-place reference and adds no decision field for it; the version 0 and version 1 decision envelopes stay unchanged; the version 2 envelope PR B introduces for decisions on agent-correction tasks names the links (section 4.5), and whether other decisions and accepted events need that envelope is for PR C's brief. Recommended: yes.
12. **Nullable proposal and batch-release hashes in version 2.** The schema makes `proposal_hash` and `batch_release_hash` nullable so that acceptance can begin before proposal pinning and PI batch release ship; whether any event may be accepted while they are null, and under what stated exception, is for PR C's brief. Does JB accept the nullable shape in the contract now? Recommended: yes.
13. **Sequencing the non-additive part of B.** B2 narrows the review decision validator and adds export refusals, which the deployment rule treats as non-additive; JB runs or sequences its deployment, after B1 and before the Kendall run. Does JB authorise that sequence? Recommended: yes.
14. **Agent authorship of the ordinary draft.** Ruled by JB, 2026-11-16: an agent may author the ordinary draft, and a deterministic, validated conversion from dossier to ordinary evidence, preserving claim lineage and the distinction between observation and interpretation, replaces a person's re-keying of the dossier (section 4.2).
15. **Review on the model of a pull request.** Ruled by JB, 2026-11-16: the author and the named reviewer are recorded with provider, model version, prompt or policy version and run. The reviewer may be an agent, comes from a provider other than the author's and examines the sources itself; one such reviewer is the minimum, and a class policy may require more. Each finding receives a recorded disposition (fixed, declined with a reason, or escalated to a person), and a new evidence version voids earlier approvals. Accepting as proposed records the proposal hash, and a typed note is required only for a departure from the proposal. Full stop is the default; full send, release under a dated policy the lead signs for a class, is a later step. Identity rulings other than same site, adjudications, text that may name a person, cultural flags, unresolved findings, and the first use of a source type, country, model or prompt version always need a person's disposition before release (section 4.5).
16. **Batch release by the lead.** Ruled by JB, 2026-11-16: batch release by the lead replaces per-item PI acceptance once batch release passes its tests; batch release is designed in its own brief (sections 4.5, 5 and 6).
17. **The governing constraint.** Ruled by JB, 2026-11-16: "Every published value traces to evidence, to a recorded review by a named reviewer, and to a named human who authorised its release, case by case or under a dated policy for its class" (section 1).
