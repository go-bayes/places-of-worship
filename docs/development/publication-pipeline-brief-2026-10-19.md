# Publication pipeline brief: the change-event contract and agent-filed corrections, with Kendall as the fixture (2026-10-19)

Status: DESIGN, narrowed. The amended section 7 of the [vocabulary reconciliation](c2-vocabulary-reconciliation-2026-08-14.md) requires a path from a principal investigator's (PI) batch release to a public release: a continuous accepted log, replay, a reviewed overlay that replaces the OpenStreetMap (OSM) dot it corrects, automatic publication with receipts, and an annual edition. This brief designs in full the two pull requests that carry the Kendall church of [EC-001](../edge-cases.md), filed by an agent, as far as a frozen export: version 2 of the change-event contract (PR A) and agent intake of corrections with a human preparer's confirmation (PR B). The seven later pull requests, from `pow accept` to the annual edition, appear in outline only (section 5), each with the design questions its own brief must answer before it is built. Rulings cited by number (D3, D4, D9, D12, D13, D18, D24, D25) are the project's standing decisions; the brief states their content where it relies on them. The brief adopts nothing by itself, and every pull request it proposes needs its own authority. Section 8 lists what we verified in code and what we infer.

## 1. The problem

The project can review a place of worship, accept the review and freeze the accepted evidence, and there the governed chain stops. Nothing turns a frozen export into an accepted event, a rebuilt master or a public map. The one bridge that exists, `scripts/build_occupancy_dated_places.py`, reads a Convex export directly, draws a dated point only where a reviewer confirmed a derived census-year location, and takes religion and denomination from the matched OSM feature, so a reviewed denomination stays off the map and the OSM dot a correction replaces stays on it. Kendall shows every gap at once: the OSM node lies 87 m from the building with religion recorded and denomination blank; the correction is a change in our knowledge, which the 2026-08-22 census design would have counted as a change in the world; and the public map lacks any means of replacing the dot.

D25 settles the model. Accepted claims enter a continuous log at each PI batch release; the master is a replay of that log; once a year the project pins an edition at a knowledge cut-off and publishes a census of change, a revision ledger and an onset-unknown class. Its amendment settles the operating terms: a public release follows each batch release and is published automatically with a receipt and a withdrawal path; a reviewed record replaces the OSM dot it corrects in the default view, with grid-thinned groups recomputed before publication; the event contract gains a correction scope and an accession intent; the ODbL question gates the first public reviewed release; each edition carries a provisional OSM tier; outputs are called an annual register. Two of those terms, the correction scope and the accession intent, change the contract every later stage reads, and the agent lane is where the Kendall correction will be filed. Here, we design those two pieces, and we outline the rest.

Write state(*S*, *T*) for the state of the world on date *S* as reconstructed under D3 from events accepted by audit time *T*: an event replays into *S* when its effective time is at or before *S* and its `recorded_at` is at or before *T*, a bounded effective time orders by its latest admissible date, and a bound that straddles *S* contributes uncertainty. Let *S*<sub>Y</sub> be 1 September of year Y.

## 2. What exists

We read the code and contracts on branch `main` at `78bcaa0e`. Seven facts set the design.

1. **The `pow` verb set stops before acceptance.** `crates/pow-cli/src/main.rs` defines `validate`, `validate-agent`, `stage`, `propose`, `diff`, `object` (hash and verify) and `export verify`. Of the seven D9 gates, `pow validate` implements schema (gate 1), source presence (gate 5) and the idempotency key (gate 7), plus coordinate ranges and date ordering; country containment and relocation distance (gate 2), future-date and source-coverage checks (gate 3), the 50 m duplicate-risk flag (gate 4) and taxonomy membership (gate 6) remain unimplemented. `pow propose` mints deterministic identifiers (`deterministic_uuid` over a seed).
2. **The canonical serialisation D15 names exists.** `crates/pow-cli/src/canonical.rs` implements `pow-canonical-json.v1` (RFC 8785) and the `pow-object.v1` hash, checked against `schemas/fixtures/pow-canonical-json.v1.json` and shared with `convex/lib/canonicalJson.ts`. The change-event schema's `payload_hash` is described as "hash of the normalised payload" without naming a canonical form.
3. **The contract binds `site_created` to `lifecycle`** and the correction types to `correction`; a `site_location_corrected` event carries `effective` and leaves its scope unstated (`schemas/change-event.schema.json`, `allOf`; the sample in `docs/examples/revisions/nz-sample-change-events.jsonl` has `effective_date: 2018-03-06`). `GeometryPayload` carries `previous_geometry_history_id`, an immutable reference; `DenominationPayload` carries `previous_denomination_code` and `StatusPayload` `previous_status`, which are values and can recur across several intervals. `GeometryPayload` carries `uncertainty_radius_m` and no location grade. `TargetYearAffect.target_year` is a date (`2018-09-01`); the Convex validator `targetYearAffect.target_year` is a number (`2018`). `supersedes_event_ids` is a free list with no rule about what a superseding event may name.
4. **The location grade is derived from the assertion.** `locationConfidenceGrade` (`convex/lib/locationAssertions.ts`) returns `building` for mode `building_identified`, which carries no radius, and `parcel_or_compound`, `street`, `locality` or `area` from the radius of an `approximate_area` assertion. It never returns `exact`; `geometry-history.schema.json` lists `exact` and `unknown` as further grades.
5. **Agent intake accepts a research dossier, and nothing else.** `convex/internalAgentIntake.ts` `ingestBundle` validates a bundle with `validateAgentReviewBundle` (`convex/lib/agentIntake.ts`), writes a task in batch `internal-agent-research` with country `NZ`, geometry from `dossier.candidate_location`, `matched_osm_id` parsed from `place_ref`, and an evidence draft marked `agent_intake_only`, plus the advisory review, claim-grain judgments and an immutable receipt. The bundle schema (`scripts/agent_research/schemas/agent-review-bundle.v1.json`) lacks every field a correction needs: a location assertion, an original point, a denomination relation, a label basis and a change class; the task's `source_context` carries origin, bundle hash and submission key only, without an `issue_report`. The validator requires `place.country_code === "NZ"` and an allowlist version it knows (`nz-v1`); a parish's own domain is refused until an allowlist names it. The submit command accepts the `dev` and `local` selectors only. An `agent_intake_only` draft cannot be accepted for export (`convex/reviews.ts`, line 270) and is excluded from the external AI review queue (`convex/claudeReviews.ts`, line 155). The personal-details screen (`hasPersonalDetails`, mirrored in `lib.py` and the Rust validator) detects telephone numbers in New Zealand formats, e-mail addresses and honorific-led names; a bare name such as "John Smith" passes it. All verified.
6. **A task's location assertion is written at creation only.** `initial_location_assertion` is set by `createIssueTask`, `createManualCandidateTask`, rapid entry and batch import; `evidence:saveEvidenceDraft` takes a task id, a draft and client context, checks `assertOwnsOrCanReview` and `evidenceIntakeRefusal` (which refuses decided statuses until a reopen), and no mutation updates the assertion on an existing task. Review decisions carry `identity_decision` as an enum (`same_site`, `conversion`, `successor_at_new_site`, `end_and_new_foundation`, `new_candidate`, `duplicate`, `split`, `merge`, `relocation`, `uncertain`) with no field for the linked place; the PI guide asks the reviewer to name it in the decision note. Idempotent submissions use `client_submission_receipts`, keyed by member and submission id with a request digest. PI acceptance refuses the PI's own submission and permits the PI to ratify a decision the PI recorded, recording `self_decided: true` (`convex/lib/acceptance.ts`).
7. **The portal's revise flow** (`enterReviseMode` and `createRevisionTask` in `apps/regions/nz/js/verification-map.js`) calls `tasks:createIssueTask` with the confirmed point as the task geometry, the record's point as `originalLatitude` and `originalLongitude`, the OSM identifier, and a `locationAssertion` with `contributor_confirmed: true`; the server stores `source_context.issue_report.original_point` and `initial_location_assertion`, checks the assertion against the task point, and at review `assertLocationOutcome` requires `location_outcome` whenever the pin moved. Drafts carry `change_class`, `denomination_or_tradition_raw`, `denomination_label_basis` and `denomination_relation` (`convex/model.ts`).

The [content-addressed review contract](content-addressed-review.md) (approved 2026-09-11) lists what an accepted event must reference, requires acceptance and withdrawal to share a processing guard, and leaves its third to fifth steps (proposal pinning, PI batch release, `pow` acceptance and replay) unbuilt. The [human review and release design](human-review-and-release.md) names the stages after a freeze as planned. The live gate is per-item PI acceptance plus a curator freeze.

## 3. PR A: the change-event contract, version 2

Items 4 and 5 of the amendment change the contract, so version 2 of the schema comes first. We propose a new file, `schemas/change-event.v2.schema.json`, beside the current file, which stays as version 1 (the repository already keeps `indicator.schema.json` beside `indicator.v2.schema.json`). Events declare `schema_version: "change-event.v2"`. `pow validate` accepts both versions by the declared version; the version 1 examples stay valid under version 1; every later stage writes version 2 only. Version 2 makes eight changes.

**Change 1: `correction_scope`.** A top-level property, `correction_scope`, with the values `whole_validity` and `interval` and the schema default `whole_validity`, permitted only when `event_intent` is `correction` and refused on every other intent. An accepted event (`review.review_status: accepted`) must carry the value explicitly, so the log is complete without any reader applying a default; a staged or proposed event may omit it. Under `whole_validity` the correction rewrites the targeted state across its whole valid interval; under `interval` it rewrites the part of that state within the bounds change 2 names.

**Change 2: the state a correction rewrites is bound to an attribute, an opening event and, where needed, an interval.** A previous value (`previous_denomination_code`, `previous_status`) identifies a state ambiguously, because the same value can hold in several intervals of one site's history, and a date alone chooses nothing after a within-site move. Version 2 therefore adds `corrects`, required on every accepted correction and refused on every other intent:

```json
"corrects": {
  "attribute": "location",
  "opened_by_event_id": "<event_id of the live event that opened the state>",
  "interval": { "valid_from": "1987-01-01", "valid_to": null }
}
```

`attribute` is one of `location`, `denomination`, `status`, `name`, `worship_use`, `attributes`, `identity`, and must agree with the event type (a `site_location_corrected` event corrects `location`). `opened_by_event_id` names the live event whose payload opened the state being corrected: for the first location state, the `site_registered` or `site_created` event; after a within-site move, the `site_location_changed` event; for a denomination state, the `denomination_changed` or registration event that set it. `interval` is required when `correction_scope` is `interval` and refused otherwise; its `valid_to` is null for open-ended. The previous-value members and `previous_geometry_history_id` stay as human-readable context and must agree with the referenced state, or validation refuses. The *conflict key* of a correction is (`site_id`, `attribute`, affected interval), where the affected interval is the referenced state's whole interval under `whole_validity` and the named interval otherwise. Two live corrections whose conflict keys share a site and attribute and whose affected intervals intersect are a conflict: validation of a set of events refuses it with `adjudication_required`, and the resolution is a reviewed adjudication (D12), never the newer winning by date. An adjudication is a new correction of the same site and attribute that names the conflicting corrections in `supersedes_event_ids`, whatever their intervals, and states its own scope and interval. Supersession is whole-event: a superseded correction contributes nothing at any *S*, so the portions of its interval the adjudication leaves uncovered revert to the underlying state, and a reviewer who wants part of a superseded correction kept re-emits that part as a further correction. Corrections of 1980 to 2000 and 1990 to 2010, say, are resolved by one adjudication covering 1980 to 2010, or by one covering 1990 to 2010 beside a re-emitted 1980 to 1990, and the fixture carries both shapes. A correction is thereby confined to the state it names: a later observed change or lifecycle event opens a new state with its own opening event, and a successor site is a different `site_id` under the operational definition (version 0.1.6).

What `effective` means on a correction. D3 defines `effective` on a correction as the time the corrected fact became true. On a `whole_validity` correction, replay reads `effective` for the D9 date-plausibility gate and for nothing else: the correction contributes no transition at any *S*, and the rewritten state keeps the interval the referenced state had. For Kendall, the corrected fact (the church stands on its building) held before any evidence we hold, so the fixture records `not_later_than` equal to the earliest evidence date with `date_precision: bounded`. The sample event's `effective_date: 2018-03-06` becomes harmless under version 2 because the scope and the target say what the date is for.

**Change 3: accession and within-site movement as paired types.** D4 pairs event types with intents so that a contributor must choose (`site_location_corrected` against `site_relocated`). We follow that pattern twice. Version 2 adds `site_registered`, bound to a new intent value `accession`, with the existing `site_created` payload (`payload_type: site_created`, carrying `site_id`, `country_code`, `geometry`, `status`). `site_created` stays bound to `lifecycle` and means founding or opening. Replay reads `site_registered` as the site entering the record with presence bounded by its `effective`: with `not_later_than` set, the site is present at every anchor *S* at or after that date; at anchors before it, with `not_earlier_than` absent, the bound straddles *S* and the site contributes uncertainty, which is the onset-unknown class of D25. An accession contributes no transition to the census of change. A `site_registered` or `site_created` event opens a state for every correctable attribute of the site: `location` and `status` from its payload, and `denomination`, `name`, `worship_use` and `attributes` with the value `unknown` where the payload omits them, so that a later correction of any attribute has an opening event to name and `previous_denomination_code: null` agrees with an `unknown` state. Version 2 also adds `site_location_changed`, intent `observed_change`, payload `geometry_update`: a building moved or rebuilt elsewhere within its site, which the operational definition keeps under one identifier and which is a change in the world, so the census counts it. The alternative to a paired accession type, widening the intents `site_created` may carry, would leave the type name saying "created" on a place that existed for a century, and we recommend against it.

**Change 4: the location grade travels with the geometry, and `exact` has no assertion source.** `GeometryPayload` and `SiteCreatedPayload` gain `location_confidence` and `location_assertion_mode`. The grade is the value `locationConfidenceGrade` derives from a confirmed assertion (`building`, `parcel_or_compound`, `street`, `locality`, `area`), or `unknown` where no reviewed assertion exists, which is the case for a `site_registered` event whose geometry is the source's own coordinate: a precisely recorded OSM point says how the source drew the place, and nothing about how confidently the project locates it. `exact` is reserved for a reviewed evidence rule that no current contract defines, so version 2 lists it and refuses it on every event until such a rule exists. `uncertainty_radius_m` is carried only when the assertion carried one. Replay copies the grade into the state table and D13's publication gate reads it; nothing downstream infers a grade from a distance.

**Change 5: `payload_hash` pinned.** Version 2 defines `payload_hash` as the `pow-object.v1` hash (`sha256:` followed by the hex digest) of the `pow-canonical-json.v1` bytes of the `payload` object. Version 1 left the normalisation unnamed. The content-addressed contract requires the same canonical form for every review object, and D15 needs a hash whose inputs are defined.

**Change 6: provenance on the review reference.** `ReviewRef` gains the members the content-addressed contract lists for an accepted event: `export_batch_id`, `export_manifest_hash`, `evidence_version_hash`, `review_snapshot_hash`, `review_decision_hash`, `proposal_hash` and `batch_release_hash`. The first five are required on an accepted event. The schema makes `proposal_hash` and `batch_release_hash` nullable, because the contract's third and fourth steps are unbuilt; whether any event may be accepted while they are null is a decision for the acceptance brief (section 5, PR C) and for JB (section 7, ruling 12). Version 2 only makes the members exist.

**Change 7: supersession and retraction at contract level.** `supersedes_event_ids` gains rules. A superseding event names explicit events, never "every event of a task", and each named event must share the superseder's `target` and attribute: a correction may supersede only a correction with the same conflict key, an observed change only an observed change of the same attribute, and so on; a `site_registered` event may be superseded only by `duplicate_merged`. A correction that adjudicates a conflict (change 2) names the corrections it resolves, whatever their intervals, and that is the one case in which a superseder's conflict key may differ from the superseded event's. A new event type `event_retracted`, intent `review_state`, payload `proposal_state` with `reason`, names the retracted events in `supersedes_event_ids`. Dependants are refused or adjudicated, never orphaned: validation of a retraction against the log refuses it when any live event references a retracted event through `corrects.opened_by_event_id`, `supersedes_event_ids` or a relocation link, unless the retraction names those dependants too (an explicit cascade, listed in the payload's `reason`) or a reviewed replacement has superseded them first. Liveness at audit time *T* is defined over the events recorded at or before *T*, and suppression runs along the lineage rather than being recomputed from the newest member. An event is *retracted at T* when an `event_retracted` recorded at or before *T* names it. An event is *suppressed at T* when some event recorded at or before *T* that is not retracted at *T* names it in `supersedes_event_ids`; whether that superseder is itself superseded makes no difference, because replacement of a replacement leaves the first replacement's judgement standing against its own predecessor. An event is *live at T* when it is recorded at or before *T*, is not retracted at *T* and is not suppressed at *T*. Only an explicit retraction restores anything: when a superseder is retracted, the suppression edges it carried lapse, and each event it named becomes live again at every later *T* unless another unretracted superseder still names it. Four consequences follow. In a chain where E2 supersedes E1 and E3 supersedes E2, at a *T* after E3 only E3 is live, at a *T* between E2 and E3 only E2 is live, and at a *T* before E2 only E1 is live. Retracting E3 revives E2 and leaves E1 suppressed by E2; retracting E2 while E3 is live is refused by the dependants rule above unless E3 is retracted with it, since E3 names E2. Retracting an adjudication that named several corrections revives every one of them that no other unretracted superseder names, and the conflict those corrections had returns with them, so the retraction's `reason` must say how the conflict is to be resolved next. A suppressed event contributes nothing to any state at *T*, so the conflict check of change 2 ignores it and an ordinary third replacement validates. A state computed at an earlier *T* still contains every event live at that *T*, which is what an audit of an earlier release needs.

**Change 8: a retrospective assertion within D3.** A reviewer who reads an October observation and concludes that worship was in use at the 1 September anchor is asserting when a fact held, which is what `effective` records. Version 2 therefore requires that a `worship_function_observed` (or `site_attributes_observed`) event whose `target_year_affects` names an anchor *A* carry an `effective` bound that covers *A*: `not_later_than` at or before *A* (the fact held by *A*), `date_precision: bounded`, `basis: reviewer_inference` or `source_statement`, and a new payload member `observed_on`, the date of the observation itself, so the observation date is kept apart from the asserted validity. The event then replays into *A* under D3's own rule and needs no special reading. An observation with no retrospective assertion carries its observation date as `effective_date` and says nothing about earlier anchors. `TargetYearAffect.target_year` stays a date in the event contract; the Convex decision's numeric `target_year` converts to the country's anchor date (`2026` to `2026-09-01`) at translation, and the conversion rule is part of the translation contract PR C will define. A retrospective assertion that contradicts a live lifecycle event (presence asserted at *A*, a closure certain before *A*) is a temporal conflict and is refused for adjudication, like a correction conflict.

Fixtures ship with the schema, under `schemas/fixtures/change-event.v2/`: the Kendall events of section 4.7 as positive cases; and negative cases for `correction_scope` on an `observed_change` event, `interval` without `corrects.interval`, an accepted correction without an explicit scope or without `corrects`, a `corrects.attribute` disagreeing with the event type, a `corrects` whose previous-value member disagrees with the referenced state, two live corrections with intersecting affected intervals, a correction after a `site_location_changed` that names the registration instead of the move, a denomination value recurring in two intervals with a correction naming the second, `site_created` with intent `accession`, `site_registered` with intent `lifecycle`, a superseder naming an event of another attribute, a retraction of a registration with a live dependant and no cascade, and the supersession cases of change 7: an ordinary supersession (the superseded correction contributes nothing and the conflict check passes); a three-event chain evaluated at audit times before E2, between E2 and E3, and after E3, with exactly one live correction at each; retraction of the chain's tip, reviving E2 and not E1; a refused retraction of E2 while E3 is live, and the same retraction accepted as a cascade naming E3; an adjudication of two corrections with unequal overlapping intervals in both shapes of change 2; and retraction of that adjudication, which revives both corrections and their conflict; a retrospective assertion whose `effective` fails to cover its anchor, a retrospective assertion contradicting a certain closure, a geometry payload with a radius and the grade `building`, any event carrying `exact`, an accepted event whose `payload_hash` disagrees with its payload, and a `site_registered` event with no `not_later_than` and no `effective_date`. The same pull request wires D9 gates 2, 3 and 6 into `pow validate`: country containment and relocation distance, future effective dates on completed events and effective dates outside the source's coverage, and taxonomy membership against the pinned instance (`schemas/denomination-taxonomy.json`, version `2026-06-12.1`, which carries `christian.baptist`; verified). Gate 4 belongs to acceptance. Enforcing gate 6 would reject the existing version 1 examples, which still carry bare codes (`anglican`, `methodist`) and the placeholder `denomination-taxonomy.draft`, and the Rust fixtures in `main.rs` that do the same; PR A migrates them to dotted codes under `2026-06-12.1`, keeping their version 1 event shapes, which closes the debt D2 named.

## 4. PR B: an agent files a correction

Today the agent lane carries a research dossier about a place: claims with cited locators, an advisory review by a second provider, run manifests, a quarantine block for personal details, and a deterministic submission key. The dossier has a `candidate_location` with a basis and radius, but nothing says "the record's point is wrong and here is where the place is", nothing records which denomination relation or change class the claims support, and intake writes a plain task, with no original point for a reviewer to rule between. The design below adds a correction proposal to the dossier, lets intake create a revision task of the same shape the portal's revise flow creates, adds the one write path a human preparer needs, adds a clearance step for text that may name a person, and keeps every safeguard the lane has. A dossier without the new block behaves as today.

### 4.1 The correction proposal in the dossier

We add one optional object to the dossier, `correction_proposal`, in a new schema version `agent-dossier.v2` with the bundle at `agent-review-bundle.v2`; the three validators (Python `lib.py`, TypeScript `agentIntake.ts`, Rust `validate-agent`) pin the schema file, so a version bump is the honest form of an additive change. The object has six parts.

1. `target`: `place_ref` (the OSM object the correction concerns, which must equal `dossier.place.place_ref`), `original_latitude` and `original_longitude` (the record's point as the source edition shows it), and `source_edition` (the tileset or extract name, such as `places-v2-20260722-grid`).
2. `moved_point`: `latitude`, `longitude`, `basis` from the dossier's existing location bases (`osm_object`, `address_geocode`, `source_map_or_photo`, `imagery`, `reader_inference`), an optional `outline_ref` (an OSM way or relation whose outline the point lies within), and `supporting_claim_ids`. Absent when the proposal corrects attributes only.
3. `location_assertion_proposed`: `mode`, `basis` (from the portal's assertion bases, which for an agent will be `named_source_description` or `other`), `confidence`, `uncertainty_radius_m` and `source_wording`. The object omits `contributor_confirmed`, because confirmation is a human act (section 4.3).
4. `denomination`: `denomination_raw`, `label_basis` (one of the portal's bases, which for a website is `current_self_description` or `named_documentary_source`), `denomination_relation`, `taxonomy_code_suggested` (checked against the pinned taxonomy, advisory only) and `supporting_claim_ids`.
5. `change_class`: `map_correction`, `genuine_change` or `uncertain`, with a `basis` string and `supporting_claim_ids`.
6. `identity_proposed`: one of the existing `identity_decision` literals (`same_site` for Kendall), with an optional `related_ref` (`osm:<type>/<id>` or a task id) where the literal needs a linked place, and `supporting_claim_ids`. The reviewer's decision, never the proposal, settles identity.

Every `supporting_claim_ids` entry must name a claim in the dossier, every cited claim must have a source on the allowlist, and the advisory reviewer's `claim_checks` must cover them; a proposal whose supporting claims the reviewer marked `not_supported` or `not_checked` is admitted with the reviewer's outcome attached, for the human to see, because the lane records disagreement rather than resolving it.

### 4.2 What intake writes

When a bundle carries `correction_proposal`, `ingestBundle` creates the task as a revision of the existing record, with the same fields the portal's revise flow produces: `geometry` at the moved point (or the original point when there is none), `matched_osm_id` and `osm_object_type` from `place_ref`, `source_context.issue_report` with `original_point`, `source_edition`, `evidence_lane: "agent_correction"`, `source_title` and `source_url` of the first supporting claim, and `automated_checks` carrying the proposal summary. It leaves `initial_location_assertion` empty, because the contract requires a confirmed assertion and confirmation waits for the preparer; it stores the proposed assertion, with its hash, inside `source_context.issue_report.location_assertion_proposed`. The evidence draft stays `agent_intake_only`, and gains `change_class`, `denomination_or_tradition_raw`, `denomination_label_basis` and `denomination_relation` copied from the proposal, so the preparer sees them in the draft they will clone. The receipt, the submission key, the bundle hash, the advisory review and the judgments are written as today. Two new judgment rows record the researcher's correction proposal and the reviewer's verdict on it (`judgment_kind: correction_proposal`), so later calibration can count how often a model's moved point survived human review.

Three safeguards need extension rather than preservation. First, the validator's country check and the allowlist are New Zealand-only; a United States run needs `country_code` generalised to the registry and an allowlist version `us-v1` whose domains include `openstreetmap.org` and, for the pilot, the church's own domain, since the lane refuses a parish domain until an allowlist names it. Second, the batch `internal-agent-research` and the fixed `target_years: [2013, 2018, 2023]` become per-country, read from `convex/lib/countryYears.ts` as the portal does. Third, the submit command's deployment selector admits `dev` and `local` only, so a run against the deployment the live portals use needs either an explicit allowance for a named deployment under JB's instruction or a run on a personal development deployment; section 7 puts the choice to JB. The ingest flag (`POW_INTERNAL_AGENT_INGEST_ENABLED`) stays per deployment and per authorised run.

**Review decisions gain a linked-place reference.** The association step the acceptance brief will need (section 5, PR C) must know which place a `successor_at_new_site`, `duplicate`, `split` or `merge` decision refers to, and today that reference lives in the decision note. PR B adds an optional `identity_reference` to `reviewDecisionInput` and the stored decision: `{ kind: "site" | "task" | "osm", id }`, required by validation when the literal needs a linked place and refused when it does not (`same_site`, `new_candidate`, `uncertain`). The review portal gains the field beside the identity list. Kendall's decision is `same_site` with no reference. The hash of a snapshot-linked decision covers the stored fields it already covers; the new optional field joins the version 1 envelope only when present, and a decision without it hashes as today.

### 4.3 Person-bearing text: human clearance of the public text set

D24 rules that a living person named in the project's records appears in public only under a pseudonym, clergy and office holders included, while contributors may be credited. The rule applies whatever the spelling and whatever a detector finds: the regex screen finds a name only behind an honorific, a capitalised-word heuristic misses "john smith", and "Rev. J. Smith preached on 3 May" passes the current screen (verified). Detection therefore flags passages for attention and never exempts a record. Every evidence version that may reach export carries a human clearance of its complete *public text set*.

**The public text set** is the ordered list of every string that any public surface might carry for a task's accepted version: source titles and locators, claim values and quoted support, notes, denomination and address text from the evidence version; and the place name, the confirmed location assertion's wording, the proposed assertion's wording and the proposal's basis strings from the task row and its `source_context`. The evidence version's envelope (`buildEvidenceVersion`, `convex/lib/evidenceVersions.ts`) holds evidence fields and occupancies and none of the task text, so the set cannot be a function of the version alone. We define a versioned *clearance input object*, `public-text-set.v1`: `{ task_id, evidence_version_hash, task_row_hash, entries }`, where `task_row_hash` is the `pow-object.v1` hash of the task's text-bearing fields (name, address, locality, `initial_location_assertion.source_wording`, and every string under `source_context.issue_report`) copied into the object verbatim, and `entries` lists each eligible string as `{ source: "evidence_version" | "task", field, text_sha256 }`. One function shared by Convex and the Rust crate computes the object from the version's canonical bytes and the task row, and the object's own `pow-object.v1` hash, the *clearance input hash*, identifies the complete set. Replacement text is clearance output: it enters no entry, and the clearance row records each replacement with its own SHA-256 and screens it as section 4.3 describes.

**Detectors mark, and only mark.** At intake the existing screen refuses the bundle as today, and for `agent-review-bundle.v2` only it is extended with United States and international telephone formats and with absolute-path patterns (`/Users/`, `/home/`, `C:\`, UNC paths); version 1 bundles keep the version 1 screen unchanged, with compatibility fixtures in all three validators (a version 1 bundle carrying a United States number passes as today; the same bundle under version 2 refuses), so B1 stays additive. A name heuristic (a run of two or more capitalised words outside the place name, the taxonomy, months, listed organisations and a small allowlist) marks entries `attention: suspected_person`; the marks travel with the set and are shown to the preparer beside each entry. A set with no marks still needs clearance.

**The clearance mutation.** `evidence:clearPublicText({ taskId, evidenceDraftId, evidenceVersionHash, clearances, clientSubmissionId })`, where the call also carries `clearanceInputHash` and `clearances` lists every entry of the set as `{ field, text_sha256, action, replacement? }` and `action` is `no_person`, `role_only` (the text is rewritten to the person's role, as "the pastor leads the congregation"), `pseudonym` (a stable pseudonym under D24's rules, supplied as `replacement`) or `withhold` (the entry is kept for reviewers and excluded from every public surface, the action for a locator that names a person). Its guards are those of the confirmation mutation (section 4.4): the caller is the task's assignee or a curator or admin, the PI's user excluded; the task is `needs_review` or `in_progress`, decided statuses refuse until a reopen; the named version must be the draft's current submitted version; every entry of the computed set must be present with a matching `text_sha256`, and an entry absent or with a stale hash refuses the whole call; a `client_submission_receipts` row makes an identical retry return the original receipt and a changed retry return `content_conflict`. The mutation recomputes the clearance input object from the current task row and the named version, refuses when its hash differs from the `clearanceInputHash` the caller passes (the task text changed under the preparer), and writes one `public_text_clearances` row (task, draft, version hash, task row hash, clearance input hash, the cleared entries with actions, the replacements with their hashes, cleared by, cleared at, receipt), appends a task event `public_text_cleared`, and never alters the version or the task.

**Binding through preparation, review and freeze.** Clearance binds to the clearance input hash, which covers the evidence version and the task text together. The preparer confirms the assertion first (its wording is part of the set), then creates and submits the ordinary draft, then clears the complete set of that submitted version; any later correction creates a child version with a new hash, and any change to a text-bearing task field changes the task row hash, so either needs a fresh clearance, and a version whose current clearance input hash has no clearance row is uncleared. The review snapshot pins the clearance input hash and the clearance row (or records its absence), so a reviewer sees cleared and original texts side by side, a missing clearance is visible, and a snapshot taken before clearance goes stale. A decision `accepted_for_export` on an uncleared version is refused in `recordReviewDecision` and `batchRecordReviewDecisions`; PI acceptance recomputes the clearance input hash for the decision's pinned `evidence_version_hash` and the task row and requires a matching clearance row; `assertTaskExportAuthority` at batch creation and freeze does the same, and the frozen bundle gains `public_text_clearances.jsonl`, which `pow export verify` checks by recomputing the object from `tasks.jsonl` and `evidence_versions.jsonl`. The intake-only draft needs no clearance, because export authority attaches to the accepted version alone.

**Retained audit material against publication-eligible content.** The bundle collector keeps every draft and every evidence version of an included task (`convex/exports.ts`), the intake-only draft and superseded versions included, and PR B leaves that collection as it is: those rows are restricted audit history, retrievable by curators and never public, and retaining them discloses nothing. Publication-eligible content is the accepted version named by the authoritative decision's `evidence_version_hash`, and that version alone must carry a clearance; `pow export verify` requires a clearance row whose hashes reproduce for that version on every agent-correction task and treats every other version as restricted history. The public projection of the later export brief reads cleared accepted versions only.

**Fixtures** in the validators and the Convex tests: "john smith leads the congregation" (no detector fires; clearance still required; cleared as `role_only` to "the pastor leads the congregation"); "John Smith leads the congregation" (heuristic marks; same clearance); "Rev. ### 4.4 The preparer's confirmation

The lane's authority boundary stands: an `agent_intake_only` draft cannot be accepted for export, and the ordinary path is a human-owned draft prepared from the dossier ([internal agent review](internal-agent-review.md), "Workflow and authority"). Preparation needs one write path that does not exist (section 2, fact 6). We add `tasks:confirmLocationAssertion({ taskId, locationAssertion, proposedAssertionHash, clientSubmissionId })`. Its guards reuse the ones evidence writes already enforce, and add three.

- **Who.** The caller holds the role `ra`, `reviewer`, `curator` or `admin`, and a dedicated guard, `assertAssigneeOrCurator`, requires the caller to be the task's assignee (an unassigned task is claimed by the call, through the existing claim path) or to hold `curator` or `admin`; a non-assignee holding only `reviewer` is refused, which is narrower than `assertOwnsOrCanReview`, and the fixture tests that case. The PI's user is then excluded, so that the PI never becomes the submission's author.
- **Task state.** The task is `needs_review` or `in_progress`, carries `issue_report.location_assertion_proposed`, and has no `initial_location_assertion`; `evidenceIntakeRefusal` applies, so a decided task (`reviewed`, `pi_accepted`, `exported`) refuses until it is reopened, and a reopened task confirms under the reopen's recorded reason. `proposedAssertionHash` must equal the hash stored at intake, so the preparer confirms what they read.
- **Content.** `assertCountryAllowsAssertionMode` and `assertAssertionMatchesTaskPoint` run as for `createIssueTask`; `contributor_confirmed` must be true; the preparer may tighten the assertion (a radius, wording) and may not move the point, which stays the task's geometry.
- **Effect.** Stores `initial_location_assertion` with `confirmed_by` and `confirmed_at`, appends a task event `location_assertion_confirmed`, and writes a `client_submission_receipts` row keyed by the caller and `clientSubmissionId` with the request digest: an identical retry returns the original receipt, a retry with changed content returns `content_conflict`, and a second confirmation under a new identifier refuses as already confirmed. The task row is covered by review snapshots, so any snapshot taken before confirmation goes stale and the reviewer decides on the confirmed state.

The portal's task panel gains the control, visible to the assignee of an agent-correction task with an unconfirmed proposal, and the RA guide gains the step.

**Confirmation is required, not advised.** A human draft on an agent-correction task could otherwise be accepted and frozen with the agent's assertion unconfirmed, because the `agent_intake_only` refusal ends when the ordinary draft exists and `assertLocationOutcome` reads the two points and never the assertion. A shared check, `assertAgentCorrectionReady(task, version)`, therefore runs wherever export authority is granted or rechecked: in `recordReviewDecision` and `batchRecordReviewDecisions` for a decision `accepted_for_export`, in `recordAcceptance` (the PI), and in `assertTaskExportAuthority` at batch creation and freeze. For a task whose `issue_report.evidence_lane` is `agent_correction` with a `location_assertion_proposed`, it requires `initial_location_assertion` with `confirmed_by` and `confirmed_at`, a `location_assertion_confirmed` task event by that user, and (section 4.3) a clearance for the pinned version. The frozen bundle carries the confirmation in `tasks.jsonl` and its event in `task_events.jsonl`, and `pow export verify` checks both for every agent-correction task. The fixture creates the ordinary draft while deliberately skipping confirmation and asserts refusal at every one of those four points.

### 4.5 Review, and the humans the Kendall run needs

The preparer opens the task, reads the dossier and the advisory review, confirms the assertion (section 4.4), creates the ordinary draft with `evidence:saveEvidenceDraft` carrying `change_class: map_correction`, the denomination fields and the receipt identifier in its source notes, submits it, and then clears the complete public text set of the submitted version (section 4.3). The task is then in the same queue as any RA revision, with the original point and the moved point for the reviewer to rule between. The reviewer records `accepted_for_export` on the current snapshot with `location_outcome: accept_moved_point`, `identity_decision: same_site`, and `target_year_affects: [{ target_year: 2026, target_year_status: "present", basis: "reviewer_inference" }]`. That assertion needs an entry path, because today the review portal derives `target_year_affects` from the draft's `target_year_statuses` with a fixed `basis: source_observation` (`targetYearAffectsFromDraft`, `apps/regions/nz/js/review-portal.js`), and a United States task's default `target_years` are 2000, 2010 and 2020 (verified), so the ordinary draft would yield no 2026 affect and no inference basis. PR B adds three pieces. Intake sets a correction task's `target_years` to the country's years plus the anchor year of the observation (2026 for Kendall). The contributor portal today renders, reads and builds drafts from the country constant `TARGET_YEARS` (`apps/regions/nz/js/verification-map.js`: the status controls, the status map, the wide-row field list, `currentFormValues` and `buildEvidenceDraft`), so a year on the task alone reaches no form; PR B makes the open task's `target_years` the source of years throughout rendering, loading of a saved draft, validation and evidence construction, falling back to the country constant only for a new nomination with no task, and, for agent-correction tasks only, the server validates `target_year_statuses` keys against the task's years on save and submit (B1). The same check on every task narrows an existing write path, since `targetYearStatusSet` accepts any string key today, so general enforcement belongs to B2, with a compatibility fixture showing an existing task's arbitrary keys accepted under B1. The preparer then records `target_year_statuses: { "2026": "present" }` with `target_year_evidence` for that year stating the inference (worship documented on 19 October; presence at 1 September inferred). The review portal's decision form lists the draft's target-year statuses as proposed affects with a basis control per year, defaulting to `source_observation` and offering `reviewer_inference` and `source_statement`; the reviewer sets `reviewer_inference` for 2026 and may change a status, and the decision stores what the reviewer chose. The fixture exercises that path end to end: a DOM test of the preparer's form on a task carrying 2026 (rendering, `currentFormValues`, `buildEvidenceDraft`) producing the draft, the server accepting it, the review portal's decision form with the chosen basis, and `recordReviewDecision`, rather than an injected decision. The PI accepts. A curator or admin composes and freezes the batch, and `pow export verify` passes on the materialised bundle. That is where PR B stops.

The code enforces a two-person minimum: the submission's author may not review it, and the PI may not accept the PI's own submission, while the PI may ratify a decision the PI recorded, with `self_decided: true` recorded on the acceptance (section 2, fact 6). For Kendall the roles are an operator who runs the agent and submits the bundle (JB, or an agent under his written instruction, since the flag and the deployment are his); a preparer who owns the ordinary draft and may be any RA, reviewer or curator; a reviewer other than the preparer; JB as PI; and a curator or admin who freezes, who may be the preparer or the reviewer. JB cannot be the preparer, because acceptance refuses his own submission. Under the code as it stands JB may be both reviewer and PI, with the self-ratification recorded; whether the first AI-filed correction should instead require three distinct people (preparer, reviewer, PI) is a policy choice we put to JB in section 7. The RA sheet and the portal's revise flow remain the fallback: an RA who files the same correction by hand produces the same task shape, and nothing downstream distinguishes the two routes except `evidence_lane`.

### PR C: `pow accept`

*Purpose.* Verify a frozen bundle, translate the reviewed task into version 2 events under a versioned translation contract, mint `site_id` at first acceptance with OSM identifiers as a crosswalk, and append to an append-only, per-country event log with chained data manifests. *Depends on* A and B. *Serves* D25 point 1 (a continuous accepted log) and amendment items 4 and 5 as written into the log. *Open design questions for its own brief:* the processing guard shared with withdrawal, including expired-lease takeover, monotonic fencing tokens, a conditional append against the expected log head, the durable acceptance point and recovery between append and the acceptance record; the base-state dependency of the content-addressed contract, with stale-base detection and pre-acceptance review of translated consequences, or a precisely stated interim exception for the PI; the replacement-bundle protocol, since `completeFreeze` requires a superseded predecessor to remain `frozen` and an `accepted` status breaks that path; supersession by explicit event reference across intervals and the freeze-time checks it needs; identity association against the crosswalk before minting, with the reviewed `identity_decision` and `identity_reference` reconciled against an existing source key (a successor that keeps the key must receive a new site), collision checks for every proposed registration (D9 gate 4), and adjudication on conflict; the translation of occupancy bases, where `organisation_founded` and `building_dedication` are evidence rather than worship onset unless a reviewed claim says otherwise, and `first_seen_only` and `last_seen_only` open and close nothing; the conversion of numeric target years to anchor dates; recomputation of the review snapshot's content hash in Rust; and the shared replay core C needs to resolve identity, which D then extends.

## 7. Rulings for JB

Each ruling is answerable with a yes, a no or a choice. Rulings 1 to 11 and 13 concern PR A and PR B; ruling 12 is the one roadmap decision that shapes A's schema.

1. **Accession and within-site movement as paired types.** Version 2 adds `site_registered` (intent `accession`) and `site_location_changed` (intent `observed_change`), and leaves `site_created` bound to `lifecycle`. Recommended: yes.
2. **The correction target and its conflict key.** `corrects` binds a correction to an attribute, an opening event and an interval where scoped; corrections with intersecting affected intervals on one attribute are refused for adjudication. Recommended: yes.
