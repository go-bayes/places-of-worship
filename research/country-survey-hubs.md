# Country Survey Hubs

Last verified: 2026-07-07.

Country cards should use these hubs as cross-country source finders,
screening sources, or comparison sources. National statistical offices and
source registries remain the preferred citation for buildable subnational
tables, site files, and census questionnaires.

## IPUMS International

**What it provides.** IPUMS International provides harmonised census and survey
microdata. The relevant harmonised variables are `RELIGION` and the historical
variable `RELIGION2`. The project holds a registration, but each extract still
needs a stated research purpose.

**Granularity.** The unit is the person record inside an IPUMS sample. Geographic
detail follows the anonymised geography released for each sample. IPUMS is best
for affiliation distributions and historical comparability. Direct site mapping
needs separate site-level sources.

**Access and licence.** Access requires an IPUMS International account and
accepted terms. Microdata should not be redistributed. Publications and reports
must cite IPUMS International and acknowledge the original statistical agencies.

**How a country card should cite it.** Cite the IPUMS variable page, the exact
country and sample years, the extract date, and the IPUMS citation text supplied
with the extract.

**Verified URLs.** `RELIGION`:
https://international.ipums.org/international-action/variables/RELIGION.
`RELIGION2`:
https://international.ipums.org/international-action/variables/RELIGION2.
Citation and terms:
https://international.ipums.org/international/citation.shtml and
https://international.ipums.org/international/terms.shtml.

## ARDA Cross-National Files

**What it provides.** The Association of Religion Data Archives (ARDA) provides
national profiles and data archives for cross-national religion files. Useful
files include Religious Characteristics of States Demographics version 2.0 and
the World Religion Project Global Religion Dataset.

**Granularity.** ARDA files usually support country-year, state-year, region-year,
or religion-by-period comparison. Most files are comparison and covariate sources
rather than source tables for mappable sites.

**Access and licence.** Archive files are file-specific. Many ARDA records provide
downloads, a Digital Object Identifier, and a required dataset citation. Country
cards should read the file page before reusing a table.

**How a country card should cite it.** Cite the exact ARDA archive file page, file
identifier, Digital Object Identifier where present, named dataset authors, and
retrieval date.

**Verified URLs.** National profiles:
https://www.thearda.com/world-religion/national-profiles. Archive categories:
https://www.thearda.com/data-archive/browse-categories?cid=A. RCS-Dem 2.0:
https://www.thearda.com/data-archive?fid=RCSDEM2. World Religion Project global
file: https://www.thearda.com/data-archive?fid=WRPGLOBL.

## World Religion Database

**What it provides.** The World Religion Database (WRD) provides country-level
religious affiliation estimates for every country, with historical series from
1900 and projections to 2050. The database also offers province-level affiliation
tables and maps for subscribed users.

**Granularity.** The usual public claim is country-level affiliation. Subnational
tables are available through the subscribed database interface and need separate
licence review before reuse.

**Access and licence.** WRD is a Brill subscription product. Use it for screening,
triangulation, and source discovery unless the subscription terms allow a more
specific extract.

**How a country card should cite it.** Cite WRD as a subscription source, with the
country, variable or table name, access month, and the Brill citation form. Keep
any restricted extracts outside the public repository unless the licence allows
publication.

**Verified URLs.** Home: https://www.worldreligiondatabase.org/. Access model:
https://www.worldreligiondatabase.org/data/. Citation guidance:
https://www.worldreligiondatabase.org/citations/. Frequently asked questions:
https://www.worldreligiondatabase.org/faq/.

## Pew Religious Datasets

**What it provides.** Pew provides global religious composition estimates and
selected survey datasets. The 2025 global report estimates the religious
composition of 201 countries and territories in 2010 and 2020 across seven
religious categories. The U.S. Religious Landscape Study provides survey-based
U.S. religious affiliation data at national, state, District of Columbia, and 34
large metropolitan-area levels.

**Granularity.** The global files are country and territory aggregates. The U.S.
Religious Landscape Study reaches subnational units inside the United States.
Other Pew religion surveys have survey-specific samples and variables.

**Access and licence.** Reports are public. Downloadable datasets commonly require
a Pew account and acceptance of Pew's data-use terms. Country cards should verify
the terms on the dataset page before storing microdata or derived tables.

**How a country card should cite it.** Cite the Pew report or dataset page, the
methodology page, country or unit, years, downloaded file name if used, and
retrieval date.

## DHS And MICS

**What it provides.** Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS) and Multiple Indicator
Cluster Surveys (MICS) provide nationally representative household survey
microdata. Religion variables are survey-specific. The DHS-VII Recode Manual
lists `V130` and `MV130` as religion variables, with country-specific questions
and codes. MICS examples in the World Bank catalogue include household-head
religion variables such as `HC1A` and discrimination variables for religion or
belief such as `MVT22E`.

**Granularity.** The unit is the person, household, or cluster record. Public
geographic detail is restricted to protect respondents. DHS and MICS support
population affiliation estimates, covariate construction, and source discovery.
Point locations for places of worship require separate site-level sources.

**Access and licence.** DHS access is free for legitimate academic research after
registration and country-specific approval. DHS data may be used only for the
approved purpose, must not be shared with other investigators, and must not be
used to identify respondents. MICS access terms depend on the survey repository
record. World Bank Microdata Library records distinguish direct, public-use,
licensed, external, and no-access files, with restrictions on redistribution and
identification.

**How a country card should cite it.** Cite the exact survey, country, year,
dataset file, recode manual or questionnaire, variable name, archive access
category, and retrieval date. Country cards must verify religion variables in
the survey-specific questionnaire or recode dictionary.

**Verified URLs.** DHS access instructions:
https://dhsprogram.com/data/Access-Instructions.cfm. DHS terms:
https://dhsprogram.com/data/terms-of-use.cfm. DHS recode manuals:
https://dhsprogram.com/publications/publication-dhsg4-dhs-questionnaires-and-manuals.cfm.
MICS programme: https://mics.unicef.org/. World Bank Microdata MICS catalogue:
https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/MICS. World Bank Microdata terms:
https://microdata.worldbank.org/terms-of-use.

## UNSD Census Table Hub

**What it provides.** The United Nations Statistics Division (UNSD) World
Population and Housing Census Programme provides census guidance, country
documents, and Demographic Yearbook census tables. Table 28 is the cross-national
table for population by religion, sex, and urban or rural residence.

**Granularity.** UNSD census tables are country-level or broad cross-tabulated
tables reported by national statistical offices. The religion table is not a
subnational boundary file. Country agents should use it to identify whether a
country reports a religion census table and then seek the national table for
buildable geography.

**Access and licence.** UNSD tables are public web data. Reuse should acknowledge
UNSD and the original national statistical office where named. Table-level terms
or export notices should be followed.

**How a country card should cite it.** Cite the UNSD World Population and Housing
Census Programme, Demographic Yearbook census table code, country, census year,
and retrieval date. If a national source is later found, cite the national source
as the primary table and keep UNSD as a cross-national locator.

**Verified URLs.** World Population and Housing Census Programme:
https://unstats.un.org/unsd/demographic-social/census/. Demographic Yearbook
census datasets:
https://unstats.un.org/unsd/demographic-social/products/dyb/dybcensusdata/.
Table 28:
http://data.un.org/Data.aspx?d=POP&f=tableCode%3a28.

## Eurostat

**What it provides.** Eurostat provides European statistical tables, including
population and housing census tables in the Eurostat database. Religion coverage
depends on national collection rules and table availability.

**Granularity.** Eurostat products can provide harmonised national and regional
statistics for European Union and associated countries. The practical unit
depends on the selected datacode and dimensions.

**Access and licence.** Eurostat allows reuse of statistical data, metadata,
publications, and dissemination tools for commercial or non-commercial purposes
when the source is acknowledged, subject to table-level exceptions and
third-party rights.

**How a country card should cite it.** Cite the dataset Digital Object Identifier
where Eurostat provides one. For customised tables, cite the Eurostat datacode
link, selected dimensions, and access date.

**Verified URLs.** Population and housing census database:
https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/population-demography/population-housing-censuses/database.
Copyright notice and reuse rules:
https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/help/copyright-notice.

## geoBoundaries

**What it provides.** geoBoundaries provides downloadable administrative boundary
files and an API for per-country metadata. The API supports `gbOpen`,
`gbHumanitarian`, and `gbAuthoritative` release types and administrative levels
`ADM0` to `ADM5`, plus `ALL`.

**Granularity.** Coverage varies by country and administrative level. Each API
response records the boundary identifier, represented year, administrative unit
count, source, source URL, source licence, build date, and download links.

**Access and licence.** `gbOpen` is the usual default because geoBoundaries
describes it as Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 compliant. `gbHumanitarian` and
`gbAuthoritative` can carry narrower source licences. Country cards should use
the licence fields returned by the exact API request.

**How a country card should cite it.** Cite product, release, ISO3 code,
administrative level, `boundaryID`, represented year, build date, source,
licence, and the API URL. Record which administrative levels were present or
missing.

**Verified URLs.** API documentation: https://www.geoboundaries.org/api.html.
Current API template:
`https://www.geoboundaries.org/api/current/{release_type}/{ISO3}/{ADM-level}/`.
Example: https://www.geoboundaries.org/api/current/gbOpen/NZL/ADM1/.

## ohsome And OpenStreetMap

**What it provides.** The ohsome API queries historical OpenStreetMap (OSM) data.
For places of worship, the core OSM tag is `amenity=place_of_worship`, with
supporting tags such as `religion=*`, `denomination=*`, `name=*`, and
`building=*`.

**Granularity.** ohsome can return counts, densities, grouped counts, and selected
feature geometries for a boundary, date, or time series. OSM coverage is
community-generated and uneven. It should be treated as a candidate inventory
and coverage signal, rather than an accepted worship-state source.

**Access and licence.** OSM data is licensed under the Open Data Commons Open
Database License. Reuse requires credit to OpenStreetMap and its contributors and
must make the ODbL status clear. Derived datasets that build on OSM may carry
share-alike obligations. ohsome API responses include attribution metadata.

**Coverage assessment method.** Use a trusted country or subnational polygon,
preferably from the same boundary source the country card proposes. Query
`/elements/count` and `/elements/count/groupBy/boundary` with a filter such as
`amenity=place_of_worship` at recent and historical timestamps. Compare counts
per population and per area with national source counts where available. Inspect
the share of records with `religion=*`, `denomination=*`, and `name=*`. Treat
sharp time-series jumps as mapping activity signals until source review confirms
a worship-use change.

**How a country card should cite it.** Cite OSM contributors, ODbL, the ohsome
endpoint, query parameters, timestamp or interval, API version when returned, and
retrieval date. Store query strings beside any derived count table.

**Verified URLs.** ohsome API documentation:
https://docs.ohsome.org/ohsome-api/stable/. Endpoints:
https://docs.ohsome.org/ohsome-api/stable/endpoints.html. Boundaries:
https://docs.ohsome.org/ohsome-api/stable/boundaries.html. Time:
https://docs.ohsome.org/ohsome-api/stable/time.html. Filter syntax:
https://docs.ohsome.org/ohsome-api/stable/filter.html. OSM place-of-worship tag:
https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:amenity%3Dplace_of_worship. OSM copyright
and licence: https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright.
